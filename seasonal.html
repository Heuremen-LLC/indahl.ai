60.1%

When the Climate Prediction CenterFollow stated a chance of 40 percent or more for a temperature category in its 3-month outlook, the stated chance averaged 48.2 percent and that category was observed in 60.1 percent of grid cells, with a block bootstrap 95% interval of 54.9 to 64.2 percent, so the reading is above: it happened more often than stated. For precipitation the stated chance averaged 45.3 percent and the category was observed 45.5 percent of the time (42.0 to 48.5), consistent. The thirds are set by CPC's 1981 to 2010 reference period, and over all cells and seasons temperature came in above normal 51.6 percent of the time and below normal 19.9 percent. Our recompute of CPC's Heidke skill score from its own archive matches the score CPC states within 1 point in 380 of 380 temperature seasons and 373 of 374 precipitation seasons with a favoured cell.

Division: HERMES

When the seasonal outlook states a chance, how often does it happen?

Each month the NOAA Climate Prediction Center issues 3-month outlooks for temperature and precipitation over the United States. For each place the outlook states the chance that the season lands in the upper third of the climate record (above normal), the middle third (near normal) or the lower third (below normal). Where no category is favoured it states equal chances, 33.33 percent each. CPC keeps an archive of these outlooks, issued half a month ahead, on a 2 by 2 degree grid over the contiguous United States, beside the category that was observed in each cell and season. This page reads that archive from the JFM 1995 season to the ASO 2026 season.

If a stated chance holds, a category stated at 40 percent should be observed in about 40 percent of such cells. This page groups stated chances into bins and counts how often the category happened. It also recomputes the Heidke skill score CPC publishes for every season, from the same archive and with CPC’s stated formula, and sets ours beside CPC’s. This page does not say why a category happens more or less often than stated. The plan, with the bins, the rules and the known-good checks, was fixed before any stated chance was set beside an observed category. All four known-good checks passed on this run.

One row is one season, one grid cell and one category, with the chance stated for that category. Rows are not independent. Every cell in a season shares one weather pattern, and neighbouring 3-month seasons share two of their months. So the interval is a moving-block bootstrap over seasons, in blocks of 12 consecutive seasons, 2,000 draws, and it is much wider than one that treats cells as independent. Reading: above if the whole interval is over the mean stated chance (the category happened more often than stated), below if it is under, consistent if the mean stated chance is inside an interval no wider than 10 points, otherwise cannot tell. A cell needs 30 rows and 24 seasons.

Our recompute of CPC’s skill score

CPC defines its score as “HSS (%) = 100 * (H - E) / (T - E) where H = Number of correct forecasts, E = Expected number of correct forecasts (1/3 of total), and T = Total number of valid forecast-observation pairs”, and the score with equal chances as “HSS withEC (%) = HSS * coverage”. We used CPC’s categorical outlook file, counted only cells with a favoured category for the first score, left out the few cells whose category in that file is not a whole number, did not weight cells by area, and set the result beside the score CPC states for the same season. The difference is ours minus CPC’s, in points.

Reading: consistent if the interval of the mean difference lies inside minus 2 to plus 2 points, above or below if it lies wholly beyond that, otherwise cannot tell. For precipitation the first score covers 374 of 380 seasons. In the other 6 every cell was equal chances. CPC lists no first score for them and an all score of 0, and ours is the same. The largest gap is 1.26 points, in JFM 2021 for precipitation, where 4 cells with a category that is not a whole number were left out. This check shows that our reading of the archive pairs each outlook with the right season. It does not test the chances themselves.

What this page holds and what it leaves out

Every season in the archive with an observed category is read, and every grid cell with an observed category, 232 cells. Outlooks still waiting for their season: 3 for temperature and 3 for precipitation. Set aside: longer leads (kept only as map contours from 2007), monthly outlooks, Alaska and Hawaii, and climate divisions, for which no archive of past outlooks with observed categories was found.
