Division: ATLAS

Status Pages Against Independent Probes

Each vendor below publishes a status page that says whether its systems are operational. Every round, our two probe seats make one plain request to a public endpoint the vendor serves. One seat is on a cloud network. The other is on a home network and is two of our machines that count as one seat. A request fails if it times out, cannot connect or returns a server error. A disagreement is a round where the page said operational and both seats failed. Two control targets with no status page set the floor for chance failures. Rules were committed before the first round.

Each vendor is read as the share of rounds in which the page and both seats agree, with a 95% interval, against one threshold set by the floor. Above: the whole interval is above the threshold. Below: the whole interval is below it and there are at least two separate episodes, so the service was less reachable than its page stated. Cannot tell, with its reason beside it: too wide to resolve when there are fewer than 300 rounds or fewer than two episodes, straddles edge when the interval overlaps the threshold, no interval formable when there is no round. One endpoint is not a whole service, and a disagreement is between the page and that endpoint on those seats in that round. Incidents a vendor records are its own. No ranking.

Plan, code and trend history for this page are public: uptime. Every plan was committed before the data it describes.
