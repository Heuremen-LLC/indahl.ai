Division: HERMES

Do the sensors meet the targets the EPA wrote down?

The EPA published performance targets for PM2.5 air sensors in 2021: a slope of 1.0 plus or minus 0.35 against a reference monitor, an intercept within 5 micrograms per cubic metre, an R squared of at least 0.70, and an error of at most 7 micrograms (RMSE) or 30 percent (NRMSE). We take every public outdoor PurpleAir sensor within 200 metres of an EPA continuous monitor, compare the two on the same UTC days, and, taking for each monitor its sensor with the median error (RMSE), ask how many monitors have that sensor meeting all four targets. We score the raw map reading, the raw cf_1 reading, and the reading corrected for humidity by the published Barkjohn equation.

The newest window is 1 March to 30 June 2026. The EPA record ends there for now. Monitoring agencies send their data to the EPA by quarter, about three months after the quarter ends, so July to September 2026 cannot be read yet. The 2026 monitor values are as the agencies sent them and are not yet certified. The previous reading, 1 June to 29 September 2025, is kept below the newest one. The two windows are different seasons, and the 2025 window held the summer smoke season.

The first two tables are live. Every hour the same sensors are set against AirNow, the EPA's real-time values for the same monitors, and the last 30 kept days of each monitor are scored the same way. AirNow values are real-time and not yet validated, so this reading can differ from the window reads below, which use the EPA's Air Quality System record. The strip shows ten sensors for the last complete hour. The sensor service gives a reading every two minutes, but each reading costs points on a fixed account, so the panel reads once an hour.

Named here and want to answer a row? Respond

Each row is the share of monitors whose sensor does not meet the four targets, with a 95 percent interval over monitors (a monitor with several sensors counts once). ABOVE means the lower end is above one half. CONSISTENT means the upper end is below one half. Sensor and monitor values are 24 hour means on UTC days, because the sensor service gives daily means on UTC days. Days where the two sensor channels disagree by 5 micrograms and 61 percent or more are left out, the cleaning rule of the same paper. Sensors near monitors may not be typical of sensors elsewhere.
