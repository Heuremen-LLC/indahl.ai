AIRQ · Air Sensors Against the EPA's Own Targets

Division: HERMES

Do the sensors meet the targets the EPA wrote down?

The EPA published performance targets for PM2.5 air sensors in 2021: a slope of 1.0 plus or minus 0.35 against a reference monitor, an intercept within 5 micrograms per cubic metre, an R squared of at least 0.70, and an error of at most 7 micrograms (RMSE) or 30 percent (NRMSE). We take every public outdoor PurpleAir sensor within 200 metres of an EPA continuous monitor, compare the two on the same UTC days from June to September 2025, and ask how many monitors have a sensor that meets all four targets. We score the raw map reading, the raw cf_1 reading, and the reading corrected for humidity by the published Barkjohn equation.

The first two tables are live. Every hour the same sensors are set against AirNow, the EPA's real-time values for the same monitors, and the last 30 kept days of each monitor are scored the same way. AirNow values are real-time and not yet validated, so this reading can differ from the June to September read below, which used the validated record. The strip shows ten sensors for the last complete hour. The sensor service gives a reading every two minutes, but each reading costs points on a fixed account, so the panel reads once an hour.

Named here and want to answer a row? Respond

Each row is the share of monitors whose sensor does not meet the four targets, with a 95 percent interval over monitors (a monitor with several sensors counts once). ABOVE means the lower end is above one half. CONSISTENT means the upper end is below one half. Sensor and monitor values are 24 hour means on UTC days, because the sensor service gives daily means on UTC days. Days where the two sensor channels disagree by 5 micrograms and 61 percent or more are left out, the cleaning rule of the same paper. Sensors near monitors may not be typical of sensors elsewhere.
