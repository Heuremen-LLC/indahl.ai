Stated Chance of Precipitation Against What Happened

The National Weather Service states a chance of precipitation for each 12 hour period, and its chance is for measurable precipitation, 0.01 inch or more. For major United States airports, each forecast is stored when it is fetched, and each period is scored after it ends against that airport's hourly reports. If a stated 30 percent holds, about 30 percent of such periods should have measurable precipitation.

Two rules for what counts as precipitation are scored on the same periods. The NWS rule is the primary reading from 10 November 2026. It counts a period when the airport's routine hourly reports inside it add up to 0.01 inch or more, with a trace read as zero. The totals come from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive of those reports. The original rule was committed on 28 October 2026, before the first forecast was stored. It counts a period when any hourly report shows an amount above zero or reports rain, drizzle, snow, sleet, hail, showers or thunderstorm. That is wider than the chance it is scored against, so it counts more periods as wet.

The NWS rule was adopted after the data had been read, and the plan says so in a dated addendum. On 10 November, in the 10 to 20 percent cell at under 12 hours, the stated chance averaged 14.1 percent. Under the original rule 43.9 percent of those periods had an event (95% interval 34.9 to 53.4, above). Under the NWS rule 16.8 percent did (95% interval 10.9 to 25.0, cannot tell because the interval holds the 14.1 percent stated and the plan named no tolerance). Over all cells, the original rule read 12 of 18 cells above, 3 consistent and 3 cannot tell. The NWS rule read 3 of 18 above, all three in the 50 to 60 percent bin, 12 consistent and 3 cannot tell. Those tallies are the readings under the rule the plan named on 10 November. Under Standards 2.0 a cell whose interval holds the stated chance reads cannot tell, so the consistent cells in both tallies now read cannot tell. Both tables stay on this page, and the original is never deleted.

Above: it happened more often than stated. Below: less often. Cannot tell: the interval holds the stated chance (STRADDLES_EDGE), or the cell has under 30 periods (TOO_WIDE_TO_RESOLVE). The plan named no tolerance, so no cell reads consistent (plan addendum of 12 November 2026, Standards 2.0). Hourly reports can miss a short shower, so the observed frequency is a floor, and the chance floor is printed with each table. Periods from one storm are not independent. No ranking.

Plan, code and trend history for this page are public: forecast. Every plan was committed before the data it describes.

Data read from: National Weather Service API · Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS archive. Each station’s forecast is fetched at most once in 6 hours.
