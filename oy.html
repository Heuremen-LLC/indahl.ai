Studies checked against their own data10Bastani et al., PNAS 2025 (University of Pennsylvania), Cornell University, Spring 2025 generative AI surveys, Kestin et al. 2025 (Harvard University), Lin, EdArXiv 2026 (mj4bu), MIT committee report 2026, Oppenheimer et al., IJAIED 2025 (Carnegie Mellon University), Pardos & Bhandari, PLOS ONE 2024 (UC Berkeley), UMD Virtual Study Assistant trial, Oct 2026, UMN, UNC, UCI expert consensus report 2026, University of Chicago, use of Jabarian & Imas 2025
what we found
  • Bastani et al., PNAS 2025 (University of Pennsylvania): Table 1 rebuilds exactly. The exam effect, −0.054 (−17%), is significant under clustered standard errors (p ≈ 0.02) and not under a wild cluster bootstrap (p ≈ 0.11; 13 GPT-Base classrooms).
  • Cornell University, Spring 2025 generative AI surveys: An item count of 2,029 against 1,988 respondents; group labels total 2,539 on a base the files do not identify.
  • Kestin et al. 2025 (Harvard University): Post-test +0.65 [+0.40, +0.91] within the 122 students with both lessons; topic and week confounded; 72 of 194 “eligible” students have one lesson row; printed “M” values are medians.
  • Lin, EdArXiv 2026 (mj4bu): An LLM grader fails 47 of 100 answers after a synonym swap, against 7% in the null; every printed figure rebuilds.
  • MIT committee report 2026: No peer-reviewed learning-outcome study cited; 20 of 33 recommendations name no evidence; the headline 25% is a student-newspaper survey.
  • Oppenheimer et al., IJAIED 2025 (Carnegie Mellon University): Grades rebuild; 89% at the scale ceiling; the printed t(177) = 4.25 carries the p of a paired test, t(26) = 2.97, p = .006.
  • Pardos & Bhandari, PLOS ONE 2024 (UC Berkeley): Means rebuild exactly; “equivalent” holds only at margins of about 13.4 points or wider; the ChatGPT group starts 10–12 points lower.
  • UMD Virtual Study Assistant trial, Oct 2026: Final grade −0.32 to −0.37 SD in the exact-match sample; 41 instructors and 3,387 students registered, 30 and 2,379 analysed.
  • UMN, UNC, UCI expert consensus report 2026: Effect consensus on 42 of 65 statements; evidence consensus on 0 of 65.
  • University of Chicago, use of Jabarian & Imas 2025: The study rebuilds: 0 of 1,992 human texts flagged at the paper’s 0.5 cut; the corpus holds no student writing.
Own documents, own numbers3George Washington University, St. John Fisher University, University of Pennsylvania
what we found
  • George Washington University: Faculty Senate resolution SR 24/8: the text prints “a 476.2% increase in the ‘cheating’ category”; its own table prints 472.6%. The table prints 84.64% of reports as cheating; 209 of 256 is 81.64%. The table has no AI category.
  • St. John Fisher University: Abstract: blanket prohibition “dropped sharply from 57% in the fall of 2023 to 23% in the fall of 2025.” Its tables: any prohibition 57% to 59%; 25 of the 34 points fall at a change from one-choice to multi-choice forms.
  • University of Pennsylvania: Faculty Senate committee report on AI and student learning: no study cited, including Bastani et al., PNAS 2025, by Penn authors.
OY4Clarkson University, Denison University, Elon University, St. John Fisher University
what we found
  • Clarkson University: ChatGPT as a writing coach in the first-year seminar: pre and post surveys, n = 64 and 53; writing self-efficacy “did not improve.”
  • Denison University: Student survey series: 53% used AI for coursework in fall 2024, 78% in spring 2025, nearly 80% in October 2025; 55% agree AI in coursework “undermines the value of a college education.”
  • Elon University: Paired sections, “must cite” and “no AI” policies: 15 of 47 respondents in “no AI” sections and 36 of 72 in “must cite” sections report using AI on a coding assignment.
  • St. John Fisher University: 3,300+ syllabi coded over five semesters under its own AI syllabus policy: syllabi with no AI policy stated, 19.5% to 2.7%.
