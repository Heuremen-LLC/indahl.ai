Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement

This Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement (the "Agreement") is made on the date Counterparty accepts it between Heuremen LLC, a New Jersey limited liability company ("Heuremen"), and the organisation named by the person accepting it ("Counterparty"). Each is a "Party"; a Party disclosing information is the "Discloser" and a Party receiving it is the "Recipient".

1. Purpose

The Parties wish to discuss a possible engagement in which Heuremen would measure, review or advise on systems, data or results of interest to Counterparty (the "Purpose"). This Agreement governs information exchanged for the Purpose. It does not oblige either Party to enter any engagement.

2. Confidential Information

"Confidential Information" means non-public information disclosed by the Discloser to the Recipient for the Purpose, in any form, that is marked confidential or that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential from its nature and the circumstances of disclosure. It includes business plans, technical specifications, unpublished data, credentials and access details, and the existence and terms of the discussions between the Parties.

3. Exclusions

Confidential Information does not include information that the Recipient can show by written record:
(a) is or becomes publicly available through no breach of this Agreement by the Recipient;
(b) was known to or possessed by the Recipient before receipt from the Discloser;
(c) is received from a third party without a duty of confidentiality to the Discloser; or
(d) is independently developed by the Recipient without use of the Discloser's Confidential Information.

4. Independent work (no restriction on Heuremen's own research)

Counterparty acknowledges that Heuremen conducts and publishes independent research, including measurements of commercially available systems made on Heuremen's own accounts, from public records and from information described in §3. Nothing in this Agreement restricts Heuremen from conducting, publishing or commercialising such work, or any results, methods, code or findings that do not use Counterparty's Confidential Information, whether made before, during or after the term of this Agreement, and whether or not they concern systems that Counterparty makes, sells, uses or competes with. The burden of showing that a disclosure by Heuremen used Counterparty's Confidential Information rests on Counterparty.

5. Obligations

The Recipient shall: use Confidential Information only for the Purpose; protect it with at least the care it uses for its own information of similar sensitivity, and no less than reasonable care; and disclose it only to its employees, members, contractors and professional advisers who need to know it for the Purpose and are bound by confidentiality obligations no less protective than these.

5A. Contact and premises

(a) "Heuremen Personnel" means Heuremen's members, managers, employees, contractors and agents. "Related Person" means any person with a personal, professional, social or family relationship with any Heuremen Personnel, including a spouse, partner, parent, child, sibling or other direct relative. "Heuremen Premises" means any property, office, facility or holding owned, leased, used or operated by Heuremen or its divisions, including their systems and accounts.
(b) Counterparty shall not, and shall cause its employees, contractors, agents and advisers not to, initiate or conduct any contact or engagement with any Heuremen Personnel or Related Person concerning the Purpose, Heuremen's work or the Confidential Information, or enter any Heuremen Premises, unless and until this Agreement has been accepted by Counterparty and confirmed by Heuremen under §12. After that, all such contact goes through the contact Heuremen names in writing.
(c) Information obtained through contact or entry in breach of (b) is Heuremen's Confidential Information under this Agreement, and is not covered by the exclusions in §3(b) or §3(c).

6. Compelled disclosure

If the Recipient is required by law, regulation or court order to disclose Confidential Information, it may do so after giving the Discloser prompt notice (where lawful) so the Discloser may seek protection, and shall disclose only what is legally required.

7. Return or destruction

On the Discloser's written request, or at the end of the discussions, the Recipient shall return or destroy the Discloser's Confidential Information within thirty (30) days, except copies retained in automatic backups or as required by law, which remain subject to this Agreement.

8. No licence, no warranty

All Confidential Information remains the property of the Discloser. No licence under any patent, copyright or other right is granted except the limited right to use it for the Purpose. Confidential Information is provided "as is".

9. Term

This Agreement covers disclosures made within two (2) years of its date. Obligations for each item of Confidential Information last three (3) years from its disclosure, and for trade secrets and credentials for as long as they remain such.

10. Remedies

Unauthorised use or disclosure may cause irreparable harm, and the Discloser may seek injunctive relief in addition to any other remedy, without posting bond where permitted.

11. General

This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of New Jersey, without regard to its conflict-of-laws rules; the Parties submit to the state and federal courts located in New Jersey. It is the entire agreement on its subject, may be amended only in writing signed by both Parties, and may be signed electronically and in counterparts. Neither Party may assign it without the other's consent, except to a successor of its business. Nothing in it creates an agency, partnership or obligation to purchase.

11A. Severability

If any provision of this Agreement is held unenforceable, it shall be enforced to the maximum extent permitted and the remaining provisions remain in full force.

12. Electronic acceptance

Counterparty may accept this Agreement electronically through Heuremen's inquiry process. Acceptance is made by a person who reads this Agreement, confirms their authority to bind Counterparty, and gives their full name, title and organisation from an identity verified by that process. Heuremen records the version of this Agreement accepted, the time, and the verified identity. The Agreement takes effect when Heuremen confirms acceptance in writing. Each Party agrees that this electronic acceptance and record have the same effect as a handwritten signature.

Privacy Notice

This notice explains how Heuremen LLC (“Heuremen”, “we”, “us”), including all of its divisions, collects, uses, shares, protects and retains personal information through indahl.ai, heuremen.org, our inquiry form, our correspondence and our engagements. We hold the information of everyone who contacts us, and of any client, in confidence.

Information we collect

When you visit our websites

Our websites set no cookies and run no analytics, advertising, tracking or session-recording scripts. indahl.ai is delivered through Cloudflare and heuremen.org is hosted on GitHub Pages. Each processes standard request data (IP address, browser type, the page requested and the time) to deliver and protect the site. The pages of indahl.ai serve their typefaces from our own site. One page of heuremen.org, the correlations page, loads typefaces from Google Fonts, which receives the requesting IP address and browser information.

If you install indahl.ai as an app on your phone or computer, your browser keeps a copy of the public pages you open on your own device so that they open without a connection. We receive nothing from that copy and it holds no personal information from you. Removing the app or clearing the site’s data deletes it. Live data feeds, the inquiry form and sign-in are never kept in it. The same app code shows the notifications you turn on, as described below.

When you send a correction or removal request

The correction or removal form collects what you enter: your name, an email address we can answer, the page concerned and your request, with the time it arrived and the country your connection reports. We use it only to handle the request, to answer you, and where needed to confirm that a removal request comes from the person or organisation named. It is never posted. A short one-way hash of your network address is kept for one hour to limit repeated requests, and the address itself is not kept.

When you submit an inquiry

The inquiry form collects what you enter: your name, organisation email address, organisation, role, a description of what you need (including the systems or results involved and your organisation’s relationships with relevant vendors), an approximate budget range and timeline, who referred you, and your confirmations regarding non-disclosure, authority and this notice. For an engagement inquiry we also record the identity you verified (your work email address, the name on that account, the sign-in method and the identifier our sign-in provider assigns), and your acceptance of our Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement and this notice (which versions, the time, that you read both to the end, and your name, title and organisation). Nothing is recorded until you submit the inquiry. We also record the time of the inquiry and the country the request came from. We do not store your IP address. A truncated one-way hash of it, which may still be personal data, is held for up to one hour only to limit repeat submissions. Each accepted inquiry is also emailed to us.

When you use the sign-up form

One sign-up form sits at the foot of the pages of indahl.ai and on the email updates page. It collects your email address and the boxes you tick. The boxes are the weekly newsletter, new desks and new data, outcome changes on every desk, and on a desk page, a division page or a brand page that desk, that division or the desks with rows about that organisation. The email updates page lists every desk and division to tick. The form also asks how alerts and updates should come: as they happen, in one daily digest or in one weekly digest on Tuesdays. The newsletter box needs no confirmation. The other boxes need a confirmation by email first, as described below. If you tick the newsletter and another box, the newsletter starts at once and the rest waits for your confirmation.

For the weekly newsletter we store your email address with the time you subscribed, the version of this notice in force then, and a random unsubscribe token. We use it for one thing: to email you the weekly changes page every Tuesday. Every newsletter email has a one-click unsubscribe link. Unsubscribing deletes your address and its token from the list at once. While you are subscribed we also keep a truncated one-way hash of your email address, only so that a second sign-up does not make a duplicate. It is deleted when you unsubscribe. A truncated one-way hash of your IP address is held for up to one hour only to limit repeat sign-ups. We never share, sell or rent the list, and we never use it for anything other than this email. The addresses are kept in a private storage area accessible only to us and are never published, logged or posted.

Alerts, new desks and new data

For every other box we store your email address, the desks, pages, divisions and organisations you ticked, the companies and other named things you follow (such as a model, a quantum backend or a school), whether you asked for every desk, whether you asked for new desks and new data and how you want alerts and updates to come, with the time you signed up and the version of this notice in force then. We then email one confirmation link to that address. Nothing else is sent until you open the link and press Confirm. A sign-up that is not confirmed is deleted after two days. Once you confirm, we store the time you confirmed and a random token for your manage and unsubscribe links. We use the address for what you ticked and nothing else: an email when an outcome changes on a desk you follow, an email when the published data changes on a desk, page or division you follow, an email when a row about a company or other named thing you follow changes or a new row about it appears, and an email when a new desk goes live, a desk publishes its first value under its change rule or a release adds an entry to the changelog. A digest puts the same emails into one a day or one a week. Our sending log keeps the first characters of your token next to each alert sent to it, and for a digest the time of the last one, with no address, so that nothing is sent twice. Those lines stay in the log after you unsubscribe, with no address and no way back to one. Every alert has a link to change what you get, where you can also add or remove the weekly newsletter. A newsletter ticked there starts at once, since the address is already confirmed. Every alert also has a one-click unsubscribe link, which removes your alerts and your new desks and new data emails. The newsletter has its own one-click link in each issue. On the manage page, Unsubscribe from everything removes both. Unsubscribing from alerts deletes your address, your choices and the token at once. While you are signed up we also keep a truncated one-way hash of your email address, only so that a second confirmation joins your existing record. It is deleted when you unsubscribe. The search for a company or other named thing on the alerts page reads one public list of names from our site and searches it in your browser. What you type is not sent to us. A truncated one-way hash of your IP address is held for up to one hour, and a truncated one-way hash of your email address for up to one day, only to limit repeat sign-ups and confirmation emails. We never share, sell or rent the list, and we never use it for anything other than these emails. The addresses are kept in a private storage area accessible only to us and are never published, logged or posted.

Feeds, the calendar and the OPML file

The feeds, the calendar file and the OPML file on the follow page are ordinary files. Fetching them sends the same request data as any page, and we keep no list of who reads them. Each feed names the public WebSub hub at pubsubhubbub.appspot.com, run by Google. When a feed changes we send that hub the address of the feed and nothing else. A feed reader that subscribes through the hub deals with Google directly, under Google’s terms.

Notifications on your device

If you press “Notify me on this device” and allow notifications, your browser creates a push subscription and we store it: the push address your browser’s push service gives it, the two public keys your browser makes for encrypting messages to it, the desks you picked, the time, the version of this notice in force then and a random token. Your browser keeps that token on your device, in its local storage and in the site’s cache storage, so the button can show whether notifications are on and so the app code can move your notifications to a new push address when your browser’s push service replaces the old one. When that happens we replace the push address and keys on your record and keep your desks. We use the subscription for one thing: to send a short notification when an outcome changes on a desk you picked. Each notification is encrypted for your browser and passes through your browser’s push service (Google for Chrome, Mozilla for Firefox, Apple for Safari, Microsoft for Edge), which cannot read it. Each message we send carries our contact address for the push service, as the Web Push standard asks. Our sending log records the first characters of the token, the time and the result of each run, never the push address or the keys. Pressing the button again deletes the subscription. When a push service tells us a subscription has ended, we delete it. A truncated one-way hash of the push address is kept with it only so that the same device does not make a second record, and a truncated one-way hash of your IP address is held for up to one hour only to limit repeat sign-ups.

Webhooks

If you register a webhook, we store the address you enter, the desks you picked, whether it is a signed webhook or a Slack, Discord or Microsoft Teams address, the signing secret we make for a signed webhook, the time, the version of this notice in force then and a random token for your manage link. A truncated one-way hash of the address is kept so that it is registered once, and a truncated one-way hash of your IP address is held for up to one hour only to limit repeat registrations. When you register, our server sends the address one challenge, or one test message for a chat app. After that we send it only outcome changes on the desks you picked. If deliveries fail ten times in a row we record when the webhook was turned off and the count. A delivery waiting for a retry is kept in our sending queue with the token and the changes it carries, and is removed once it is sent or dropped. Our sending log records the first characters of the token, the time and the result of each attempt, never the address or the secret. Deleting the webhook from its manage page erases the address, the secret and the hash at once. An address you register may itself carry personal information, for example a name in a host name, so please register only addresses you control.

Downloading or deleting your data

On the manage page of an email or webhook sign-up, and at Your data for notifications on a device, you can download everything we hold for that sign-up as a JSON file, or delete all of it at once. Send logs keep only the first characters of the sign-up token, never the address, and a deletion does not change them. A deletion gives you a receipt that holds the kind of record and the time, nothing else. We keep deletion receipts for 400 days.

Counts on our transparency page

Our transparency page counts alerts sent, sign-ups removed and deletions, for everyone together. To count removals, we keep on our own computer a list of one-way hashes of the storage keys of current sign-ups. A hash cannot be turned back into an address, link or device, and it is dropped at the next count after its sign-up is gone. Counts under 5 are shown as fewer than 5, and a channel total is shown only when at least 5 different recipients are behind it. We run our own test webhook to check that alerts arrive, and it is left out of every count. Alert receipts name the desk, the change and the kinds of channel that carried it, and hold nothing about who received it. Signatures on our feeds, API answers and webhooks add nothing to what we collect.

When you add a PDF link under a Recount Matrix

The one-field box under each Recount Matrix stores the link you paste, the table it was added under and the time. It stores no name, email address or IP address. A truncated one-way hash of your IP address is held for up to one hour only to limit repeat submissions. We read each link before anything changes in a table.

When you add a response

The response form stores the reference of the page you answered, the link to your PDF, the host name of that link and the time. It stores no name, email address or IP address. A truncated one-way hash of your IP address is held for up to one hour only to limit repeat submissions. We read each file before anything is posted. A posted response shows its date and the host it came from.

When we correspond or work together

If we reply to an inquiry or enter an engagement, we keep our correspondence, the agreements we sign (including non-disclosure agreements and statements of work), invoicing records, and the data you provide for the engagement.

Information we do not seek

We do not ask for, and ask you not to send through the form, payment card details, government identification numbers, health information or other special-category data. Engagement data containing such information is handled only under a written agreement that provides for it.

How we use it

  • To review inquiries for fit, independence (including conflicts with vendors we measure) and authority, and to reply.
  • To negotiate, perform and invoice engagements.
  • To email the weekly changes page to the addresses that asked for it.
  • To email outcome alerts, and news of new desks and new data, to the addresses that confirmed them.
  • To send the device notifications and webhook deliveries you turned on.
  • To keep our websites secure, and to detect and prevent abuse of the inquiry form.
  • To meet legal, tax and accounting obligations, and to establish or defend legal claims.

We do not sell personal information, share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, use it for automated decision-making with legal or similarly significant effects, or use it to train models.

Legal bases

Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, we rely on our legitimate interests in reviewing inquiries and running our business securely. We also rely on steps taken at your request before entering a contract, and on the performance of that contract. We rely on compliance with legal obligations. Where we ask for it, we rely on your consent, which you may withdraw at any time.

Who we share it with

We share personal information only with service providers that process it on our behalf and under their contractual obligations: Cloudflare (delivery of indahl.ai, the storage of the inquiry form, the weekly newsletter list, the alerts list, push subscriptions and webhooks, and email delivery), the push service your browser uses (delivery of the notifications you turn on, encrypted so that it cannot read them), WorkOS (verifying the work identity of people who send an engagement inquiry), GitHub (hosting of heuremen.org), Google Workspace (our email), and Google Fonts (typefaces on one page of heuremen.org). We disclose information when required by law or valid legal process, to protect our rights or the safety of others, or in connection with a merger, acquisition or sale of assets, in which case this notice continues to apply to the information transferred. We never name a client, partner or engagement without written consent.

International transfers

We are based in the United States, and our service providers may process information in other countries. Where the law requires it, transfers are made under appropriate safeguards such as the European Commission’s standard contractual clauses relied on by those providers.

How long we keep it

  • Inquiries that do not lead to an engagement, and the copy emailed to us: up to 24 months from receipt, then deleted.
  • Correction or removal requests, and the copy emailed to us: up to 24 months from receipt, then deleted.
  • Engagement records, agreements and invoices: for the life of the engagement and as long afterwards as contract, tax and legal obligations require.
  • Engagement data you provide: returned or deleted at the end of the engagement on request, except where we must keep it by law.
  • Weekly newsletter addresses: until you unsubscribe, then deleted at once.
  • Sign-ups for alerts or new desks and new data that are not confirmed: two days, then deleted. Confirmed addresses for alerts or new desks and new data: until you unsubscribe, then deleted at once.
  • The one-way hashes used to limit repeat submissions, sign-ups and registrations: one hour for a network address, and one day for an email address entered for alerts or new desks and new data.
  • The one-way hash of a subscribed email address that prevents duplicates: until you unsubscribe, then deleted at once.
  • Push subscriptions for notifications on your device, and the hash that prevents duplicates: until you turn notifications off with the button or the push service tells us the subscription has ended, then deleted at once.
  • Webhooks, their signing secrets and the hash that prevents duplicates: until you delete the webhook from its manage page, then deleted at once. A webhook that was turned off after failed deliveries is kept, turned off, until you delete it.
  • The sending logs for notifications and webhooks, which hold no address, key or secret: kept as operational records of what was sent.
  • Inquiry cookies. A signed cookie records that you accepted the agreements and is kept up to two hours. A cookie that ties a sign-in to your browser is kept up to ten minutes. A signed cookie holds your verified identity while you complete an engagement inquiry and is kept up to 45 minutes. We set no other cookies and no tracking cookies.

How we protect it

Inquiries are stored in a private storage area accessible only to us. We use the least access an engagement needs, prefer credentials that clients own and can revoke, never write credentials into code or reports, and keep client data separate from our published research. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, and we will notify affected people and authorities of a breach where the law requires.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to know what personal information we hold about you, to receive a copy, to have it corrected or deleted, to restrict or object to its use, and to withdraw consent. California residents have the rights to know, delete and correct, and to non-discrimination for exercising them. We do not sell or share personal information as those terms are defined. We honour Global Privacy Control signals, although we have no sale or sharing to opt out of. To make a request, use the correction or removal form, which is open to anyone and needs no agreement and no work email, or the inquiry form with “Privacy request” as the reason. We reply to every privacy request, verify your request against the information we hold and respond within the time the law allows. You may also complain to your local data protection authority.

Children

Our websites and services are intended for organisations and are not directed to anyone under 16. We do not knowingly collect information from children.

Changes to this notice

We will post any change here with a new effective date. Material changes will be noted at the top of this page.

Contact

Heuremen LLC. For any question about this notice, use the correction or removal form, or the inquiry form with “Privacy request” as the reason.
