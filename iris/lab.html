YO: A Small Model Trained to Audit IBM’s Records

YO v7 is the model at work. In YO v4’s planned test on IBM records held out from training, it found 905 of 928 qubits stale by one to two days in records whose eight qubits all sat near that line, where YO v2 found 403. YO v5, trained further on same-date items (8 qubits from one record that share a calibration date), found 2,312 (seed 47) and 2,297 (seed 48) of 2,320 stale qubits in such items, where YO v4 found 2,240. Seed 47’s gain is above zero and seed 48’s cannot be told from zero. Seed 47 was below YO v4 on the share of its flags that were right and seed 48 on the verdicts, so YO v4 stays at work. YO v6, trained further on young groups, flagged 2 and 1 of the 1,974 young qubits sitting beside a stale one with its two seeds, where YO v4 flagged 94, but neither seed’s gain on older same-date stale qubits was clear, one seed was below YO v4 on whole-window recall and the other found fewer stale neighbours, so YO v4 stays at work. YO v7, trained again with YO v4’s whole-window and band items restored, found 0.993 to 1.000 of the 2,320 older same-date stale qubits across the three retest seeds, where YO v4 found 0.969, without being shown to flag more young qubits, so by the rule written before training YO v7 seed 51 now does the live reads (one of the first two seeds failed). YO v8, trained further on verdicts near the edges of the rule, scored higher on exact border verdicts than YO v7 in all three seeds (0.754 to 0.769 against 0.729, one seed’s band reaching zero) but each of its three seeds did worse on another reading, so by the rule written before training YO v7 stays at work. YO Read, a second adapter trained to read quantum-hardware papers, was scored again on its 148 held-out papers under a rule fixed and timestamped before the run, after 22 of those papers were found to have been given the wrong text (the answers it is scored against did not change; that check, and the decision to rescore, were made after we had seen the first scores, so the rescore is a correction defined after the data, under a rule fixed before it ran). On whether a paper prints the dates of its hardware runs, two of its three seeds beat always answering no (0.919 and 0.939 of papers right, against 0.804), and the third does not (0.831), because it says yes too readily: it catches 28 of the 29 papers that print dates but also says yes on 24 of the 119 that do not. On whether a paper links its own data or code, all three seeds beat always answering no (0.811, 0.757 and 0.811, against 0.514), which is a low bar, and 5 of the 148 answers it is scored against there are still wrong: our code read them as yes where the List entry says the paper prints no link. On whether a paper says its data are available on request, the seeds score 0.980, 0.959 and 0.966 against 0.912 for always answering no, but none survives the correction for testing several questions at once, and with only 13 papers that say so, this test is too small to show a gain reliably. Each paper counts once per question, and three seeds are a count of trained copies, not a rate. Two further checks of YO Read were planned and timestamped before they ran. In the first, three copies were trained on the same papers with the yes and no answers shuffled between papers. None of the three beat always answering no on dates or on data links, so three adapters trained on shuffled answers did not reproduce the gain. On data links two of them scored below always answering no (63 and 69 of the 148 papers right, against 76). In the second, three copies were trained on the base model’s own answers instead of the real labels. On dates YO Read beat two of them and not the third, which was paired with seed 63, the seed that also did not beat always answering no. On data links YO Read was ahead of all three by 7 to 16 of the 148 papers, but no gap survives the correction for testing several comparisons at once. On dates and data links YO Read was right on more papers than its partner in all six pairs, a reading defined after the data. So on dates and data links the gain is not shown to need the real labels, and three seeds may be too few to show a gap this size. On data available on request YO Read beat all three, and this is not evidence that the real labels matter. Before training, the base model’s own answers said yes on 28% of the training papers where the real share is 11%, so that comparison was marked a weak test, and the three copies trained on those answers fell below always answering no (95 to 105 of the 148 papers right, against 135). YO Read itself did not beat always answering no on that question with any of its three seeds. On dates the base model’s answers were close to the real share in the 497 training papers (25% against 22%) and not in the 43 validation papers (37% against 16%). The plan did not say which of those two sets decides the flag, so both are shown, and no conclusion depends on which. All of these results measure how well YO Read matches the List reader’s notes, which record what a paper leaves out, not whether it reads each paper correctly. On data links 5 of the 148 answers it is scored against are known to be wrong, because our code read the note as yes where it says no link is printed (arXiv 2210.17548, 2304.04666, 2306.01639, 2401.10293 and 2406.09316), and 7 of the answers it was trained on have the same fault. A third check was planned and timestamped, and has now run once. The same nine adapters answered 288 papers on the List that were not in their training or test sets. The result is read on 88 of them, papers screened for same work: the List reader read them in full and wrote a note on each, and papers that are the same work as one already used were taken out. That is too few papers to show a small gain, so this test is underpowered. On those 88 papers, YO Read agreed with the List reader on data available on request better than the best fixed answer and better than the copies trained on the base model’s own answers, on all three seeds. That result rests on the 12 papers whose answer is yes: YO Read found 10 to 11 of them (86, 87 and 87 of the 88 papers right, against 76 for always answering no), and it never got wrong a paper that always answering no got right. On run dates the result depends on four labels: it holds on the 88 papers and not when the four labels we already knew were wrong or doubtful are taken out, so by the rule fixed before the run it is not shown. On data links the result was not shown: YO Read was right on 69, 67 and 70 of the 88 papers, against 70 for always answering yes, and these data cannot rule out a useful effect. The run dates reading is sensitive to which papers are included. With the one same-work paper described below taken out, run dates replicates under the same rule, against depends on four labels with it in. The stamped verdict, depends on four labels, stands. One paper moving one seed across the threshold is the fragility, not evidence for run dates. Including that paper lowers run dates, so depends on four labels is the conservative call. Its answers are no on all three questions, and so are the answers on the paper it was translated from, in the training file. YO Read said yes on its run dates with one seed and on its data links with all three, against both papers’ answers, so those answers are not memory of the trained paper. The 88 still contain one known version of a trained paper (zenodo.18154889, a Turkish translation of a paper YO Read was trained on) and one borderline later edition (LCL-833, the same programme in a later edition). The 88 stay the headline as planned, and the readings without them are published beside it. Every check fixed before the results were opened gives the same verdict on data available on request. On the full 288, which include 5 papers whose own text was in training, no verdict is read. One of the three copies trained on shuffled answers said no to every item, so that seed of the check collapsed into always answering no. It was scored as it is, and a collapsed check is weaker evidence than a working one. The plan was stamped in a Bitcoin block before the run, and the answers in a later block when it ended. The run happened once. Six notes added to the plan (A7 to A12) were written after the answers were stamped. A7 is in Bitcoin block 974624 and A8 to A12 are in block 974635, both before the results were opened, but that the answers were unread until then is our own word. Every sentence in the training files is fixed by Bitcoin block 974519, and the training files of the first seed, with their answers, are vouched for by its stamped adapter, reproduced bit for bit. The answers used for the base-model and shuffled copies are kept in git only. The spreads around these differences were worked out after the data. As before, this measures how well YO Read matches the List reader’s notes, not whether it reads the papers correctly. The list of limits is published with the plan. YO Read is not in use yet: using it as a second reading of each paper before a QIA notice goes out waits on our word. YO v7 reads each new IBM record live on the QIA page.

Tables, method and data

YO is Qwen3-1.7B, a small open-weight model (Apache-2.0), trained with LoRA to read IBM’s published calibration records the way an auditor does. It has two jobs. The first is to flag qubits whose T1 or T2 was measured more than a day before the record’s stamp, and error values of exactly zero. The second is to give one of four verdicts on a measured value against a stated one: ABOVE, BELOW, CONSISTENT or CANNOT_TELL. The records are IBM’s own calibration history for every processor our account can see, up to 180 days back. The newest month is held out as the test.

The plan was committed before any data was built, and the baselines were scored on the test items before training began. The test reading was read once, after the run, and checked by a referee before it was posted. YO v3 then continued training YO v2 on qubits stale by one to two days and was not adopted. YO v4 continued training YO v2 on records whose qubits all went stale together, under a third plan committed before its data, was tested on records no earlier test used, and replaced YO v2 as the model at work. The Two Finalists, Head to Head

Every Fault family reads cannot tell in both head-to-head rounds, and no winner is named.

Tables, method and data

After eleven rounds, the two models with the lowest mean Fault rate among readable replies under the Guard wording were smollm2:1.7b and qwen3:4b. That mean included families in which fewer than 10 replies could be read, which the written rule leaves out. With every cannot tell family left out, the two lowest are smollm2:1.7b and olmo2:7b, and qwen3:4b has no readable family. The head to head below uses the two models as first chosen and was not re-run with olmo2:7b. The selection rule and the reading rule were committed on 27 October, before any round was read. The scoring rules that read replies were amended afterwards in dated addenda, as the note under the table says. Both finalists then answered the same two fresh question sets, rounds 12 and 13, in the same arms at temperature 0.
