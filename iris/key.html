IRIS codes: what moves a language-model result besides the model

Intervals are 95%, by percentile bootstrap over items unless the row says otherwise.

CodeNameWhat happensMeasuredIndexed studies where it touches the data
L1Option orderThe answer changes when only the order of the options changes.falcon3:3b 42.5%, mistral:7b 27.3%, phi4-mini 27.8%, smollm2:1.7b 50.0% of answers change with the options reversed, net of the exact repeat (our setup, four-option questions). GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet 4.5 and Gemini 2.5 Flash flip the verdict on 31.0%, 31.3% and 30.9% of prompts when the two answers swap position. That is a different measure, taken from an earlier experiment in the judge calls released with arXiv 2610.30512, run once per judge with no repeat floor (our recount). The pinned October GPT-4.1 runs are the only ones with a repeat floor. Their flip is 12.5 points [8.0, 16.7] above it.arXiv 2610.30512
L2Prompt wordingThe answer changes when only the wording or format of the prompt changes.With the answer format changed (the story, question and options without labels, and a reply of the letter only): gemma3:4b 11.3%, llama3.1:8b 12.0%, mistral:7b 13.1% and olmo2:7b 20.8% of answers change, net of the exact repeat (our setup, four-option questions). For falcon3:3b, smollm2:1.7b, granite3.3:2b and phi4-mini more than 10% of the 200 items could not be read in the new format, so those cells are cannot tell.arXiv 2610.30512
L3Repeat spreadThe same setup run again, with another seed, or on another GPU or host gives different answers.Our setup, on our Mac server: an exact repeat gave a different answer on none of the 173 to 200 readable items for seven of the eight models read and on 1 of 195 for falcon3:3b. This is a determinism check, not a noise floor. Mamba-2.8B with the same seed on a different GPU and host: 76 of 8,000 question-position cells (0.95%) change, A40 against A10G (from the files released with arXiv 2610.31135).arXiv 2610.30512, arXiv 2610.31135, arXiv 2610.25626
L4Length limitReplies are cut off by the length setting before an answer is given.deepseek-r1:1.5b 108 of 200 and qwen3:4b 191 of 200 replies hit our length limit, so their cells are not read.—
L5Serving backendOutcomes differ between reruns of the same model name, and differ more on prompts where the provider’s reported serving backend changed between the runs.GPT-4.1 as judge: the outcome changed on 27.3% of prompts where the reported serving backend changed between two runs and 17.5% where it did not, +9.8 points [+2.1, +17.6], one run pair. This splits prompts by a reported fingerprint and does not name a cause. Prompts with a changed backend sat somewhat nearer the decision boundary, and within margin bands (defined after the data) the gap is +8.0 points [+0.7, +15.3] (from the files released with arXiv 2610.30512).arXiv 2610.30512
L6Answer reading ruleThe score depends on the rule used to read the answer out of the reply.F1 moves 6.13 points [4.43, 7.83] on the adversarial split, 5.37 on popular and 3.15 on random depending on which yes or no tokens are read. This is one model, LLaVA-1.5-7B at 4-bit precision, so we cannot say how much of any reported gain a matched evaluation would keep (from the files released with arXiv 2610.25964). On arXiv 2610.17203, our planned rule scores a judge by the share of its 16 picks per item. Under it the GPT-5.6-Sol judge scores 72.9 against 74.5 printed. The printed 74.5 rebuilds exactly only under a rule found after the data, which scores the judge by its more-picked answer.arXiv 2610.25964, arXiv 2610.17203
L7Model as referenceA model’s output is the reference or the judge the results are scored against.The GPT labeller's gold labels, once reviewed, give 0.960 accuracy and put it ahead of every trained classifier by 0.115 to 0.145. The gold was built by reviewing that labeller's output, so the lead cannot be separated from anchoring (arXiv 2610.25159). Paired on items decisive for both, the Gemini 3.1 Pro judge agrees with the reader majority 0.657 [0.486, 0.800] of the time on G1 against G2, consistent with chance, and 0.839 [0.710, 0.968] on G1 against G0, above chance (arXiv 2610.21468).arXiv 2610.25159, arXiv 2610.21468, arXiv 2610.17203
L8BatchingSeveral items go into one call, and the answers differ from one item per call.Labelling 4 or 8 records per call agrees with single-record labels 0.02 to 0.03 less than two single-record runs agree with each other (from the files released with arXiv 2610.24999).arXiv 2610.24999, arXiv 2610.15732
L9Answer options offeredAdding or removing a “none” or abstain option changes the score.Allowing a “none” answer raises macro-F1 by +0.21 for GPT, +0.27 for Gemini and +0.20 for Claude (from the files released with arXiv 2610.15732). Our setup, with a none option offered: gemma3:4b gives a false “none” on 0.755 [0.696, 0.815] of persona items. We counted a reply as none by a rule stated after we had seen the replies, and our prompt or format may drive this rate.arXiv 2610.15732
