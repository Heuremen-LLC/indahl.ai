# QIA queue ticker: plan (29 Oct 2026, committed before the first read)
What it shows: for every IBM processor our paid instance can see, the number of jobs IBM reports as pending, read once per hour of Nova ticks (every 9 ticks).
Beside it: the wait our own jobs saw on that processor, measured as the time from created to running in IBM's job record. Median and longest of the last 20 finished jobs, and the age of any job still queued.
Rules:
- Pending count is IBM's number read through the runtime service. We add nothing to it and do not turn it into a predicted wait. IBM publishes no estimate and we publish none.
- Our waits are our jobs only. Different job sizes wait differently, so a processor with fewer than 3 finished jobs of ours shows no median.
- Every read is saved to data/history.jsonl, one row per processor per read. A failed read is saved as NOTHING_RETURNED, never skipped.
- The page shows the last reading and the change against the earliest reading inside the last 24 hours.
- No QPU seconds are used. Keys are read from key files and passed straight to the service.
Outcomes per read: COMMITTED (a count came back) or NOTHING_RETURNED.

## Note 1 (29 Oct, committed before seven days of readings exist): the analysis this record is for
Question: does the number of pending jobs IBM reports predict how long our own jobs wait?
Rule, fixed now. When 7 days of hourly readings are held, each finished job of ours is joined to the last reading of its processor taken before the job was created (a job with no reading in the 90 minutes before it is dropped and the number dropped is reported). For each processor with at least 20 joined jobs, the Spearman rank correlation between pending count and wait, with a bootstrap 95% interval over jobs (4,000 resamples, seed 7). Outcome per processor: ABOVE if the interval sits wholly above 0.3, BELOW if wholly below 0 and the pending counts spanned at least a factor of 3, CONSISTENT if wholly within -0.3 to 0.3, otherwise CANNOT_TELL. Fewer than 20 joined jobs, or pending counts spanning less than a factor of 3: CANNOT_TELL.
Our jobs are not a random sample of jobs: they come from a few runs, mostly short. The result is a statement about our jobs only. The reading is shown on /qia only after it is computed under this rule, and the page says how many jobs were joined and dropped.


## Note (29 Oct, from the internal audit)
This plan did not state its unit and what it does not claim in one place. Stated now, with no change to any rule, read or outcome. Unit: one hourly reading of one processor's pending count, and one finished job of ours for the wait. Not claimed: a predicted wait, that our jobs stand for anyone else's, or anything about IBM's scheduling policy.


## Note 2 (29 Oct, from the internal audit)
The analysis rule in Note 1 bootstraps over jobs. Our jobs come from a few runs and many share one earlier reading, so they are not independent. The first analysis will also be read with runs as the unit and the number of runs printed beside the interval. The rule's outcome is read on the run-level interval where the two differ.

## Addendum (29 Oct)
The reading cadence was every 9 ticks of the tick bus, about 52 minutes, to match the hourly ticker asked for on 28 Oct. It is now every 2 ticks, about 12 minutes, so a rise or fall in a chip's queue shows within minutes. The call is one request per processor and costs no quantum seconds. Nothing else changes. The queue against wait analysis (Note 1) uses the same rule and reads the denser series the same way.

## Addendum, 29 Oct 2026 (his "a python run that only tests the queue ... updated hourly"): queue probes
IBM publishes no wait time, so a wait is measured by sending a job. queue_probe.py sends the smallest real job (one qubit, X, measure, one shot, tag qia-queue-probe) to one operational processor per run, the one probed longest ago, and logs its wait (created to running) and its charged seconds in data/probes.jsonl. Rides her ticks every 11 (about an hour), so each processor is re-timed about every 6 hours. Measured cost of one probe on ibm_fez, 30 Oct 00:15 UTC: wait 1.2 s, charged 2 s. Guards: at most one probe waiting per processor, at most 50 s charged to probes in 24 hours, none if the account is under 600 s. Probe waits enter the existing "our median wait" (last 20 of our jobs per processor), so they are our own jobs like any other.
- Tested 30 Oct 00:17 UTC (his question: can a job that will not work give the wait for free?): two one-shot jobs on ibm_fez that could not run normally, a 10 s delay (ran) and a 1000 s delay (Error 6050 at the front of the queue, value does not fit u32). Both got a running stamp (waits 1.2 s and 1.0 s) and both were charged 2 s. IBM charges its 2 s minimum once a job reaches the front, even when it fails there. A job cancelled while still queued is not charged but gets no running stamp, so it carries no wait. So the free reading is jobs in front (backend status), and a measured wait costs 2 s. Script: dud_test.py.
- 30 Oct 00:20 UTC, his "we just need to know how long it is": a one-shot job queued at the back of ibm_phoenix (2,308 in front) and cancelled while still queued was charged 0 s, but IBM's server record for it held only "Queued": no position, no expected start (line_peek.py). The length of each line is IBM's jobs-in-front count, read free. The hourly probe rider is stopped. queue_probe.py stays for a timed wait on request (2 s each).
- 30 Oct 00:23 UTC, his "labs should check QIA for the fez wait time": the paid plan skips most of the line (kingston: our jobs ran in about 1 s with about 330 in front), so its waits do not tell a lab what to expect. The Open plan, the free shared line, is probed instead: queue_probe.py --open on his Parallax key's open instance (fez, kingston, marrakesh are the Open plan processors), one one-shot probe per run every 26 of her ticks (about 10 a day), 2 s each, capped 20 s a day, never below 200 s of the Open plan's 600 s per 28 days. Each probe stores the jobs in front at sending and its wait. The table's "Approx. wait, Open plan" = jobs in front now x the median seconds per job in front from those probes, labelled with the probe count; "not on Open plan" for the other processors.
- 30 Oct 00:25 UTC, his "why are you limiting us to the open plan ... we have every us east and europe plan": probes now go to every processor about every hour on both paid instances (Darkwave Disco, us-east: boston fez kingston marrakesh phoenix pittsburgh; Quaker, eu-de: aachen), 2 s each, floors 100 s and 50 s; the Open plan probe stays as before. A status-only collector every 2 ticks writes finished waits within minutes. The QIA headline table shows "Wait now, paid plan" (latest probe, or how long it has waited so far) and "Approx. wait, Open plan". The public probe is indahl.ai/qia/probe.py (also code/qia/probe.py in the public repo): --front for free jobs-in-front, a processor name for one timed probe on the user's own key.
