# FEDSEP: the Federal Reserve's stated projection error range against what happened. Plan, written before any outcome is compared.

## Claim read
Each Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) of the Federal Open Market Committee carries Table 2, "Average Historical Projection Error Ranges", with the note that "under certain assumptions, there is about a 70 percent probability that actual outcomes for real GDP, unemployment, consumer prices, and the federal funds rate will be in ranges implied by the average size of projection errors made in the past", read from the September 2025 SEP at federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20250917.htm. The ranges are plus or minus the root mean squared error of past projections released in the fall. Stated: the actual outcome falls within the stated range of the projection about 70 percent of the time. Record: the outcome.

## Reads (fixed now)
Projections: the September SEP of each year from 2013 to 2025, Table 1 median and Table 2 range for the current year and the next year, for four variables: change in real GDP, unemployment rate, PCE inflation (Table 2's "Total consumer prices") and the federal funds rate. September is used because the error ranges are measured on fall projections. A September page without the median row or Table 2 is NOT_COMPARABLE for that year. Other meetings are read and stored for later and are not scored.
Outcomes, from the St. Louis Fed's public FRED graph downloads (no key), taken at the latest revision on the day of the read, with the date stored: real GDP growth is the percent change in the fourth-quarter level (GDPC1) from the fourth quarter of the year before; the unemployment rate is the fourth-quarter average of UNRATE; PCE inflation is the percent change in the fourth-quarter average of PCEPI from the fourth quarter of the year before; the federal funds rate is the fourth-quarter average of FEDFUNDS, which is the Fed's own description in the SEP note for the shaded area. A target year is scored only when its fourth quarter is complete in the data.
Unit: a (variable, target year, horizon) triple. Horizon 0 is the current year in the September SEP of that year, horizon 1 is the next year in the September SEP of the year before. The projections for one target year at two horizons, and the four variables of one year, share the same economy, so the interval is made over years and not over triples.

## What is counted
Inside when |outcome minus median| is at most the stated range for that variable and horizon in that September's Table 2, outside otherwise. Per variable and horizon: the count outside, the share outside with a Wilson 95% interval treating each year as a unit. Pooled over the four variables, horizon 0 and horizon 1 separately: the share outside, with a 95% interval from a bootstrap over years (4,000 resamples, seed 7), since the four variables of one year are not independent.
Outcome against the claim, which expects about 0.30 outside: ABOVE if the lower end of the interval is above 0.30, BELOW if the upper end is below 0.30, CONSISTENT if 0.30 lies inside an interval no wider than 0.45, otherwise CANNOT_TELL. Descriptive, no outcome: the mean signed error (outcome minus median) for each variable and horizon, and the largest outside miss, in the units of the variable.

## Known-good
A planted set where the median and outcome are exactly at the stated range counts inside, one hundredth over counts outside. The Table 1 parser must return the September 2025 medians 1.6, 4.5, 3.0 and 3.6 for 2025 and ranges 1.4, 0.5, 1.0 and 0.5 from Table 2. The FRED reader must return 2024 values that match the St. Louis Fed's published annual series to within 0.05. A failure stops the pass.

## Hand check (fixed now)
Every outside miss at horizon 0 is opened by hand against the SEP page and the FRED series before it is listed.

## Not claimed
That the Committee's projections are good or bad. The ranges describe the average past error of fall projections by various forecasters, and a single year can sit outside without any contradiction. Nothing about interest rates as a market, about what any rate means for any price, or about any future outcome.

## Addendum 1 (29 Oct, after the first read and its hand check, before anything was shown)
The hand check of the horizon 0 misses found that one of the six, the federal funds rate in 2022, comes from the choice of basis and not from the projection. The SEP's median for the federal funds rate is the level at the end of the year (the midpoint of the target range). The plan scored it against the fourth-quarter average of the effective rate, following the SEP note that the shaded range is for the average level in the fourth quarter. In 2022 the rate rose through the quarter, so the September 2022 median of 4.4 against a fourth-quarter average of 3.65 reads outside the 0.5 range, while the year-end target midpoint was 4.375, inside it.
The main test is unchanged and stays as fixed, with the fourth-quarter average. A sensitivity row defined after the first read is added, labelled so, where the federal funds rate is scored against the year-end midpoint of the target range (the mean of FRED's DFEDTARU and DFEDTARL on the last day of the year). It carries its own outcome under the same rule and is not used in the main result. The 2023 real GDP miss is 0.02 beyond a range printed to one decimal and is listed as it is, with that remark.

## Note 1 (29 Oct, hand check of the first read, before anything was shown)
The six horizon 0 misses were opened. Each median and range was read from the September SEP page (Table 1 and Table 2) and checked against the stored parse: 2020 real GDP (median -3.7, range 1.1), 2020 unemployment (7.6, 0.3), 2021 PCE inflation (4.2, 0.8), 2021 unemployment (4.8, 0.5), 2022 federal funds rate (4.4, 0.5), 2023 real GDP (2.1, 1.3). The two unemployment outcomes were also recomputed from the BLS public API (2020: 6.77, 2021: 4.17) and match FRED. The real GDP, inflation and funds-rate outcomes come from FRED only. The 2022 funds rate row is explained in addendum 1 and the 2023 GDP row is 0.02 beyond its range. No row was a reading error and no rule changed. Two of the six misses (2020) fall in the year the pandemic began, which the plan keeps in.

## Note 2 (29 Oct, correcting Note 1, before anything was shown)
Note 1 said the two unemployment outcomes were recomputed from the BLS API. They were not: the BLS public API without a key returns only the last three years, so 2020 and 2021 are not in it. The 2020 and 2021 outcomes are from FRED only. The BLS API check is the gate's, which compares the 2024 fourth-quarter unemployment average from FRED with the BLS API and must agree within 0.05. The medians and ranges in Note 1 were read from the SEP pages as stated. No row or rule changed.

## Note 3 (29 Oct, from the internal audit)

2. The hand check opened only the this-year misses. The next-year misses are now listed with the note Not yet opened by hand, and none is claimed as opened.
3. The plan reads Septembers from 2013, and the code starts at 2015. The September 2013 and 2014 pages carry no median row (the median was first published in 2015), so those two years are not comparable and are not read.
4. Six of the 11 years take Table 2 from the minutes PDFs through a pattern match. The gate gains a known-good case: the September 2016 minutes must read the real GDP ranges 1.3 and 1.9 and the unemployment ranges 0.3 and 1.0, and the Table 1 median 1.8 and 2.0.
5. Stated and not fixed: with a width cap of 0.45 the GDP this-year row reads CONSISTENT from an interval of 0.05 to 0.48, which excludes little. The funds rate in the main row is scored against the fourth-quarter average as fixed, with the year-end sensitivity row beside it.
