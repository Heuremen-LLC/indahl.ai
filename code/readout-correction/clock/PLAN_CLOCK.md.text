DARKWAVE CLOCK -- plan (written 31 Oct 2026 ~18:30 EDT, before any job of this run). Wayfinder and Bones.
QUESTION. On 31 Oct the keystone run measured, per untouched qubit, the phase a mid-circuit readout of a 39-qubit pack adds (the kick), and
gave most of it back with rz(-kick) on every untouched qubit after every look. How long does a table measured on one day keep giving the
phase back? Each fire of this run is one point on that clock: the 31 Oct table's age at the fire, against a table measured minutes before.

SETUP (circuits from keystone.py, copied byte for byte). Setup S: all 117 untouched qubits on the equator, N = 8 looks of the pack E, settings
X and Y, 2,000 shots per circuit. Chips ibm_kingston and ibm_marrakesh. Arms in the same job, interleaved: D (the pack idles exactly the readout
length per look) | M (the pack is read out, no correction) | MC (+ the committed 21 Oct table) | M31 (+ the table measured in the keystone
run's job C on 31 Oct, held fixed for every later fire) | MT (+ today's table, from this fire's own calibration job C, the keystone's 8
circuits, minutes before). Job order: C, then A (keys in order), then B (keys reversed). Level-1 transpile, trivial layout, seed 31, no
dynamical decoupling, no twirling. The dry run checks equal scheduled durations on the untouched qubits for every arm (kingston 4,921 dt,
marrakesh 6,057 dt on 31 Oct), no CZ, and that the corrected arms add rz gates.

READINGS (per chip per fire; 99 % bootstrap over the 117 qubits; FINAL = the pooled reading if copies A and B agree, else CANNOT_TELL):
 R1 share given back, MC, M31, MT: 1 - median|residual of the arm| / median|kick of M|, vs 0.5 (the keystone's S7 ruler, unchanged).
 R2 THE CLOCK: share(M31) minus share(MT), paired on the same resampled qubits. ABOVE if the band sits above 0, BELOW if below 0,
    CONSISTENT only if the whole band sits inside [-0.05, +0.05], otherwise CANNOT_TELL. BELOW = the 31 Oct table gives back less than
    a table measured minutes before (it has aged). CONSISTENT = it still gives back as much, within 0.05.
 R3 share(MC) minus share(MT) and R4 share(MC) minus share(M31), same ruler (the 21 Oct table on the same clock, older start).
 The age of each table at the fire is printed beside every reading. Day 0 is the keystone's own reading (31 Oct, pooled S7 MF: kingston
 0.928, marrakesh 0.795). No time constant, fit or curve is claimed by this plan; a later dated addendum, written before the reading it
 applies to, can add one.
KNOWN-GOOD: the readout kick itself, per chip per fire: the pooled median signed kick of M (deg per readout, 99 % qubit bootstrap) must sit
 below 0 and within 0.5 to 2 times the committed 21 Oct median (kingston -2.864, marrakesh -2.393), the keystone's rule unchanged. On
 31 Oct real hardware it read kingston -2.424 and marrakesh -1.683, both inside. A chip-fire that fails it is "not decided" and none of its
 readings are read.
SYNTHETIC (synth_clock.py through the unchanged readers, both chips, real tables and coherences): first pass (synth_results_clock_FIRST.text)
 put a 0.25 deg per-qubit jitter inside the no-change world and read BELOW in 2 of 10 marrakesh draws. The no-change world now carries only
 the table's own calibration noise (0.15 deg), and the 0.25 deg jitter is its own probe. Second pass (synth_results_clock.text, 20 draws per
 world per chip): no change -> CONSISTENT or CANNOT_TELL 40 of 40; aged (0.7 x table + 1 deg) and moved (+1.5 deg per qubit) -> BELOW
 79 of 80, the one other draw a marrakesh known-good refusal (0.7 x a table already at about half the committed kick falls outside the
 known-good window); null -> known-good fails 40 of 40. RESOLUTION: a change of about 0.25 deg per qubit is mostly below this ruler
 (jitter probe: kingston CONSISTENT 5 / CANNOT_TELL 15, marrakesh BELOW 1 / CANNOT_TELL 19); changes of 1 deg read BELOW.
BUDGET. Estimate about 31 s per chip per fire (cap 60 s per chip-fire in fire_clock.py); instance remaining 2,107 s at the dry run.
 First fire: both chips tonight. Later fires during the week as seconds allow, each on the same code and plan.
WHAT IT CANNOT SAY. No mechanism is named. A BELOW says the 31 Oct table no longer matches today's kick on these qubits; it does not say why.
 One fire is one point; the copies A and B are the only repeat inside a fire.

ADDENDUM 1 (31 Oct 2026 ~19:00 EDT, after the first fire was read, before any later fire). The FINAL rule compares the two copies' LABELS.
From the next fire on, analyze_clock.py also prints the copy GAP of the clock reading with its width: (share M31 - share MT) in copy A minus
the same in copy B, 99 % bootstrap over qubits. The FINAL rule is unchanged. For the first fire this gap was computed after the data:
kingston +0.009 [-0.038, +0.050], marrakesh +0.045 [-0.053, +0.133]; neither resolves, and on marrakesh the copies differ in sign.

ADDENDUM 3 (1 Nov 2026, written after the second ibm_marrakesh fire was read, before any 2 November fire). post_clock.py prints second
rulers beside every chip-fire into readings_second.text: (a) the pooled clock reading over bootstrap seeds 1 to 20, (b) the per-qubit count
of qubits where the 31 Oct table leaves a larger |residual| than today's table, with an exact sign test, (c) the signed median residual of
each arm (positive = over-correction), (d) the shot-noise floor of the residual. Why: on 1 Nov ibm_marrakesh the FINAL clock reading was
BELOW with the band's upper end at -0.0002, BELOW in 3 of 20 seeds; the share folds the sign of the residual; the fresh table's residual sits
near the floor. The setup, the shots and the FINAL rule are unchanged, so every fire stays comparable. For the 1 November fires these
readings were defined after the data.
