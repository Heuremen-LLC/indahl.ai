# WATER: projected reservoir levels against measured levels. Plan, written before any projection was compared with a measurement.

## Claim read
Each month the Bureau of Reclamation publishes the 24-Month Study. It states a projected end-of-month water surface elevation for Lake Powell and Lake Mead for each of the next 24 or so months. Three inflow scenarios are published. Reclamation describes them in each report's catalog entry:
- Most Probable: "a median hydrologic condition which statistically would be exceeded 50% of the time".
- Probable Minimum: "a dry hydrologic condition which statistically would be exceeded only 90% of the time".
- Probable Maximum: "a wet hydrologic condition which statistically would be exceeded only 10% of the time".

Question: how far is the measured end-of-month elevation from what each study stated, by how many months ahead it was stated, and how often does the measured level fall inside the range between the Probable Minimum and Probable Maximum projections?

The stated probabilities describe inflow, not elevation. Reading the elevation range as a range that should hold about 80 percent of measured levels (90 minus 10), and the Most Probable elevation as a median, is our reading. It is named as ours wherever it is printed.

## Data
Stated side. Every 24-Month Study report that Reclamation lists in RISE catalog record 6331 (https://data.usbr.gov/rise/api/catalog-record/6331), January 2000 onward. At plan time the record lists 530 reports: 325 Most Probable, 119 Probable Minimum, 86 Probable Maximum. Each report is a PDF.
Values are read two ways and each row records which:
1. HDB model table. Many reports print a Model Run ID. Reclamation's public HDB service returns that run by ID (https://www.usbr.gov/pn-bin/hdb/hdb.pl, table M, monthly, Lake Powell sdi 1928 on uchdb2, Lake Mead sdi 1930 on lchdb). A run is used from HDB only if its values match the report's own printed table on every month both carry, to 0.01 ft. Otherwise the run is not used from HDB.
2. Report text. The "Reservoir Elevation End of Month" column of the report's Lake Powell and Hoover Dam - Lake Mead operations tables, read from the PDF text. Older reports are scanned pages with machine-read text. A text value is kept only if it parses to a number with two decimals inside the reservoir's range (Lake Mead 895.00 to 1229.00 ft, Lake Powell 3370.00 to 3711.00 ft) and differs from the value one month before and one month after in the same report by less than 30 ft (Mead) or 40 ft (Powell). A value that fails is CANNOT_TELL (unreadable) and is counted apart.

Measured side. Reclamation HDB monthly observed end-of-period water surface elevation (table R, monthly, Lake Mead sdi 1930 on lchdb, Lake Powell sdi 1928 on uchdb2). This is the same quantity, from the same database, that the model table projects.
Cross-checks, reported but not used for scoring: the HDB daily end-of-period reading on the last day of each month, Reclamation's Lower Colorado end-of-month elevation table for Lake Mead (https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/hourly/mead-elv.html), and the USGS daily mean at 09379900 Lake Powell at Glen Canyon Dam (NGVD 1929, from April 2019). USGS publishes no current Lake Mead elevation: its site 09421000 carries only a Reclamation daily storage series that ends on 29 Sep 2017.

All sources are public and need no key. The user agent names this site.

## Which projection counts for which month (fixed now)
Issue month: the month named in the report title ("October 2026 Most Probable 24-Month Study" is issue month 2026-10). Reports are published about mid-month.
Target month: the month whose end-of-month elevation is stated.
Lead: target month minus issue month, in months. Lead 0 is the end of the issue month, about two weeks after publication. Lead 1 is the first month that begins after the report was issued. Rows with lead below 0 are the report's past months and are not scored. Summary cells cover leads 0 to 23. Rows beyond lead 23 are kept in the record and not put in cells.
One row is one issue month, one scenario, one reservoir, one target month.
Two official Most Probable runs in one issue month (from August 2026, runs that assume a 6.0 maf and a 7.0 maf Lake Powell release): both are scored row by row. The summary cells use the 7.0 maf run, which the 2027-2028 Operating Guidelines (Table 1, April step 1) name as the baseline. The 6.0 maf run is shown apart.
Any other duplicate (same issue month and scenario, no variant named): the report with the later RISE publication date is used, the other is listed apart.
A Probable Minimum or Probable Maximum report pairs only with the reports of its own issue month.

## Rules (fixed now)
Band. For an issue month that has both a Probable Minimum and a Probable Maximum report, the band at a target month is the interval between the two projected elevations (low = the smaller, high = the larger). If the Probable Minimum value is above the Probable Maximum value, the row is still scored on that interval and counted as inverted. A Most Probable value outside its own band is counted.

Row outcome (band test). Measured above high + 0.005 ft: ABOVE. Measured below low - 0.005 ft: BELOW. Otherwise: CONSISTENT. No band issued that month, or one edge missing or unreadable for that target month: CANNOT_TELL. No measured value for the target month yet: NOTHING_RETURNED.

Most Probable error. Error = measured minus Most Probable, in feet. Positive means the lake ended higher than stated. Sign at 0.01 ft resolution: above, below or equal.

Cells, one per reservoir and lead (0 to 23).
- Band cell. n = rows with a band and a measurement. k = CONSISTENT rows. Also shown: ABOVE and BELOW counts.
- Most Probable cell. n = rows with a Most Probable value and a measurement, ties left out. k = rows where the measurement was above. Also shown: median signed error, median absolute error, and the 10th and 90th percentile of the error (the middle 80 percent of errors).
- Interval. Months in one water year share one year's weather, so rows are not independent. Each cell's 95% Wilson interval is computed on water-year clusters: n_eff = the number of distinct water years (October to September) among the target months in the cell, k_eff = round(n_eff x k / n). The interval is printed with that label.
- Band cell outcome against the 80 percent reading: lower bound above 0.80 = ABOVE (the band held more measured levels than 80 percent), upper bound below 0.80 = BELOW (it held fewer), whole interval inside 0.70 to 0.90 = CONSISTENT, otherwise CANNOT_TELL. n_eff under 10 = CANNOT_TELL.
- Most Probable cell outcome against the median reading: lower bound above 0.50 = ABOVE (the lake ended above the Most Probable projection more than half the time), upper bound below 0.50 = BELOW, whole interval inside 0.40 to 0.60 = CONSISTENT, otherwise CANNOT_TELL. n_eff under 10 = CANNOT_TELL.

Headline: the band cells and the Most Probable cells at lead 6 for each reservoir. Every other cell is printed in the table with its counts, agreements as well as misses.

Floor. 2 reservoirs x 24 leads x 2 tests = 96 cells. If every stated figure held and cells were independent, two-sided 95% intervals would put about 5 cells at ABOVE or BELOW by chance (expected 4.8, a count above 9.2 exceeds chance). Cells share target months across leads, so they are not independent, and the page says so.

No cause is named. A gap between a projection and the measured level is a gap. The page does not say why.

## Known-good, run before any comparison
1. HDB model read: for 3 reports that print a Model Run ID, the HDB values match the report's printed Lake Mead and Lake Powell end-of-month elevations on every month both carry, to 0.01 ft.
2. Text read: for 3 scanned reports, the parsed Lake Mead and Lake Powell values for 3 target months each match the values read directly from the page text.
3. Measured read: for 3 months, the HDB monthly observed value equals the HDB daily end-of-period reading on the last day of that month to 0.01 ft (Mead and Powell), equals the Lower Colorado end-of-month table (Mead), and is within 1 ft of the USGS 09379900 daily mean (Powell, a different gauge and statistic).
If any check fails, every reading in that run is CANNOT_TELL (heuremen_kit.apply_gate) and the original outcome is kept beside it.

## Second test: USGS provisional values against approved values (snapshot, starts empty)
USGS serves the current value only. Provisional daily values (qualifier P) are stored by us on each read for a fixed list of Colorado River system gauges: 09379900 (Lake Powell elevation, 62614), 09380000 (Colorado River at Lees Ferry, discharge), 09402500 (Colorado River near Grand Canyon), 09404200 (Colorado River above Diamond Creek), 09415000 (Virgin River at Littlefield), 09180500 (Colorado River near Cisco), 09315000 (Green River at Green River, UT), 09379500 (San Juan River near Bluff). When a stored day later reads with qualifier A (approved), the revision is approved minus the first provisional value stored. Rows appear only for days we stored as provisional before they were approved. No history before our first read is claimed. Outcome per gauge with at least 30 revised days: share of days revised by more than 1 percent (discharge) or 0.1 ft (elevation), Wilson 95% interval, CANNOT_TELL below 30 days. No threshold claim is tested yet: USGS states no expected revision size, so this test reports sizes only until a stated figure is found and added by dated addendum.

## Live panel (readings, not scored)
Current Lake Mead and Lake Powell elevation (Reclamation HDB hourly and daily), change over 1, 7 and 30 days, and the elevations named in the governing documents, each quoted with its document and section. Lake Powell also from USGS 09379900 beside it. Other reservoirs: current storage against the capacity the operating agency states (Reclamation for Lake Mohave, Lake Havasu, Flaming Gorge, Navajo and Blue Mesa, and California CDEC for Shasta and Oroville). A percent of stated capacity is a reading, not a test.

## Reading done before this plan was written
To answer a question about a reported rise of Lake Mead, we read the last 60 days of Lake Mead and Lake Powell measured elevations (HDB hourly and daily, RISE daily, USGS 09379900) and the Navajo Reservoir daily record. A news search returned one projected value (a September 2026 projection for 31 December 2026), a month not yet measured. We opened 3 reports to learn their table layout (January 2005 Most Probable, January 2015 Probable Minimum, September 2026 Most Probable 7 maf) and confirmed that HDB run 1427 returns the January 2005 Lake Mead values printed in that report. No projection had been set against a measured end-of-month value when this plan was written.

## Addendum 1 (10 Nov 2026, before the first comparison)
Written after reading only the stated side: the report files, their printed values and the HDB model runs. No projection had been set against a measured value.
1. Reading pages. Many reports store their table pages turned sideways, and the scanned ones sit slightly crooked. The reader turns each page upright and takes the tilt out before it reads rows. The operations table is the first page under the reservoir's heading that holds at least 12 dated rows with an elevation inside the reservoir's range. Headings are matched loosely, because machine-read scans misspell them ("Hoover Darn", "Lake Head").
2. Printed values against HDB runs. On the 416 reports that print a Model Run ID, the printed values and the HDB values agreed to within 0.015 ft on 19,611 of 19,841 months (the print rounds to 0.01 ft and HDB keeps unrounded values, so a 0.01 ft difference is rounding). The other months fall into two kinds. In most runs a few months differ by one or two digits, such as 3588.51 read where HDB holds 3598.51. These are misreads in our machine reading of the scan, not in the print. In 9 runs nearly every month differs by amounts that are not digit misreads (for example 3555.04 printed and 3554.91 in HDB), so the stored run is not the one that was printed, or it was changed after printing. The two kinds separate cleanly: the first kind agrees on 68 percent of months or more, the second on 48 percent or less.
Rule, replacing "on every month both carry" in the Data section: a run is used from HDB when it agrees with the print within 0.015 ft on at least 60 percent of the months both carry. Its HDB values are then used for every month of the printed table. A run below 60 percent is not used from HDB and the printed values stand. The 60 percent line was set from the gap in this distribution, before any comparison.
3. Values read only from the print (reports without an ID or with a run below 60 percent) are not checked digit by digit. On months where both readings exist, our reading of the print differed from a matching HDB run on about 0.3 percent of months. The page states this rate for the print-only values.
4. Known-good checks 1, 2 and 3 were run with the final reader and all passed: 3 HDB runs equal to their print on every month (6 tables), 21 values read directly from the page text of 3 scanned reports equal to the parsed values, and 3 months of measured values equal across the HDB monthly table, the HDB daily reading, the Lower Colorado table and, for Lake Powell, within 1 ft of USGS.

## Addendum 2 (10 Nov 2026, after the first comparison, after an outside referee pass)
Written after the first comparison was read and after a refuting referee read the desk. No rule, threshold, lead, band or outcome definition changed. One stated-side reading fix changes one count, as item 2 says.
1. The 80 percent figure is Reclamation's statement, not our reading. The Claim read section above says the stated probabilities describe inflow, not elevation, and calls the 80 percent reading ours. That was wrong. Since January 2010 the Probable Minimum and Probable Maximum reports print this on their first page: "There is approximately an 80% probability that a future elevation will fall inside the range of the minimum and maximum inflow scenarios. Additionally, there are possible inflow scenarios that would result in reservoir elevations falling outside the ranges indicated in these reports." We found it in 182 of the 205 Probable Minimum and Probable Maximum reports. Both reports of 79 of the 86 band months carry it. The 7 band months without it print tables only: 2006-08, 2008-04, 2008-08, 2009-04, 2009-08, 2016-10 and 2023-10. The RISE catalog entries the plan quoted do not carry the sentence. Only the report files do. The band test is a test of that sentence. The Most Probable median test stays our reading, since Reclamation describes the Most Probable inflow as a median and states nothing about elevation for it.
2. Reader fix, stated side only. Two Lake Powell tables were not found because the scanned heading reads "Powell" or "Lake Powe ll" (August 2020 Probable Minimum, HDB run 3122, and August 2001 Most Probable). The heading match now allows those forms, with the same content test as before (at least 12 dated rows in Lake Powell's range). The August 2020 table matches HDB run 3122 (February 2021 3582.55). This adds one band row to Lake Powell at lead 6, which moves that cell from 49 of 82 to 49 of 83, and adds one row to most other Lake Powell band cells.
3. The page no longer compares the count of cells reading above or below with a chance figure. The plan's floor assumed independent cells, and the plan itself says they are not. The page lists the cells that read above or below and says they share reports and target months.
4. Checks added after the data, printed beside the plan's outcome and never used for it: the same counts read with water years weighted by their row count, with neighbouring water years paired into blocks, and with rows counted as independent. The correlation of the per-water-year share between neighbouring water years is printed by lead. The band cells are also read on the 79 months that print the sentence. The plan's water-year ruler treats water years as independent, and at leads past about six months neighbouring water years are correlated, so it is not a conservative ruler at those leads. On these checks Lake Mead's six-month band reads below on every ruler tried. Lake Powell's sits at the edge of the 80 percent line: its upper bound is 78.1 percent on the plan ruler and 78.7 to 81.8 percent on the other cluster rulers, which read cannot tell under at least one way of rounding the count.
5. With about 20 water years in a cell, a 95% interval is about 0.33 wide, so no band cell can fit inside 70 to 90 percent (that needs about 61 water years) and no Most Probable cell inside 40 to 60 percent (about 96). The desk can read a miss or cannot tell. It cannot read CONSISTENT at this sample size, and the page says so.
6. The misread rate of our print reading is stated by era: 8 of 790 compared months (1.0 percent) for 2000 to 2005 reports, and 0.12 to 0.39 percent for later eras. Addendum 1 item 3 gave 0.3 percent overall. Print-only values come mostly from 2000 to 2011 reports. A misread of a whole digit, such as 10 ft, can pass the neighbour rule.
7. The page now shows the 6.0 maf rows apart, the inverted and zero-width band counts, the count of Most Probable values outside their own band, the measured-side cross-checks, and the Most Probable cells at lead 6 in the headline, as the plan says.
8. Disclosure added to "Reading done before this plan was written": before the plan was sealed we also read the full Lake Mead daily history from 1935 (its record low and its largest one-day jumps) and the narrative pages of the October 2026 Most Probable study. Neither set a projection against a measured month.
