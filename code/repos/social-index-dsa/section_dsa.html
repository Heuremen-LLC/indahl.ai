Two filings, one platform

Each very large online platform in the EU states its content moderation counts twice. Its transparency report gives totals for each half-year in a template the European Commission fixed. Its statements of reasons go one by one into the Commission’s DSA Transparency Database. We read the report’s figures against the count of the same platform’s statements for the same half-year, and print where the two agree as well as where they differ. YouTube, Google Maps and Google Play report their ads moderation in separate files, so their main rows leave out statements flagged as ads, and their ads are read on rows of their own.

Two of the four checks committed with the plan failed. Under the plan as committed every row would read cannot tell. We located both failures before reading the comparisons and replaced the checks in dated addenda. The readings below use those addenda, and every rule set after the data is labelled on its row.

Database aggregates read 9 November 2026 (HST), with statements submitted up to 7 November 2026. Reports are read in the latest version each platform publishes.

How we read it

The stated figure is the one the platform printed in its report, quoted from the file with its name and date in the data. The delivered figure is the number of statements the same platform filed, counted from the Commission’s monthly aggregates of the database. We count statements by the date each decision applies from, including statements filed late up to the newest aggregate, and again by the date each statement was submitted. The band runs between the two counts. It also carries the partially automated category, statements that name only Iceland, Liechtenstein or Norway, and the measures the law does not require in the database: orders from authorities and spam. Above means the database holds more than the report states, beyond the whole band and by more than a tenth. Below means it holds less, by the same test. Either must clear its edge by at least 100 statements. Consistent means the whole band lies within a tenth of the stated figure. Cannot tell means none of these. Some differences neither source sizes, so no reading names a cause. A below reading can come from statements the law does not require where the recipient’s contact details are unknown or for deceptive high-volume commercial content that a report does not label as spam (DSA Article 17(2)), from a filing that counts one decision per item, per account or per restriction type, from a report that counts content filtered at upload, and from statements filed after the newest aggregate. An above reading can come from how each filing decides which decisions concern the EU, and from one decision filed as more than one statement, since the aggregates cannot detect re-filings.

The plan and its rules were committed before the first count of the database by platform. Changes made after the first counts are dated addenda to the plan. Rows that use a rule defined after the data say so. These cover the separate ads files, the notice rows, notes in which a platform says a field counts something the database does not hold, bounds taken from a report’s own restriction columns, splits the two filings label differently, and a minimum of 100 statements for an above or below reading. On six days in February and May 2026 the Commission’s simple aggregate counts statements twice. We read the complete aggregate, which matches the Commission’s published daily totals within 0.05% on every day but three at the turn of September and October 2026. The bands carry those three days, and two days in November 2026 with no published file. That allowance is wide on purpose, and it is the reason many trusted flagger and own-initiative illegal-content rows read cannot tell.
