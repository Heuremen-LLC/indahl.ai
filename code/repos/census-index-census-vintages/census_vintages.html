What a Vintage restates

The Census Bureau publishes a Vintage of population estimates each December. A Vintage gives the July 1 estimate of every state for every year it covers. Each Vintage restates earlier years, so the same state and year can be read in several Vintages. Below, the figure from the earlier Vintage is called the earlier figure and the figure for the same state and year in a later Vintage is called the later value. Vintages 2019 and 2020 are built on the 2010 Census. Vintages 2021 and later are built on the 2020 Census. A Vintage is allowed to restate its past and this page makes no claim about which figure is the better one. The page reads where a later value sits against an earlier figure.

The frame is the 50 states and the District of Columbia. The Census Bureau publishes no width with any population estimate, so a comparison has a width only where something the Census Bureau published can supply one.

Where the width comes from

After the 2020 Census the Census Bureau ran the Post-Enumeration Survey and published a standard error for the net coverage error of every state. That figure says how precisely the survey measured the net coverage of the 2020 count for the household population. It is a sampling error of the survey. It is not a width on any population estimate. We use it as a yardstick and compute the half-width as 1.645 times the standard error, in percent, times the earlier figure. The multiplier is the two sided 90 percent multiplier that the Census Bureau uses for its survey comparisons. The yardstick is ours.

The yardstick is centered on zero. It ignores each state’s published net coverage error, both its sign and its size, so a state the survey says was undercounted or overcounted gets the same centered interval as any other. The survey covers the household population and the figures here are resident totals, so we apply the width as a relative width, and that step is ours too. A second yardstick that is centered on each state’s published net coverage error is shown further down, beside this one.

The yardstick fits a comparison that crosses the census, such as an estimate built on the 2010 Census against one built on the 2020 Census, or an estimate against the census count. It does not fit a comparison between two Vintages on the same base. They share the level of that base. We do not use the spread between Vintages as a width either. That spread is the thing being read, and it would make every row read inside by construction. So every comparison between two Vintages on the same base is read CANNOT_TELL with the reason no width. We wrote that rule down before we computed anything.

The reading rule

For each state the interval is the earlier figure plus or minus its half-width. A later value inside the interval, edges included, reads CONSISTENT. A later value over the top reads ABOVE and one under the bottom reads BELOW. A state with no width reads CANNOT_TELL, and each reason is counted apart. The four readings are counted for each comparison and printed as four counts. No share is read against 90 percent. The interval is a yardstick and not a coverage figure for the later value. CONSISTENT does not say an estimate is right. It says the later value is within what the survey of the 2020 count can separate.

A second yardstick, added after the referee

The outside referee asked what happens if the interval is centered on each state’s published net coverage error and not on zero. We added that second reading after the referee. It is not a change to the sealed rule above, and no outcome in the tables above uses it. For each state the gap is the later value minus the earlier figure, as a percent of the earlier figure. The second reading takes the published net coverage error away from that gap and reads the rest against 1.645 times the standard error. The sealed reading stays the lead. The two are shown side by side. Two reasonable yardsticks disagree, and the disagreement is part of what the data says.

What this page holds and what it leaves out

It holds the state July 1 estimates of Vintages 2019 to 2025, the April 1 2020 estimate of Vintage 2020, the estimates base of Vintages 2022 to 2025, the 2020 Census count and the survey standard errors from the CENSUS COVERAGE desk. It leaves out counties, age, sex and race detail, Puerto Rico, July estimates against the April count (the dates differ), and any reason a Vintage restated. Vintage 2026 arrives in December and this page does not refresh.

What came before the seal. We copied the raw files and read the first rows of two API pulls before the plan was written. The scout had also printed California, Texas and Arkansas. No restatement, outcome or count existed then. The seal covers the text of the plan and its rule. The code was committed after the first reading. After the outside referee we added the second yardstick, the signed medians, the Vintage 2021 totals check and this wording, and none of them changed a sealed reading.
