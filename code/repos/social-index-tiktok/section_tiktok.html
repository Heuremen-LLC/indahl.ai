TikTok: the quarterly report against its EU filings

Every quarter TikTok publishes how many videos it removed in each market and how many of those removals were automated. TikTok also files its EU moderation decisions, one statement of reasons each, to the EU's DSA Transparency Database. We read the two filings against each other for every quarter since the database opened in September 2023, and we print where they agree as well as where they do not.

How we read it

The report side is TikTok's own figure, summed over the EU markets it lists. Its range runs from that sum to the sum plus what the unlisted EU markets, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein could hold under TikTok's own listing rule. We do not know whether TikTok files statements for users in Norway, Iceland or Liechtenstein, so the range allows both. Rates in the report are printed to three decimals, and the range carries that rounding.

The database side counts TikTok statements with video content and a removal decision, not ads, dated by the day the decision was applied. Its range runs across that day shifted one day either way, and across a narrow and a wide reading of video. The narrow reading keeps removals for breaking TikTok's rules only. The wide reading adds images, synthetic media and other content, because TikTok counts photo posts and stories as videos.

No range on this page is a statistical interval. Both sides are full counts, and each range shows how far a figure moves across the matching choices we fixed before reading the data. The gap range is the database range minus the report range, as a share of the report figure for counts and in percentage points for shares. Above means the database figure is higher than the report allows under every choice. Below means it is lower under every choice. Consistent means the gap range includes zero and stays within 5% for counts or 1 percentage point for shares. Cannot tell means the gap range includes zero and reaches past that size, or a check on the files failed. Nothing returned means one side holds no figure for the row. We measure gaps and name no cause.

Removed within 24 hours is compared with the days between the posting date and the decision date. The database keeps both as whole days, so its side is a range.

Our plan first let the submission date set one end of the database range. Before we set any figure against another, the files showed that TikTok filed no statement on some days and, from July 2025 to January 2026, filed up to 94% of a month's removal statements one to three months after the decision. TikTok's report counts cases closed in the quarter, so we date by the decision and print the count by submission date beside it. The data file keeps the outcome under the first rule for every row.

Our committed check on the database files failed on 11 February 2026 and 30 September to 2 October 2026, so Oct to Dec 2025, Jan to Mar 2026 and Apr to Jun 2026 read Cannot tell. The column marked defined after the data replaces that check with TikTok's own daily totals, published on the database's download page. They match the files exactly on 1,091 of 1,138 days. Where the files hold more statements than TikTok's total, the excess can come off the low end of the database range (the largest is 8 May 2026, by 858,890). Where they hold fewer, the shortfall is added to the high end (the largest is 30 September 2026, by 184,124).

TikTok's figures for each quarter are the same in that quarter's own release and in the latest release, for every EU market we read.
