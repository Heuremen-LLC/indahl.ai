# CENSUS COVERAGE: the 2020 Census count of each state against the Census Bureau's own coverage survey. Plan, written before any reading was computed by this desk.

This product uses the Census Bureau Data API but is not endorsed or certified by the Census Bureau.

## Claim read
The stated figure is the 2020 Census count of each state, the count Census published. The second measurement is the Census Bureau's Post-Enumeration Survey (PES), an independent sample survey matched case by case to the census. Census publishes from it a net coverage error for each state with a standard error. A negative net coverage error is an undercount (census count below the PES estimate) and a positive one is an overcount. Census defines it as census count minus the dual-system estimate, as a percent (PES20-G-02RV, June 2022, section 2).
The PES is the Census Bureau's own measure of its own count. Nothing on this desk says the census is wrong. A reading says only where the stated count sits against the interval of the second measurement.

## Population covered (cited, not assumed)
The PES covers the household population living in the 50 states and the District of Columbia on 1 April 2020. It excludes people in group quarters (jails, nursing homes, college dormitories, shelters, military barracks and ships) and people in Remote Alaska areas. Source: Source and Accuracy of the 2020 PES Person Estimates, "Target Population" (https://www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/decennial/coverage-measurement/pes/2020-source-and-accuracy-pes-estimates.pdf), and the footnote of Appendix Table 3 of PES20-G-02RV ("The Post-Enumeration Survey did not evaluate the coverage of the population living in Remote Alaska"). The census count in the PES tables is the household count, rounded to the thousand, and is smaller than the resident total in the redistricting file. Both are shown. The reading applies to the household count.
Puerto Rico is in a separate group (A_TOTPR), is not a state and is not in the frame. The frame is 50 states and the District of Columbia, 51 areas, called areas here.

## Data
Census Data API, dataset 2020/dec/pes. Group G_STATES (Census count, net coverage error in percent, standard error in percent, 51 areas plus a Puerto Rico row with no values). Group H_STATES (components of coverage by state, rows COUNT and SE: correct enumerations, erroneous enumerations by duplication and for other reasons, whole-person imputations, omissions). Groups A_TOTUS and B_TOTUS (United States total). Resident totals for the context column come from the 2020 redistricting file (dec/pl) as pulled by the scout. Census PES release tables: PES20-G-02RV (PDF, Appendix Tables 3 and 4). Demographic Analysis: the Census Bureau release of 10 March 2022 (CB22-CN.02), Table 1.
Raw files are copied to data/raw as served. The API key is read at call time and is never written to any file.

## Rules (fixed now)
Interval. Census states that PES comparisons are "statistically significant at the 90 percent confidence level (alpha = 0.10) using a two-sided test", and that the net coverage error figure "displays the 90 percent confidence interval" (source and accuracy statement, PES20-G-02RV table footnote). The interval for an area is NCE plus or minus 1.645 times its standard error. This is the normal multiplier for a two-sided 90 percent interval and is our computation. Census prints an asterisk on an area that is significantly different from zero, and those asterisks are used only as a gate, below.
Outcome per area, from the published NCE and SE as served, no tolerance:
- BELOW: the interval is wholly below zero (the upper end is under zero). The census count sits below the PES estimate, an undercount.
- ABOVE: the interval is wholly above zero (the lower end is over zero). The census count sits above the PES estimate, an overcount.
- CONSISTENT: the interval holds zero, edges included.
- CANNOT_TELL: no NCE, no SE, or an SE of zero or below. Each reason counted apart.
The four outcomes are counted over the areas and printed as four counts. CANNOT_TELL is never folded into a share or into CONSISTENT. No share of areas is scored.
Expected flags with nothing wrong: at 90 percent two sided, 51 areas give 5.1 flagged areas if every true value were zero, about half on each side. Computed and printed as a floor (heuremen_kit.floor, rate 0.10). It treats areas as independent, which they are not exactly, so it is a rough floor. It is described and is not a verdict. Net coverage error also holds nonsampling error that the standard error does not describe (Census says so in the same statement). Nothing is said about why an area reads as it does.
Components. For omissions (H_STATES_010), erroneous enumerations by duplication (H_STATES_006) and for other reasons (H_STATES_007), each state value as a percent with its SE and the same 1.645 interval is set beside the United States value of the same component from B_TOTUS (rows R13, R8, R9, percent column) with that national SE shown. Outcome of a state against the national point value: BELOW if the state interval is wholly under it, ABOVE if wholly over it, CONSISTENT if it holds it, CANNOT_TELL where the state SE is missing or the sentinel -9999.9. The national SE is shown and does not enter the rule. Total erroneous enumerations are not served by H_STATES, and the two parts have no published covariance, so no total is formed. Whole-person imputations and correct enumerations are shown and not read. This reading is of components against the national rate and is separate from the net coverage reading above. It is described with counts and is not merged with it.
United States PES cell. The United States total (A_TOTUS) is read with the same rule as its own cell.
Demographic Analysis cell (separate, a second method, never merged with the PES cells). Demographic Analysis (DA) estimates the total resident population from births, deaths, migration and Medicare records, not from a survey. Census Table 1 gives the DA net coverage error as percent for the low, middle and high series for the 2020 Census. The DA cell reads the three series against zero: BELOW if all three are under zero, ABOVE if all three are over zero, CONSISTENT if the three hold zero between them. The series are scenarios Census gives and not a statistical interval, and Census states no standard error for DA, so the cell is read as a range and called that. The middle series and the sign of each series are printed. The DA population is the total resident population and the PES population is the household population excluding Remote Alaska, so the two cells are not the same people and are never pooled. We compare, in words only, whether the PES national estimate and its interval sit inside the DA range. No number combines them.

## Both alternatives
A. An area's interval holds zero, so the second measurement does not separate the census count from the PES estimate. CONSISTENT.
B. An area's interval sits wholly on one side. BELOW or ABOVE, stated as where the census count sits against the survey estimate with the width shown. Either result prints the same way.

## Known-good gates (a failed gate turns every outcome into CANNOT_TELL)
1. Reader. Appendix Table 3 of PES20-G-02RV is parsed from the Census PDF for all 51 areas. Census count, NCE and SE equal the API values for every area. Five areas (Arkansas, Texas, California, Hawaii, Florida) are also read by hand from the PDF page and written in CHECKS.md.
2. Census flags. The areas Census marks with an asterisk in that table (and names in the report text: undercounts for Arkansas, Florida, Illinois, Mississippi, Tennessee and Texas, overcounts for Delaware, Hawaii, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New York, Ohio, Rhode Island and Utah) equal exactly the BELOW and ABOVE sets of this rule, each on the right side.
3. Join. The NCE in H_STATES (row COUNT, H_STATES_011) equals the NCE in G_STATES for every area, and the United States NCE in A_TOTUS equals B_TOTUS (R14 percent column). Components read by hand for Arkansas (omissions 10.1 with SE 2.0) match Appendix Table 4.
4. Rule. Synthetic rows with a known answer read BELOW, ABOVE, CONSISTENT and each CANNOT_TELL reason correctly, and the heuremen_kit self-test passes.

## What was seen before this plan
The scout (census_scout/SCOUT.md, 11 Nov 2026) printed California, Texas and Arkansas with NCE and SE. While drafting this plan, and before it was sealed, the full list of 51 NCE and SE values was printed once together with a quick marker for 1.645 times SE, and it matched the list of areas the Census report names in its text (six undercounts, eight overcounts). No summary file, count of outcomes or page existed at that time. The rule above is the Census rule and was not chosen after seeing the list. This is disclosed on the page.

## Set aside
Census regions (E_REGIONS), race, age, tenure and housing-unit coverage, Puerto Rico, the 2010 comparison, apportionment or funding effects, any cause for a coverage error, and any comparison with population estimates.
