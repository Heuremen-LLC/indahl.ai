## Their headline
Six judges recover the reviewers' preference (the accepted revision scored above the first) from 80.8% (claude-sonnet-5) down to 63.5%
(deepseek-v4-flash), "each at p < 10^-5 by sign test", against a 50% chance line. Table 1 intervals are a clustered bootstrap over
declarations and replicates. The judges are noisy: a rerun "flips between a fifth and nearly half of its verdicts".

## KNOWN-GOOD (ours, ours/known_good.py, before the readings)
From the six trace files, with our own code: pairs keyed by (pr_number, declaration_name, replicate), aligned when the final scores above
the initial, ties counted as misses (their metrics.py, read). Alignment per judge against Table 1, with trajectories missing from the
release counted by judge, variant and replicate.

## Readings (defined after reading the paper and code; four outcomes; 95% intervals)
C1 Sign test with the declaration as the unit. Replicates share a declaration, so for each judge each declaration gets one verdict: aligned
   when the final scores above the initial in at least two of its three replicates. Exact two-sided sign test on declarations (aligned
   against not aligned, then aligned against inverted with ties left out), beside the pooled test over all pairs. Reports whether
   p < 10^-5 holds for each judge at the declaration level.
C2 Ties three ways. Per judge: alignment with ties as misses (theirs), ties as half, and aligned among decided pairs (ties left out), each
   with a declaration-clustered bootstrap (10,000, seed 42). Whether the order of the six judges changes. The chance line for "aligned
   over all pairs" with ties as misses is below 50% by half the tie share, stated beside it.
C3 Replicate flips. Per judge: the share of declarations whose verdict (aligned or not) differs between two replicates, averaged over the
   three replicate pairs, and the share whose three replicates do not all agree. Which definition gives the printed range.
C4 Missing trajectories. Per judge, the pairs lost to omitted errored runs, and alignment with each lost pair counted as a miss over all
   654 pairs, beside the printed value.
C5 Cost bound. Per judge priced in the repo's make_frontier.py table, cost per pair with every prompt token at the fresh-input rate (an
   upper bound, since the traces do not record the cached share), beside the printed USD per pair.

## Addendum 1 (30 Oct 2026, after C1-C5; C5b is defined after the data)
C5 found gpt-5.4-mini's printed 0.136 above the upper bound the repo's own rates allow (0.046). The scout noted that 0.75 in and 4.50 out
per million tokens reproduce it. C5b recomputes gpt-5.4-mini's cost per pair at those rates, every prompt token fresh, beside 0.136.

## Addendum 2 (30 Oct 2026, after the referee pass; C1b, C2b and C6 are defined after the data)
Corrections from the referee: C2's "within about a point" is wrong (1.7 points with ties as misses); C3's range is the author's own README
figure ("between 21% and 45% of pairs"), so C3 is not raised as new; C5's 0.046 bounds nothing in the paper, since the repo table may be the
stale one, and is restated as a question about which rates are intended. New readings, each defined after the data:
C1b single replicates: the exact sign test per judge on each replicate alone, ties as misses and ties left out.
C2b paired gaps: the difference between judges adjacent in Table 1, per tie rule, with a paired declaration-clustered bootstrap (10,000,
seed 42).
C6 the chance line: make_frontier.py line 158 says ties are "vanishingly rare"; the observed tie shares from C2 sit beside it.
