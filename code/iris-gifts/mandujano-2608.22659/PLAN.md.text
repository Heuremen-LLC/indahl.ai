## Readings (four outcomes; every one below is defined after reading his paper and is labelled so in any write-up)
K2 provenance: his 1,497 rows against TIGER-Lab's released per-item outputs for the same six models (correct = predicted letter equals the
   answer key, keys matched by question_id). Count rows that agree, disagree, or have no upstream match, per model.
G1 pairing under correction: Holm over the 15 pairs with unpaired p against paired p (how many survive each). His text says pairing barely
   changes the uncorrected count (5 either way); this reads the corrected count.
G2 a second ruler: exact McNemar (two-sided binomial on discordant items) beside the paired z for all 15 pairs, Holm on both.
G3 the near miss: Holm, which his paper recommends for an exact guarantee, applied to the Eq. 3 contrasts (Table 4 raw p), against the
   Bonferroni flag the paper prints. Does the third row (raw p .0034) clear Holm's step (.05/13)?
G4 distance to the line: for Mistral-7B-v0.1 against Yi-6B, the fewest single-item score changes (one model, one item, 0 to 1 or 1 to 0)
   that move the call across its line under each rule (paired z with Bonferroni, paired z with Holm, Eq. 3 identity with Bonferroni and
   with Holm, exact McNemar with Bonferroni and with Holm), in both directions.
G5 live (GPU, separate addendum before any run): the same six models are open weights. A rerun of Mistral-7B-v0.1 and Yi-6B on his 1,497
   questions through TIGER-Lab's own 5-shot chain-of-thought setup on our two machines, two repeats each, gives a repeat floor and a machine
   floor in item flips to set against G4. Weights, conversion, prompt and extraction get their own addendum and known-good (our replies
   against TIGER-Lab's released replies on a sample) before any count.
