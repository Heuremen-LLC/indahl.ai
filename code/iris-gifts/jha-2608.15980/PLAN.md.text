## KNOWN-GOOD (ours, ours/known_good.py, before the readings)
From MultiPref-Train.jsonl with our own code: the number of comparisons (printed 10,461), the comparisons where both crowd labels agree and both
expert labels agree on overall_pref collapsed to A, Tie or B (printed 2,885), and on those the share where the pool labels differ (printed 23.6%)
and where they reverse A against B (printed 9.2%).

## Readings (defined after reading the paper; four outcomes; 95% intervals)
J1 Table 3 under independent per-model shifts. The printed column follows from treating adjacent swaps as independent events, each with
   probability P(N(0, 2.6) > gap). The paper's own setup is a per-model shift (SD 1.9 pp, so 2.6 pp on a difference). Simulate K models spread
   uniformly over the same span (adjacent gaps as printed: 4.0, 2.2, 1.1, 0.7, 0.4 pp), each shifted by an independent N(0, 1.9 pp), and count
   draws where any pair changes order (1,000,000 draws per K, seed 42), beside the printed 0.28, 0.86, 0.9997, 1.00, 1.00. Also the 0.8 pp pair
   (printed 38%).
J2 Within-pool against cross-pool disagreement at the single-annotator level, on all comparisons: the share of label pairs that differ
   (A, Tie, B) and that reverse (A against B) for crowd against crowd, expert against expert, and crowd against expert (the four cross
   pairs averaged per comparison), with a comparison bootstrap (2,000 resamples, seed 42) on the cross minus within differences. It says how
   much of the pool difference is the difference between any two annotators.

## Addendum 1 (31 Oct 2026, written after J1-J2 were computed and before J1b; J1b is defined after the data)
J1 used SD 1.9 pp per model, which gives SD 2.69 pp on a difference, not the 2.6 pp the paper measures on the pairwise difference. J1b repeats J1
with SD 2.6/sqrt(2) = 1.84 pp per model, so the difference SD equals the paper's, same draws and seed.

## Addendum 2 (31 Oct 2026, after the referee pass; J2b and J3 are defined after the data)
Corrections: J1 is not new. The camera-ready release (github whose-gold, 3 Oct 2026) already replaces Table 3 with 0.29, 0.92, 1.00 from a
simulation with shared models and withdraws it as validation, so J1 only confirms both columns. J2's interval resampled comparisons only, while
89 crowd workers and 138 experts supply every label, so it is too narrow, and "the pool adds a point" is causal wording on an observational
contrast. New readings, each defined after the data:
J2b J2's cross minus within differences with an annotator-grouped jackknife standard error (delete one annotator id at a time).
J3 a pool-blind baseline for the 23.6% and 9.2%: split each comparison's four annotators into two mixed pairs (crowd 1 with expert 1 against crowd 2
with expert 2, and crowd 1 with expert 2 against crowd 2 with expert 1), keep comparisons where both pairs are unanimous, and count majorities that
differ and that name opposite winners, beside the actual pools; the pool excess (actual minus each mixed split) with the annotator-grouped
jackknife standard error.

## Addendum 3 (31 Oct 2026, after J2b and J3 printed; defined after the data)
The delete-one-annotator jackknife removes each comparison four times (once per annotator on it), so with no annotator effect it can overstate
the width. Before any J2b or J3 interval is used: KNOWN-GOOD for the ruler. Shuffle the evaluator ids among the annotations of each pool (each id
keeps its number of labels, the link between id and label is broken), 20 shuffles, seed 42, and compare the jackknife SE with the comparison
bootstrap SE (2,000 resamples) on the same shuffled data, where the comparison bootstrap is the right width. Second ruler beside it: an
annotator bootstrap with product weights (each annotator id drawn Poisson(1), a comparison weighted by the product of its four annotators'
draws, 2,000 draws, seed 42), run on the shuffled data too. A ruler whose shuffled SE is more than 1.5 times the comparison SE is reported as an
upper bound only.

## Addendum 4 (31 Oct 2026, after the second referee pass; defined after the data)
The second referee found annotator slot order is chronological (slot 1 before slot 2 in 92% of crowd and 94% of expert comparisons), so a
single mixed split depends on slot labels. J3p replaces J3's single splits: the order-free baseline that pools both mixed pairings (kept,
differ and opposite counts summed over split 1 and split 2). Rulers for the excess (actual minus J3p): the comparison bootstrap, which the
referee's simulation with the real annotator design found close to the right width (0.85 to 1.03 of the truth), printed with the
jackknife as the upper-width ruler beside it. The upper-width rulers have about 5% power at a true +2 points, so no CANNOT_TELL rests on them.
