## 3. Attack on the frame
Width fairness. The served widths are month specific, not one 2026 number: US sales RSE 7.7 to 10.2 (year means 2016 to 2026), starts 4.8 to 6.2, completions 5.4 to 6.8, retail CV 0.5 in 2016 rising to 1.0 in 2026, Northeast sales 23 to 35. So the width moves a lot over time (retail doubles), and "a 2026 width applied to 2016 prints" is slightly wrong: it is the current copy of each month's field. Whether Census revises E_ for old months is undocumented, so as printed is unknown, which the desk says. It does not change a reading: the 90th percentile of |later - first| in stated half-widths is 0.54 (sales S1), 0.65 (S2), 0.40 and 0.49 (starts), 0.45 and 0.47 (completions), 0.46 and 0.57 (retail). A half-width scaled by 0.65 keeps every US cell at or above 90 percent. So ABOVE is structural: sampling error of the level (RSE 5 to 10 percent) is several times the size of the typical revision (median 1 to 3 percent), and no plausible drift in the width flips it. ABOVE reads "revisions are small against the stated sampling interval". It does not mean the interval is too wide, since the interval answers a different question (below).
Regional exception: Northeast sales (RSE about 30) gives 97 of 105 inside at S2 with 8 above and the max miss is 3.3 to 3.9 half-widths, so the regional tail is real and reads consistent. The first-read headline ("all housing cells read ABOVE") is true only for US totals plus Midwest, South, West in S1/S2. It is not true of Northeast S2/S3 (consistent) or West/South S3 edges, and the table prints that correctly.
Interval construction. Housing: 1.645 x RSE/100 x first print, RSE is relative so base is the first print. Correct per the Census SOC methodology and the release footnote (release text I read: "Average RSE (%)"). Retail 1.753 x CV x level matches the retail release text verbatim (I fetched the 15 Oct 2026 release: "the estimated value +/-1.753 x CV x (the estimated monthly total)", and "the median estimated coefficient of variation is given", fix 2). Base: the first print p1, a reasonable plug-in because the true standard error is relative to the true value, which is unknown. Rounding of the whole-number RSE: shown as sensitivity, does not flip.
What the test means. Census's interval is a sampling interval around the true value of that month under complete enumeration. "Later print inside the first print's interval" is not a test of that statement: a revision is mostly nonsampling (late reports, benchmark), and the first print's interval is symmetric around the first print, not around the truth. The test reads whether revisions are small compared to the stated sampling error. It cannot say the interval is wrong or too conservative, and an outside row cannot say Census erred. The page says part of this (a later value is not the quantity 90 percent is stated for). Fix 3 closes it. The power is low: any width above about half the stated one gives the same reading.
Independence. Wilson assumes independent rows. Months overlap and regions sum to the US, so the true interval is wider. Because worst misses are under one half-width in US cells and the 90th percentile is about half, the above readings survive any plausible dependence (an effective n of 30 still gives a lower bound near 0.88 for 30 of 30 and the shares are well above that margin in the median half-width terms). The printed caveat is correct, and "narrower than it should be" is the honest wording.
Annual revision handling. As the plan says. Dropped rows are the larger revisions (retail S2 dropped rows: 19 of 22 inside = 86.4 percent, median 0.41 half-widths against 0.20 for scored). Housing dropped rows are also inside (all US dropped rows in sales and starts S1/S2 are inside). Excluding biases retail S2 upward (95.2 percent scored, 92.9 percent with all rows, interval 87.1 to 96.2). Housing: no reading change. The exclusion rule uses "any month 14+ months older changed", which flags 11 housing annual vintages (every May) correctly and 13 retail (adds 2018-05-25 and 2019-... style off-cycle ones, and the two big ones).
Retail break. 2025-04-25 (documented, nonemployers out, median -1.8 percent level shift on old months) and 2026-09-28 (AIES revision, max 4.6 percent) are both real, both visible in ALFRED. The plan's 3 percent rule found only the second. Every retail S3 row has a first print before 2026-09-28, so all 128 S3 rows are dropped under either date, and excluding S3 entirely is right (every later value there crosses at least one revision, and S3 also crosses April 2025 for rows printed before it). S1 and S2 rows are dropped for any annual vintage in the window, so the break only added 1 row each, and reading is the same under April 2025. Fix 1 states this.
