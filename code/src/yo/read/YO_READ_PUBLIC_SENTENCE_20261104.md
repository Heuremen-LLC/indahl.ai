YO Read, a second adapter trained to read quantum-hardware papers, was scored again on its 148 held-out papers under a rule fixed and timestamped before the run, after 22 of those papers were found to have been given the wrong text (the answers it is scored against did not change; that check, and the decision to rescore, were made after we had seen the first scores, so the rescore is a correction defined after the data, under a rule fixed before it ran). On whether a paper prints the dates of its hardware runs, two of its three seeds beat always answering no (0.919 and 0.939 of papers right, against 0.804), and the third does not (0.831), because it says yes too readily: it catches 28 of the 29 papers that print dates but also says yes on 24 of the 119 that do not. On whether a paper links its own data or code, all three seeds beat always answering no (0.811, 0.757 and 0.811, against 0.514), which is a low bar, and 5 of the 148 answers it is scored against there are still wrong: our code read them as yes where the List entry says the paper prints no link. On whether a paper says its data are available on request, the seeds score 0.980, 0.959 and 0.966 against 0.912 for always answering no, but none survives the correction for testing several questions at once, and with only 13 papers that say so, this test is too small to show a gain reliably. Each paper counts once per question, and three seeds are a count of trained copies, not a rate.
