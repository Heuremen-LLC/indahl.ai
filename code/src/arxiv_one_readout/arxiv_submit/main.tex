\title{One Readout, Three Clocks: Phase Kicks and Decoherence of Untouched Qubits during Mid-Circuit Readout, Measured against an Equal Idle}

\author{AUTHOR NAME}
\email{Indahl@heuremen.com}
\affiliation{Heuremen LLC}

\date{\today}


\begin{abstract}
We compare each mid-circuit readout with an idle of exactly the same scheduled length, on the same qubits and in the same job, and measure what the readout does to qubits that are never measured, on IBM heavy-hex processors. With 39 of 156 qubits read out, the untouched qubits' phase is kicked by about 1.6 to 2.9$^\circ$ per readout (median over 117 untouched qubits), on four processors in a first run and on three of them in a repeat whose plan was timestamped on a public server before the jobs were created and whose analysis was timestamped before any result was opened. The kick is not extra elapsed time: qubits whose idle phase turns either way are kicked the same way. It is not larger when the measured qubits start in $|1\rangle$, it comes from the readouts themselves (reading out 1 of the 39 qubits leaves at most about $0.15^\circ$), it reaches qubits three to four couplings from the nearest measured one, and over 72 readouts it does not wear out on two of three processors (on the third it is 5\% smaller). Readouts also cost untouched qubits superposition (0.004--0.021 less coherence) and Bell pairs entanglement (0.007--0.010 less concurrence after four readouts) on three processors; both survive when every measured neighbour is verified in its $|0\rangle$ cluster from the raw readout signal, and the entanglement cost exceeds the cost when only one qubit is read out on the same schedule, so a time shift common to both is excluded. The measured qubit itself loses per readout what idling 1.3 to 1.7 stated readout lengths would cost it. All plans, circuits, analysis code, job identifiers and outputs are released; raw IQ files are available on request.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A dispersive readout of a transmon populates a resonator, rings up and down, and dephases and can excite or relax the measured qubit \cite{Gambetta2006,Boissonneault2009,Sank2016}; the resonator emptying time and ways to shorten it are well studied \cite{McClure2016,Bultink2016,Walter2017,Swiadek2024}. Unmeasured qubits are also affected: stray coupling to neighbouring resonators and spectral overlap of readout tones dephase them in multiplexed readout \cite{Heinsoo2018}, spectator effects can be mitigated with tunable couplers \cite{Vallesa2026}, and static ZZ coupling makes a qubit's frequency depend on its neighbours' states \cite{Sundaresan2020,Kandala2021,Ketterer2023,Zhao2021}. Measurement-induced dephasing and Stark shift of a read qubit have a general perturbative treatment \cite{Malekakhlagh2022}. Mid-circuit measurement has been characterised with gate-set tomography \cite{Rudinger2022} and, on IBM hardware, with a randomised-benchmarking suite that quantifies errors on measured and spectator qubits \cite{Govia2023}; dynamic circuits depend on it \cite{Corcoles2021}.

Here every readout step is paired with an idle step of exactly the published readout duration, on the same qubits, in the same job, with identical scheduling for the untouched qubits. We read three quantities on those qubits: phase, which a frequency shift or a phase kick moves, and single-qubit coherence and two-qubit concurrence, which a phase cannot move. Each reading is then repeated beside the ordinary causes that could produce it: extra elapsed time, excitation or leakage of the measured qubits, and the number and position of the qubits read out.

\section{Methods}

\paragraph*{Processors and roles.} On each 156-qubit processor (ibm\_fez, ibm\_kingston, ibm\_marrakesh; ibm\_pittsburgh in the first run) the same 39 qubits are measured; the other 117 receive no gates during the steps and are read out only at the end. The readout duration $L$ is the processor's published \texttt{measure} duration: 1.660\us{} (fez), 2.180\us{} (kingston, pittsburgh), 2.684\us{} (marrakesh).

\paragraph*{Steps.} After preparation and a barrier on all qubits, $N$ steps follow, each ending in a barrier: in the idle arm the measured qubits wait exactly $L$; in the readout arm they are measured. Untouched qubits start on the equator (phase and coherence, read in the $X$ and $Y$ quadratures) or in Bell pairs $|\Phi^+\rangle$ on coupled qubits (concurrence, X-state form from $ZZ,XX,YY,XY,YX$). Coherence is $|\langle X\rangle+i\langle Y\rangle|$. Circuits were transpiled with the identity layout and as-late-as-possible scheduling (Qiskit 2.5.2, qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.49.0, SamplerV2); no dynamical decoupling was used.

\paragraph*{Runs.} (i) First run, 21 October: $N\in\{0,2,4,8,16\}$, 2000 shots, one job per processor; plan hashed locally 51\,s before the first job (not externally timestamped); fez, kingston and marrakesh were declared, pittsburgh was added afterwards with the same plan, and an ibm\_boston job did not return. (ii) Superposition and entanglement run, same day, $N\in\{0,8,16,32\}$. (iii) Control run: plan and circuits pushed to GitHub at 23:00:47 UTC, IBM created the first job at 23:01:05, and the analysis was pushed at 23:02:19, before any result was opened and before the second and third jobs; every circuit runs twice in its job (two shuffled copies); arms with 1, 10 or 39 qubits read out on the same schedule. (iv) Raw-readout runs (coherence; Bell pairs): every mid-circuit readout returned as its raw IQ signal, so each measured neighbour can be verified in its own $|0\rangle$ cluster (Mahalanobis distance below 3.5 to the calibration cluster) on every readout. (v) Deciding runs, 22 October (UTC): measured qubits packed in one region to read the kick against distance, readout counts up to 81, measured qubits held in $|1\rangle$ against an idle in $|1\rangle$, and a fresh-clock run in which untouched qubits sit in $|0\rangle$ during 0, 27 or 72 readouts (or 72 idles) and then start a new clock. Runs (iii)--(v) were each pushed to GitHub before their jobs; server timestamps are on the data pages.

\paragraph*{Ruler.} Every reading is a difference between two arms of the same job, per qubit or per pair, median over qubits, with a 95\% bootstrap interval (2000 resamples). Four outcomes are allowed: above 0, below 0, consistent with 0 (the interval is then the bound), or cannot tell (fewer than five units, or the two copies of a circuit disagree). Copies are compared by their difference. Signs are carried, never folded. Processors are combined only by value and width (inverse variance), with the chip-to-chip spread printed, and only where they agree. The deciding-run analyses were tested on synthetic data with a planted effect and with none (10 of 210 null readings resolved, 4.8\%); on real hardware, copy~0 minus copy~1 of the control run resolves 2.0\% of 147 readings with the median and 4.8\% with a 20\% trimmed mean. This is the floor of the ruler.

\section{Results}

\paragraph*{A phase kick per readout.} Each readout kicks the phase of untouched qubits by $+1.73^\circ$ [1.29, 2.21] (fez), $+2.41^\circ$ [1.96, 3.58] (kingston), $+2.13^\circ$ [1.44, 2.79] (marrakesh) and $+1.79^\circ$ [1.16, 2.66] (pittsburgh) in the first run (median over 117 qubits; this excess-phase analysis was written after the data, at an outside reviewer's suggestion; the pre-registered ratio of readout to idle phase, 1.17--1.20, depends on which slowly precessing qubits are kept). The timestamped control run gives 1.62, 2.86 and $2.39^\circ$ on fez, kingston and marrakesh, all intervals clear of 0 (its script reads the $Y$ axis with the opposite convention, so the printed sign is opposite).

\paragraph*{Not extra elapsed time.} Extra time would advance each qubit along its own precession, so qubits precessing backwards in the idle arm would be kicked the other way. They are kicked the same way: the 21 to 36 qubits per processor whose idle phase turns opposite to the others are kicked by 1.0 to $3.4^\circ$ per readout (fez 1.18 and 0.96, kingston 3.03 and 2.80, marrakesh 3.37 and $1.52^\circ$ in the control and raw-readout runs), every interval clear of 0.

\paragraph*{Not larger with the measured qubits in $|1\rangle$.} With an idle arm that also holds the measured qubits in $|1\rangle$, the kick in $|1\rangle$ is $0.18^\circ$ (fez) and $0.92^\circ$ (marrakesh) per readout smaller than in $|0\rangle$, and on kingston (whose $|0\rangle$ reading has shifted copies) no difference is resolved, at most $0.005^\circ$ larger. Holding the measured qubits in $|1\rangle$ without any readout shifts the phase by $2.5$--$3.4^\circ$ per step, which is why the comparison needs the matched idle.

\paragraph*{It comes from the readouts, and reaches past the wiring.} On the same schedule, reading out 1 of the 39 qubits leaves at most about $0.15^\circ$ (fez, kingston; on marrakesh the two copies disagree); reading out all 39 gives the full kick. With the 39 measured qubits packed in one region, untouched qubits three to four couplings from the nearest measured one are kicked by 0.81, 1.77 and $1.30^\circ$ per readout (fez, kingston, marrakesh; 15 qubits, every interval above 0). They are not wired to any measured qubit.

\paragraph*{It does not wear out.} In the fresh-clock run (clock coherence 0.53--0.76 in the reading window, so the phases are readable), a clock started after 72 readouts gets the same kick as one started after none to within $0.12^\circ$ on fez, and $0.13^\circ$ less of $2.57^\circ$ on marrakesh; on kingston, 72 readouts against 72 equal idles differ by under $0.06^\circ$. An earlier count run seemed to show the kick fading after about 27 readouts, but by then most clocks had no coherence left to read a phase from (67--97 of 117 below five times the shot noise); that reading is not interpreted, because the clocks cannot be read.

\paragraph*{Superposition cost.} The median untouched qubit next to a measured one keeps 0.004 to 0.021 less coherence after readouts than after the same idle: on kingston and marrakesh after 2, 4 and 8 readouts (2 readouts from the control run), on fez after 2 and 4 (not resolved after 8; without the clean-shot selection below, fez is not resolved). In the raw-readout run, keeping only shots in which every measured neighbour was verified in its $|0\rangle$ cluster on every readout (97--99\% of shots), the loss remains: kingston $+0.0056$ and $+0.0062$ (4, 8 readouts), marrakesh $+0.0087$ and $+0.0205$, fez $+0.0042$ and $+0.0057$ (2, 4). Shots with a neighbour read as $|1\rangle$ therefore do not account for it. The pre-registered loss ratio at $N=32$ of the superposition and entanglement run did not detect this deficit: a ratio shrinks a fixed deficit toward 1 once the idle loss is large.

\paragraph*{Entanglement cost.} Untouched Bell pairs keep 0.007 to 0.010 less concurrence than after the same idle after 4 readouts on all three processors, in shots where every measured neighbour was verified in $|0\rangle$ (marrakesh 0.0093, kingston 0.0100, fez 0.0070; pairs whose idle concurrence is above 0). On the same schedule the loss with 39 qubits read out exceeds the loss with 1 read out by 0.0088, 0.0104 and 0.0065: a time shift per step that is the same whatever number of qubits is read is excluded.

\paragraph*{The measured qubit.} A qubit that is read out loses more than idling does. On fez, kingston and marrakesh (median over about 30 measured qubits, three reads on 4 to 7 October) a measured qubit loses per readout what idling 1.5 to 1.7 stated readout lengths would cost it, and on ibm\_pittsburgh, ibm\_boston, ibm\_phoenix and ibm\_aachen 1.3 to 1.4. This conversion assumes a single-exponential decay curve, which these qubits do not strictly follow. In a separate run on 21 October, the 95\% upper bounds on extra readout time that advances untouched qubits' phase are 4, 1 and 12\% (rounded up from 3.7, 0.8 and 11.4\%; fez, kingston, marrakesh), against the 50--70\% needed to explain the measured qubit's loss as a longer readout. Under repeated readout the 30-qubit average on ibm\_fez falls to one half after 22.6 to 25.5 readouts (three jobs, 11 October).

\section{Discussion}

A mid-circuit readout on these processors does three things to qubits it never touches. It kicks their phase by about two degrees, by an amount set by the readouts themselves (not by elapsed time, the state of the measured qubits, or a fixed chip-wide offset), reaching qubits several couplings away and not wearing out on two of three processors. It costs them a small, fixed amount of superposition. And it costs Bell pairs among them a small amount of entanglement. The last two survive the verification of every measured neighbour from its raw signal, and the entanglement cost is larger with 39 qubits read out than with 1 on the same schedule. A fixed phase kick leaves coherence and concurrence unchanged; a kick that varies from shot to shot would reduce them, and these runs do not separate the two. The mechanism of the kick is not identified by these runs; a control-system phase-frame update and a physical frequency shift during the readout remain open, and the reach of three to four couplings constrains both.

\paragraph*{Limitations.} The first run's kick analysis was written after the data, and the plans of the first run and of the superposition and entanglement run were hashed locally but not externally timestamped; runs (iii)--(v) were each pushed to a public server before their jobs. Intervals are within-job bootstraps; processors are the independent units, three in most runs. The fade of the kick past about 27 readouts and any compounding of the superposition cost past about 9 cannot be read with these clocks, which are dead by then. Where the kick stops with distance is not established. For fez coherence (2, 4 readouts), kingston concurrence (4) and the fez 39-versus-1 reading, the committed analysis printed `copies disagree'; they are used here because a copy-gap check defined after the data found no resolved shift between the copies. Leakage to a level read as $|0\rangle$ is not excluded for the superposition cost. All runs are on IBM processors in October 2026.
