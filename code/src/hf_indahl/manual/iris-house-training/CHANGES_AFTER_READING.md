# Changes and timing that were not strictly fixed in advance

Each change below was made after the round in question had produced results, or is a step that ran before its rule's timestamp. Nothing here changed a stamped rule; where a rule was added later, it says so.

1. Round two's evaluation queue stopped on an error in its own checksum step: it looked for the list-question files in the wrong folder. The check was re-run from the right folder; every file listed in the stamped SHA256SUMS files for round two's plan and changes (A14 to A19) and for the list-question items (P4M) matched its stamped hash, and the evaluations then ran unchanged.
2. Round two's training started before its plan's Bitcoin block, as the plan allowed (training produces no reading): the first training log was created at 13:48 UTC and the block is 974942 at 14:40 UTC. Every evaluation started after the block.
3. Training failures: seed 63's first run and both of seed 64's runs reached a loss that was not a number. A rule written before any round-two evaluation (retrain once with the same seed; a second failure counts the seed as failed) was applied. Seed 63's retrain began at 14:36 UTC, about four minutes before that rule's block, so for that retrain the rule is self-witnessed. Seed 64 failed twice and was not evaluated.
4. The script that computed round two's readings was written after the evaluation rows existed and before any of its numbers were seen; two fixes to how it matched rows across files were made before it printed anything.
5. Defined after the data: a secondary read of the unclassified answers to the list questions. Its rule, including a 0.0015 tolerance for "a value from the list", was timestamped before it ran. It over-credits 8 of 151 answers compared with exact matching. A non-blind reading in full of 46 answers (40 drawn at random plus 6 chosen as tolerance-only) sides with exact matching. The timestamped rule stays the reported one, with the exact count beside it.
6. Reruns of the evaluations, with the same seeds, run while the house's other model seats used the same GPU. Round one's evaluation reproduced all 2,040 rows. Seed 63's trained model reproduced all 1,020 of its rows. The untouched model's reruns did not always reproduce: in seed 63's evaluation, its first sample differed from round one's on 173 of 340 rows (25 changed score, 11 of them on P3 items, which nets to only three rows on P3 because flips ran both ways); in a second rerun of seed 63's evaluation that first sample matched round one again, while the second and third samples each differed on 35 rows (9 changed score). A rerun of seed 62's evaluation reproduced all 1,020 trained rows; its untouched-model rows differed from round one's only in the second sample, on 19 of 340 rows (3 changed score). Across these reruns the trained arms reproduced every time (3 of 3), and the untouched model's scored share wrong was 0.4809, 0.4823, 0.4829 and 0.479, its share not right 0.600, 0.599, 0.599 and 0.599. No cause is named. Those runs are in `eval/`.
7. Our internal report had said the trained models got worse on the P3 family. That was an artifact of counting only answers the code could score. Both counts are now reported, and the direction depends on which is used.
8. The public text went through several referee passes before release. Wording and some stated figures were corrected: the untouched model's repeat spread on the every-answer count (0.015, beside 0.008 on the scored count), the unreadable share (22.5 to 22.9%), the P3 framing, and how the hand-read sample was described. The evaluation rows and the numbers computed from them did not change.
