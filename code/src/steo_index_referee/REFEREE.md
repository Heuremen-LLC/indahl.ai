## 2. Findings

**R1. Excel error cells in the .xls copies read as the number 42. Severity: blocks ship.**
Evidence: `cg_20111119111250_LUTRMTMZ.xls`, sheet Fig1, row 43 on: Historical month cells hold a price, every interval cell holds 42, and so do Settle, Implied Volatility and Days to Expiry for months with no data. 42 is the BIFF error code for #N/A, and xlrd returns error cells (type 5) as their code. `xls_sheets()` in steo_tick.py does `v = sh.cell_value(r, k)` and keeps it without looking at the cell type, so 42 enters as `'42.0'` and `fnum` accepts it. The xlsx reader is fine (`t="e"` cells come through as the text `#N/A`). In all 10 .xls copies there are 860 error cells in the Lower/Upper columns (430 rows). 392 are months before release (counted as "months begun at release"). 38 are future months with an error cell, scored Lower = Upper = 42.0 and read ABOVE. All 38 are WTI. (The 392 historical-month error rows are split between the two sheets; I did not need that split.) They are 38 of the 38 WTI plan-cell "above" rows, and 38 of 62 ABOVE rows overall. Some also sit in the 1 to 2 month cell (10 of 10 above) and the 13 or more cell (21 of 21 above), which is why those cells read BELOW at 66.0 and 72.3 percent.
Gates did not catch it because gate 2 reads three xlsx cells and gate 4 reads only the history column.
Fix: in `xls_sheets`, skip cells with `sh.cell_type(r, k) in (xlrd.XL_CELL_EMPTY, xlrd.XL_CELL_BLANK, xlrd.XL_CELL_ERROR)` (or map type 5 to the text `#N/A`). Rerun steo_tick.py, steo_headline.py and build_page.py. Add gate 6: no used row has Lower == Upper, and no used copy has a numeric 42 in any Lower/Upper cell with an error-typed source. Add one known-good row from an .xls copy (for example a real interval cell of `cg_20120207180925_FDZOLAQ5.xls`, row 67 on, Lower 71.63 / Upper 120.56 style value) and one planted error cell that must come out as no interval. Then send the rebuilt desk back for a recount. I expect my table above.
Effect on the page: the headline "392 of 490 ... 80.0 percent ... below" becomes "392 of 452 ... 86.7 percent ... cannot tell", with no "above" share. The Addendum 1 headline sentence becomes WTI 91.0 percent (82.8 to 96.8, consistent). **R9. Delivered quantity and timing, described not scored. Severity: note, one optional copy line.**
The chart label and the Jan 2012 text say the interval is for "the market's expectations of monthly average WTI prices" in the contract month. The sheet's own Days to Expiry column (present for the 61 lead-1 rows to 7 rows at lead 23 where it exists) has a median of 15 days at lead 1, 34 at lead 2, 55 at lead 3, 117 at lead 6, 244 at lead 12. So the interval is built from the implied volatility of the option that expires about 2 to 3 weeks into the month before the delivery month, at that expiry, while the delivered number is the calendar-month average of daily spot prices that ends roughly 6 weeks after the options expire for lead 1. NYMEX contract months settle against a delivery month, EIA spot is a calendar-month average of daily prints, so (i) the expiry date of the price the bounds describe sits before the month starts, (ii) the averaging shortens variance and the extra time lengthens it. I do not claim a direction. I could not reproduce the sheet's bounds from F x exp(+-1.96 x vol x sqrt(days/365)) (median 8 to 9 percent off), so I do not state the exact formula. The page is a fair test of EIA's own words (EIA calls it a monthly average). Optional line: "The bounds are built from options that expire before the month starts, as the workbook's Days to Expiry column shows, and the spot price is the average over the month." That is a described fact, no cause for a result.
