# Social platform data scout

Read on 9 November 2026 (HST), 10 November UTC. Asked: does Meta publish any social media data, does anyone, check Reddit too. Scope: public data a Heuremen LLC desk could reuse, especially where a platform states a number and the delivered data can be read against it. No sign-ups, no accounts, no paid tiers were used. Samples are in `~/bones-forge/research_social/samples/`.

## The short answer

Yes. The biggest public social media dataset in the world is not published by any platform. It is the EU's DSA Transparency Database. Every online platform in the EU must file a "statement of reasons" for each moderation decision. The Commission publishes all of them under CC BY 4.0, with no key. October 2026 alone holds 834,739,154 statements from 212 platforms. Facebook filed 34,175,412 of them, TikTok 17,056,103, Instagram 8,109,875, YouTube 5,029,409, X 722,059, Reddit 47,116.

The platforms also publish their own DSA transparency reports. Since February 2026 these follow one machine-readable template of CSV files. So the same platform states its moderation counts twice: once per decision in the database, once as totals in its report. Reading one against the other is exactly the house method. We ran the first pair tonight (YouTube, below) and the gap is large.

Meta itself publishes enforcement totals (PDF and web pages), an ad library behind a login, and a research library that LLCs cannot join. Reddit's API needs approval and a separate agreement for any commercial use. Reddit's moderation shows up in the EU database, keyless. Bluesky is fully open, including a signed public stream of every moderation label it has applied since April 2023.

## Ranked desk ideas

### 1. Two filings, one platform (DSA database against DSA report)

The stated number: each very large platform's harmonised DSA transparency report, a zip of CSV files in the Commission template (Implementing Regulation 2024/2835). File `8_automated_means.csv` gives "Number of measures solely taken by automated means" and "Number of measures not taken by automated means". Other files give own-initiative measures, notices, appeals and accuracy ranges. Reports are half-yearly, due end of February and end of August.

The delivered data: the same platform's statements of reasons in the DSA Transparency Database for the same half-year. The Commission's monthly aggregate files count statements by platform, day, category, decision type, content type, source and the two automation fields.

The comparison: per platform and per half-year, total measures, the solely automated count and the not automated count, against the database counts. Report the gap with an interval and one of the four outcomes. The interval has to carry the known scope differences: the report counts by decision date and the aggregates by submission date, and late filings land in the next month. Read both codebooks first and write the matching rule into the plan before the first number.

Proof run tonight, YouTube, 1 January to 30 June 2026:

| | Report (Google, 28 Aug 2026) | Database (statements filed Jan to Jun 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Solely or fully automated | 34,496,297 | 24,141,114 |
| Not automated | 1,931,541 | 16,216,323 |
| Partially automated | not a field | 2,580,414 |
| Total | 36,427,838 | 42,937,851 |

The not automated count differs by a factor of about 8.4. This is a reading, not a finding yet. The report's scope and the database's partial category have to be aligned before any outcome is printed. It is enough to show the desk has teeth. Sources: `samples/google_dsa/yt/8_automated_means.csv`, `samples/dsa_agg/h1/`.

Coverage: every VLOP that files to the database. That includes Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, X, LinkedIn, Snapchat, Pinterest and Reddit (Reddit is not a VLOP but files statements), plus the marketplaces (Google Shopping filed 495,152,032 statements in October alone). The Commission's page "DSA brings transparency" links every VLOP report: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/dsa-brings-transparency. Google's reports are also in a public, listable bucket: `https://storage.googleapis.com/transparencyreport/?prefix=report-downloads/`.

A second reading from the same data, worth a row of its own: the automation fields do not agree with each other. In October 2026 Facebook and Instagram filed 0.0% of statements as fully automated decisions (all "partially automated") while marking automated detection on 98.8% and 97.7%. X filed 97.1% as fully automated decisions and 0.0% as automated detection. TikTok's own Q2 2026 report states that automation accounted for 97.4% of all video removals worldwide. In its EU filings for October, 87.1% of statements are fully automated.

### 2. Bluesky labels, counted from the signed stream

The stated number: Bluesky's 2025 Transparency Report (29 January 2026). Press coverage gives 16.49 million labels applied, 2.44 million takedowns and 9.97 million user reports. Read these from Bluesky's own post before use: https://bsky.social/about/blog/01-29-2026-transparency-report-overview. A third party audit is reported to count about 10.6 million labels, so the question is already live.

The delivered data: `wss://mod.bsky.app/xrpc/com.atproto.label.subscribeLabels?cursor=0`. This is the Bluesky moderation service's own label stream. It replays from sequence 1 (13 April 2023), and every label is signed by the service's key. It is keyless. Tonight it delivered 15,001 frames in 8 seconds, so a full replay of a year takes hours, not days.

The comparison: count 2025 labels by value, net of negations (a label can be removed later with `neg: true`), against the stated total and the stated split. Counts are exact, so the interval comes from how negations and re-labels are counted. The plan must fix that rule before the replay. This repeats every year and can run live: labels per day against any stated rate.

### 3. TikTok by country, report against filings

The stated number: TikTok's quarterly Community Guidelines Enforcement data. It comes as a zip with a long-format CSV of 114,923 rows for Q2 2026, including "Total videos removed" per market and by task type (Automation, AI Moderation). URL taken from the "Download data" button: `https://sf16-va.tiktokcdn.com/obj/eden-va2/zkyhviozhk_YLNJ/ljhwZthlaukjlkulzlp/2026_Q2/26Q2USexcl/2026Q2_CGER_English.zip`.

The delivered data: TikTok's statements of reasons in the DSA database for the same quarter, restricted to video content and removal decisions.

Proof run tonight, April to June 2026: the 25 EU markets TikTok lists (Luxembourg and Malta are not broken out) sum to 17,864,150 videos removed. The database holds 18,928,826 TikTok statements that are video content with a removal decision. That is close, inside about 6%. The desk would show where the two agree as well as where they don't. A desk that only prints gaps is not the house style. Global extras from the same file: 188,298,141 videos removed in the quarter, 163,084,298 of them by automation.

### 4. Political ad spend: Google's library against campaign filings

The stated number: Google's political ads transparency bundle. It is keyless, updated daily (last modified 9 Nov 2026 23:45 GMT), 316 MB zipped: `https://storage.googleapis.com/political-csv/google-political-ads-transparency-bundle.zip`. It holds advertiser stats, weekly spend, geographic spend and per-creative spend (the creative file is 2.96 GB unzipped).

The delivered data: US federal committees report every payment to Google on FEC Schedule B. The FEC bulk files are keyless (the FEC API needs a free api.data.gov key).

The comparison: per committee and per quarter, Google's reported spend against the committee's reported disbursements to Google. Google reports spend in ranges for creatives and in exact USD for advertisers. The two disagree for known reasons, such as agency payments and timing. The interval and the matching rule (advertiser to FEC committee ID) are the work. Meta's equivalent would be the strongest pair, but the Meta Ad Library report refused a scripted read (HTTP 403) and its API needs an identity-confirmed Facebook account.

### 5. Stated users: one company, two regulators

The stated number: each VLOP's "average monthly active recipients in the EU", required every six months under DSA Article 24(2). The same companies' SEC filings state DAU or MAU by region (Meta, Reddit, Snap, Pinterest).

The delivered data: none for usage itself. No platform publishes delivered usage that we could check. This desk reads two statements against each other and against what is public. Region definitions differ (the EU against "Europe" against "Rest of World"). Wikipedia pageviews for each platform's article are a CC0 control series, not a measure of use. For Mastodon, each server self-reports in two places: nodeinfo (mastodon.social: 268,991 active in the month) and weekly logins at `/api/v1/instance/activity` (about 160,000 a week). The outcome is mostly CANNOT_TELL with an honest reason. It ranks fifth because the delivered side is weak.

Others considered: NY AG terms of service reports (filed under the Stop Hiding Hate Act, PDFs at ag.ny.gov) and California AB 587 reports (searchable at https://oag.ca.gov/ab587/submissions). Both are extra stated sources for desk 1 in the US. The Commission's Digital Services Terms and Conditions Database (https://platform-contracts.digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/) tracks versions of each platform's stated rules. It could pair with a CHANGE-style desk.

## Keys and accounts Wayfinder would need

None for the top four desks. Desks 1 to 3 run on keyless public files and streams. Desk 4 runs on keyless FEC bulk files.

Optional, in order of use:

1. api.data.gov key (for the FEC API, desk 4). Site: https://api.data.gov/signup. Free. Only needed if the bulk files are too slow. Terms: standard rate limits. Nothing forbids commercial use of FEC data, but the law forbids using individual contributor names to solicit or for commercial purposes (52 U.S.C. 30111(a)(4)). Desk 4 reads committee disbursements, not contributors, so this stays clear.
2. Meta Ad Library API. Site: https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/api. Free. Needs a personal Facebook account, Meta's identity confirmation (government ID) and a developer app. Terms: Meta Platform Terms and the Ad Library API terms. Read the terms before applying. Only worth it to give desk 4 a Meta side.
3. TikTok Commercial Content API. Site: https://developers.tiktok.com. Free. Needs a TikTok developer account. Eligibility for a commercial applicant was not confirmed tonight. Read before applying.

Not recommended:

- X API: paid only ($0.005 per post read, card on file).
- Reddit Data API: approval required, and commercial use needs a separate agreement with Reddit.
- YouTube Data API: free key, but the policies forbid aggregating data "to gain insights into YouTube's usage".
- Meta Content Library and TikTok Research API: not open to an LLC.

## What we did not do

We did not log in anywhere, accept any terms, or touch the Reddit dumps. We did not edit the site. Desk 1's YouTube table is a raw reading. It needs the scope rule and a referee pass before any surface uses it.
