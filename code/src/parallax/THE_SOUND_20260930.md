## 2. THE MODEL: damped oscillation fit

Fit `Dl - envelope = A * exp(-(l/l_d)^m) * cos(2*pi*l/l_A - delta)` to the broadband-subtracted
spectrum by grid search + closed-form linear solve (numpy only). **Cutoff test, three envelope
choices:**

| envelope | l_A | l_d | m | A | chi2 (n=83) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| poly, log10(l), deg 4 | 304.0 | 850.0 | 1.50 | 868.6 | 11416.9 |
| poly, log10(l), deg 6 | 302.0 | 850.0 | 1.50 | 876.7 | **10006.5 (best)** |
| Gaussian smooth, sigma=650 | 332.0 | 2178.6 | 3.60 | 67.4 | **177065.1 (failed)** |

The wide-Gaussian envelope is a documented failed step: sigma must clear the acoustic period
(l_A~300) to avoid re-absorbing the oscillation into the "smooth" curve; sigma=650 instead
over-smooths the rapid low-l rise on a linear-l kernel, producing a bad envelope and a chi2 17.7x
worse than either polynomial. **Primary envelope = poly deg 6** (lowest chi2, chosen post-hoc from
the table above, not assumed beforehand):

**l_A = 302.0 +/- 0.0** (acoustic scale, in l) &nbsp;&nbsp; **l_d = 850.0 +/- 44.0** &nbsp;&nbsp;
**m = 1.50 +/- 0.07** &nbsp;&nbsp; A = 876.7 +/- 70.3

Errors from 400 bootstrap draws (local refit around the global optimum). **Textbook column**
(Silk damping predicts roughly exp(-(l/l_d)^2), l_d ~ 1400-1500): this fit's own l_d (850 +/- 44)
sits well below the textbook range, and its own m (1.50 +/- 0.07) sits well below the textbook
m=2 -- this simple two-parameter damping-envelope fit, alone, does not reproduce the textbook
numbers; that mismatch is reported as a finding, not corrected toward the textbook value.

## 3. THE LOSS: three ways, plus a bonus fourth

Applied to the fitted oscillation (envelope held fixed, positions/heights recomputed on the full
model curve):

| variant | amp factor | l_d factor | peaks found | ratios | chi2 (n=83) | vs. undamaged (10006.5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| undamaged fit | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 5 (l=233.7,535.4,833.7,1128.0,1419.5) | 2/1=.717, 3/2=.703, 4/3=.659, 5/4=.605 | 10006.5 | -- |
| (a) amp *= exp(-1.83) | 0.1609 | 1.0000 | **1** (oscillation nearly erased) | -- | 18511.5 | FARTHER |
| (b) amp *= exp(-1.10) | 0.3332 | 1.0000 | 3 (l=209.8,513.6,802.4) | 2/1=.718, 3/2=.719 | 15377.2 | FARTHER |
| (c) l_d *= exp(-1.83) | 1.0000 | 0.1609 | **1** | -- | 21809.7 | FARTHER |
| (c2, bonus) l_d *= exp(-1.10) | 1.0000 | 0.3332 | 2 (l=217.3,450.9) | 2/1=.677 | 19909.3 | FARTHER |

**Every one of the three (plus the bonus fourth) loss variants makes the fit worse, not better** --
all four have higher chi2 than the undamaged fit, and all four also destroy peaks outright (the
1.83-sized losses wipe out peaks 2-5 entirely; even the smaller 1.10-sized amplitude loss loses
peaks 4 and 5). None of the three loss variants lands closer to Planck's measured spectrum than
the plain damped-oscillation fit does. Ratio-by-ratio, both surviving variants ((b) and the
undamaged fit) still disagree with the measured ratios by the same amount (2/1 off by +0.25,
3/2 off by -0.26) -- the extra loss does not fix or worsen the ratio mismatch that was already
there before any loss was applied; it just deletes the higher peaks.

## 4. THE INVERSE

What single factor would make the fitted oscillation's peak-height ratios match the 4 measured
ratios exactly (fixed peak positions, grid search + bootstrap, numpy only)?

- **Amplitude factor: hit the search-grid floor (f -> ~0.001), no interior minimum.** Squared
  error against the measured ratios keeps falling all the way to zero amplitude -- i.e., no
  finite amplitude-only rescaling reproduces the measured ratio pattern; the fit only "improves"
  in the degenerate limit of erasing the oscillation almost completely (model -> envelope).
  Reported as a failed inversion, not forced to a fake interior value.
- **Damping-length factor: f_ld = 0.2765 +/- 0.0015.** This one DOES land at an interior minimum.
  Converted to series-taus: **gap_inverse_ld = -ln(f_ld) = 1.286 +/- 0.005 series-taus.**

Placed beside the clock gap: **G_a = 1.099 +/- 0.077**, **G_s = 1.827 +/- 0.157**,
**gap_inverse_ld = 1.286 +/- 0.005**. The l_d-route inverse loss sits *between* the two clock
gaps -- about 2.4 sigma above G_a and about 3.4 sigma below G_s (errors added in quadrature; the
inverse's own error is tiny compared to either clock-gap error, so the clock gap's own spread
dominates both comparisons). It is in the same neighborhood as both, closer to G_a.
(The amplitude-route inverse gives no comparable number: gap_inverse_amp = 6.908, but that value
means nothing here since it comes from the grid floor, not a real minimum -- not placed beside the
clock gap.)

## 5. Plain words

The fitted damped oscillation, on its own (no clock-gap loss applied), already misses Planck's
measured peak-height ratios by 25-28% on the first two ratios -- this simple two-parameter model
is not a great fit to the real acoustic peaks even before any extra loss is added (chi2=10007 over
83 bins). Adding loss of the clock gap's size makes that worse in every form tried: multiplying
the whole oscillation's amplitude by exp(-1.10) or exp(-1.83), or shrinking the damping length by
the same factors, both raise chi2 (worse fit to Planck) and both destroy real, measured peaks
(the higher peaks vanish first) rather than reshaping them into something closer to what Planck
actually shows. In plain terms: losing "clock-gap" amounts of energy, applied as either a flat
amplitude cut or a length-scale cut, does not make the toy oscillation look more like the real
early universe -- it makes it look like an early universe with fewer peaks, which is not what's
observed.

The one place a clock-gap-sized number showed up on its own, unforced, was in the INVERSE
direction: asking what damping-length shrinkage would be needed to match the measured ratios
(not assumed, discovered by grid search) landed at 1.286 series-taus -- inside the same rough
neighborhood as the clock's own two candidate gaps (1.10 and 1.83), nearer the smaller one. That
is suggestive, not proof: it depends on holding peak positions fixed and on one particular (post-hoc
best-chi2) choice of broadband envelope, and the amplitude-route inversion found no comparable
number at all (it degenerates to zero). So: the real sound's peak structure sits closer to a
damping-length loss of about 1.3 series-taus than to either clock gap exactly, and that number is
closer to the clock-minus-arrow gap (1.10) than to the clock-minus-split gap (1.83).
