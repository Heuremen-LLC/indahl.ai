## 1. COPPER — time-irreversibility of the SERIES itself (not frac(fwd>bwd))

**Data note.** The local copy of the copper collector's output had gone stale (226 rows). Pulled fresh from
the laptop (`ssh c-train-express:~/copper_spectra/data.jsonl`, live collector, **n=398** now, up from 226) to
`parallax/out/do_better/data/`. Same 4 lines as before: 200 (actual bin 200.272 Hz), 212 (211.716 Hz), 300
(300.407 Hz), 12 (12.398 Hz) — per-minute dB level.

**Three tests, per line, each against a 1000-shuffle floor** (destroys order, keeps the value distribution —
this is the null of "no time structure at all"). Script: `copper_irreversibility.py`.

### (a) Rise/fall asymmetry — up-step vs down-step sizes and run lengths

| line | fwd (mean up − mean down) size, dB | shuffle floor mean±sd | p | fwd (mean up-run − mean down-run) | floor mean±sd | p | KS(up-size vs down-size) D, p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | +0.048 | −0.007±0.341 | 0.901 | −0.008 | +0.002±0.085 | 0.922 | 0.052, 0.945 |
| 212 | −0.088 | +0.021±0.373 | 0.804 | +0.022 | −0.005±0.088 | 0.798 | 0.055, 0.923 |
| 300 | −0.165 | −0.009±0.359 | 0.655 | +0.050 | +0.002±0.089 | 0.581 | 0.069, 0.714 |
| 12 | −0.397 | +0.014±0.353 | 0.288 | +0.088 | −0.004±0.086 | 0.319 | 0.060, 0.865 |

**No line clears p<0.05 on either the step-size or run-length asymmetry.** 12 Hz has the largest raw
size-asymmetry (−0.397 dB) but it's still well inside the shuffle floor (p=0.288) — the floor for this
statistic is wide because a 226–398-point EMA-free step series is intrinsically noisy at this granularity.

### (b) Third-order autocovariance asymmetry, A(k) = E[x_t² x_{t+k}] − E[x_t x_{t+k}²], k=1..10

40 tests (4 lines × 10 lags), each against its own 1000-shuffle floor. Full table in
`copper_irreversibility_results.json`; flagged cells:

| line | k | observed | shuffle floor mean±sd | p (1000 shuffles) | p (10,000-shuffle recheck) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **212** | **5** | **+81.59** | +0.01±23.10 | **0.000** | **0.0004** (4/10,000) |
| 300 | 9 | +72.35 | +0.10±28.37 | 0.016 | not rechecked |
| all other 38 cells | — | — | — | 0.15–0.99 | — |

**212 Hz, lag 5 is a real, floor-confirmed outlier** (~3.5σ, survives a 10,000-shuffle recheck and clears a
Bonferroni threshold across all 52 shuffle-tests run in this pass, 0.05/52≈0.00096 > 0.0004). **But it is
isolated**: lag 4 (−25.7, p=0.27) and lag 6 (−6.1, p=0.79) on the same line show no trace of it — a genuine
third-order time-asymmetry effect should not vanish between adjacent lags this sharply. Read plainly: this
is a measured, non-floor anomaly at one specific (line, lag) cell out of 40, not a smooth signature of
time-direction in the 212 Hz series. 300 Hz/lag 9 (p=0.016) does not survive Bonferroni and is reported for
completeness only.

### (c) Ordinal-pattern (length-3) irreversibility, KL(forward-pattern-freq ‖ backward-pattern-freq)

| line | observed KL | shuffle floor mean±sd | floor 95th pct | p (one-sided, KL≥floor) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | 0.00144 | 0.01327±0.01351 | 0.04091 | 0.955 |
| 212 | 0.00633 | 0.01305±0.01309 | 0.03882 | 0.629 |
| 300 | 0.01405 | 0.01266±0.01183 | 0.03517 | 0.337 |
| 12 | 0.01033 | 0.01350±0.01324 | 0.04067 | 0.450 |

**No line's forward/backward ordinal-pattern distribution differs from its own shuffle floor.** All four
observed KL values sit inside (in three cases well below) their null's mean — the permutation-pattern test
finds no directional structure at all.

**Tries, item 1:** test (a) 2 statistics × 1000 shuffles × 4 lines = 8000; test (b) 10 lags × 1000 shuffles ×
4 lines = 40000, plus one 10,000-shuffle recheck of the single flagged cell; test (c) 1000 shuffles × 4 lines
= 4000. **Total: 52,000 shuffle draws** (52 distinct shuffle-floored tests) + 1 confirmatory 10,000-shuffle
rerun.

**Verdict.** Two of three tests (rise/fall, ordinal-pattern) find **no** time-irreversibility in any of the
four copper lines, on a floor built for the raw series rather than a derived gradient statistic. The
third-order test finds one specific, floor-confirmed anomaly (212 Hz, lag 5) that does not extend to
neighboring lags — reported as a real but narrow, unexplained single-cell effect, not a line-wide
time-direction signature. This is a stronger, more direct null (and one real anomaly) than the prior
frac(fwd>bwd) pass could produce, because it tests the series' actual up/down and multi-point structure
instead of one EMA-derived scalar.

## 2. RIGETTI — the deficit IS the object (Wayfinder's correction: no decay fit, sign kept)

**Correction acted on mid-pass:** the lag axis here is shot count, not real time (CLOSE_THE_FREE_ONES
already established no swept `wait_us` exists inside a `shuffle` series) — fitting any decay/tau to it
would invent a timescale the data don't carry. This section instead reports the RAW lag-1..6 profile, per
sequence type (a, aba, bab, b, bb, ba), per qubit (q0, q61, q41, q90), each cell against its **own
1000-shuffle floor** (not the earlier binomial/z approximation), then lays Rigetti's numbers next to
Marrakesh's **with sign kept** and asks whether the disagreement is stable. Script: `rigetti_lag_profile.py`,
data: `standoff/chip/{run.npz,blink.npz,tune_fit.json}`.

Qubit pushed status (swing>0.3, from `tune_fit.json`): q0=True, q61=True, **q41=False**, q90=True. q41 is

approximation) and it is the one place this dataset disagrees with Marrakesh's sign; everywhere else
examined, Rigetti's failure-clumping agrees in sign with Marrakesh's carry.

## 3. THE BUZZ — 0-cloud vs 1-cloud on the same rescaled footing

**Rescale, grounded in code already on disk.** `odd_pubphase.py`'s own formula for a fitted-sinusoid phase's
standard error is `1/(amp * sqrt(n))` (amp = fitted oscillation amplitude = that seat's own signal radius
against unit per-shot noise after `buzz_common.iq_residual`'s own per-cloud robust normalization; n = shots
in the record). `nu_precise.json` already stores amp and n for every (job, pub, seat). This is exactly "in
units of that seat's own cloud radius and own cloud angular spread": SEM_phase(job,pub,seat) =
degrees(1/(amp·√n)). z(job,pub,seatpair) = raw_offset_deg / sqrt(SEM_A² + SEM_B²). Script:
`buzz_rescale.py`, data: `the_buzz/{nu_precise.json, phase_seats.json}`. All 12 records rescaled, none
skipped.

**Raw check first: is amp (cloud radius) actually very different between clouds?** 0-cloud mean amp=0.1578
(sd 0.0270, n=18 seat-records), 1-cloud mean amp=0.1671 (sd 0.0147, n=18). **Not hugely different** — the
raw-degree scale gap (0-cloud offsets ~14-20°, 1-cloud ~40-88°) is NOT mainly an amplitude/SNR artifact; it
reflects a genuinely different fixed phase offset between the two prepared states, which rescaling by SEM
does not remove (rescaling narrows the SPREAD comparison, not the mean).

**Rescaled (z-score) job-mean spread, side by side with the cloud-blind floor (1000 draws, same recipe as
`pair1_cloud_blind.py` but on z instead of raw degrees):**

| cloud | column | raw-degree spread (old) | rescaled (z) spread | cloud-blind floor mean±sd | P(floor ≤ observed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-cloud | seat80−116 (d80) | 1.92° | **0.292** | 1.426±0.416 | **0.004** |
| 0-cloud | seat100−116 (d100) | 1.79° | **1.506** | 6.287±1.890 | **0.006** |
| 1-cloud | seat80−116 (d80) | 6.11° | 1.133 | 1.426±0.416 | 0.309 |
| 1-cloud | seat100−116 (d100) | 3.41° | 1.832 | 6.287±1.890 | **0.043** |

**Do they now agree? Partially, and in the same qualitative pattern as before rescaling — not a different
story.** 0-cloud stays clearly tight on the rescaled footing on BOTH columns (p=0.004, 0.006) — its
tightness is not an artifact of a lower noise floor, since the floor test now accounts for each record's own
fitted SNR directly. 1-cloud's d80 column remains indistinguishable from the cloud-blind floor (p=0.309,
same conclusion as before rescaling), while its d100 column is borderline tight (p=0.043) — this mirrors the
ORIGINAL raw-degree test's own asymmetry (CLOSE_THE_FREE_ONES row 40 found 1-cloud p=0.051 for d80, p=0.012
for d100 — same pattern, d100 tighter than d80, preserved under rescaling). **Reading it plainly:** the
0-cloud/1-cloud tightness gap survives correction for the one confound this pass could rule out (per-record
SNR/amp differences were in fact small, not the driver); the remaining asymmetry (0-cloud tight on both
columns, 1-cloud only marginally tight on one) is not resolved by this rescale and stands as before —
neither confirmed as pure artifact nor as proven seat-lock, now on firmer footing because the "different
absolute scales" confound specifically raised in the prompt has been measured (amp difference: 6%, not the
dominant term) and found not to explain the gap by itself.

**Tries, item 3:** 1000 cloud-blind draws × 2 bins (A/B) × 2 columns (z80, z100) = 4000 random-bin spread
evaluations, plus 4 observed-vs-floor p-value comparisons.
