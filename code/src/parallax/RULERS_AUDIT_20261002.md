## 2. The rulers I OVERLOOKED

### On PITTSBURGH (the "blank" chip). It is not as blank as I said.
1. **The design itself is a prediction, and it was fired at Pittsburgh.** The three waits (60 / 240 / 300 us) were picked from what we expected of Kingston and Marrakesh (a fast drain near 60-80 us, a fading time near 300). The rest between shots (500 us) and the shot count came from Kingston's numbers too. Pittsburgh was measured with a ruler cut to fit another chip.
2. **The maker's ruler is on Pittsburgh whether we read it or not.** IBM calibrates that chip daily, and the raw readout we get back has already passed through the maker's own readout settings. We did not READ their numbers. Their ruler was still in every shot.
3. **Our eyes on the other two chips are now a prediction about Pittsburgh.** Since last night we have said "a look takes a bite, on both chips", "the clock stretches only on the chip we predicted on", "the third is the one that matters". Each of those is an expectation of what Pittsburgh will show. None is sealed. They exist, so they are rulers.
4. **Blind-drawn bits.** Kingston's and Phoenix's bits were drawn from the maker's list of usable bits. Pittsburgh's were drawn blind and may include bad ones. A difference on Pittsburgh could be that, and not the blank.
5. **Always fired last.** In every run the order was Kingston, Phoenix, Pittsburgh. Pittsburgh's shots are always the latest in the night.

### On PHOENIX (the "maker's only" chip). It stopped being maker's-only after Run 1.
6. **We read Phoenix's Run 1 and Run 2 before the next run fired, and talked about them.** I compared it to Kingston, computed what plain fading "should" give on it, and reported "Phoenix shows the same bite". From Run 2 on, Phoenix carried our expectations as well as the maker's. Unsealed, unlogged until now.
7. **The maker's list picked Phoenix's bits** (usable bits only), as on Kingston.

### On KINGSTON (ours)
8. **Its numbers were read again at every fire** (three extra reads of the maker's calibration that Phoenix and Pittsburgh did not get).
9. **Every Kingston prediction was the PLAIN story** ("a look does nothing", then "a look takes Run 2's bite and nothing else"). So Kingston was boxed by the textbook expectation, not by ours. What we call "ours" is the textbook's ruler in our handwriting.
10. **The hats were spoken between runs.** "Some proportion, inverse or not, with time and energy" (after Run 1) and "data through substrate, that's what the energy gain/loss looks like" (after Run 2) were said before Runs 2 and 3 fired. They are predictions on Kingston and Phoenix, unsealed. I logged them as hats (§103, §106) and did not count them as rulers.

### On ALL THREE
11. **The analysis is one ruler laid on three chips**: the frame (off = 0, on = 1 at look 1), a single fading curve fitted to the middle of nine bits, the fit's search limits.
12. **The "clock stretches with looks" reading has a ruler inside it.** The clock with two earlier looks is fitted on later times (300 to 540 us) than the other two (60 to 300 us). The nine bits fade at very different rates, and a mix of fast and slow bits reads slower the later you look. That alone can stretch a clock. Phoenix has the same construction and did not stretch, so it is not the whole story, but it is not ruled out for Kingston.

## 3. What this does to last night's reading
- "The chip we predicted on behaves differently from the chip we didn't" is too clean. Honest version: three chips, each under a DIFFERENT STACK of rulers:
  - Kingston: the textbook's sealed numbers + the maker's numbers read four times + our eyes + the hats.
  - Phoenix: the maker's numbers read once + our eyes from Run 1 on + the hats.
  - Pittsburgh: the maker's unseen calibration + a design cut for another chip + our unsealed expectations from the other two + blind bits + always last.
- No chip is free of rulers. They differ in WHICH rulers and HOW MANY. That is still three, and each can be read against the other two; but the reading is "more rulers / fewer rulers / fewest we could manage", not "predicted / unpredicted / blank".
