## 2. THE LOSS, IMPOSED, in the physics: three ways

Applied at both clock gaps (e^-1.10 = 0.3332, e^-1.83 = 0.1609), using the envelope-ratio-at-l=1000
operational definition (same smoothed-envelope machinery as the extrema method) so the three
routes are directly comparable:

**(a) Silk damping via ombh2.** Bisection-searched ombh2 in the physically plausible range
[0.010, 0.045] (baseline 0.02237) for the value giving envelope ratio = target at l~1000.
**FAILED for both gaps -- not bracketed.** Across that entire range the envelope ratio only spans
[0.911, 1.129] -- nowhere near 0.333 or 0.161. Silk damping's sensitivity to the baryon density
alone, over any physically sane range, cannot produce a loss anywhere near clock-gap size. This
knob is far too weak.

**(b) Reionization optical depth tau.** Bisection-searched tau in [0.02, 0.5] (baseline 0.0544)
for the same envelope-ratio target. **FAILED for both gaps -- not bracketed.** Range spans only
[0.412, 1.071] in envelope ratio; tau=0.5 (a wildly unphysical value, ~10x Planck's) only reaches
ratio 0.412, still short of 0.333, let alone 0.161. Reionization's damping effect on this envelope
metric is likewise far too weak to source a clock-gap-sized loss on its own.

**(c) Extra empirical envelope exp(-(l/l_x)^2).** This one is unconstrained by physics (l_x is
free), so it trivially hits the target ratio by construction: l_x=953.9 for e^-1.10, l_x=739.8 for
e^-1.83. But applying it: **chi2 to data blows up to 363,806 and 479,339 respectively (vs baseline
81.3)** -- a catastrophic fit degradation, ~4500-5900x worse. Ratio 2/1 collapses to 0.36 / 0.30
against measured 0.47. Exactly as the toy pass found: any loss of this size, imposed anywhere near
l~1000, destroys the peak structure Planck actually measured. This confirms the toy pass's finding
3 independently, now against the real best-fit spectrum instead of a toy fit.

## 3. THE INVERSE, in the physics

**Extra envelope route:** grid + refined search for the l_x that minimizes chi2 to data, starting
from the best fit. **The fit lands at the no-extra-loss boundary (l_x -> very large, i.e. no extra
envelope wanted at all).** Grid-best chi2 (81.35) equals the no-extra-loss chi2 (81.35) to within
numerical noise. Bootstrap (400 draws, resampling data within Planck's published errors): 369/400
draws hit that same boundary. **Fitted extra loss at l=1000: factor = 1.0000 +/- 0.0001, i.e.
gap_inverse_envelope = 0.0000 +/- 0.0001 series-taus.**

**Tau route:** grid + refined chi2 minimization over tau, starting from the best fit.
**tau_fit = 0.0533 +/- 0.0020** (error via delta-chi2=1 curvature rule, not a full per-draw CAMB
bootstrap -- logged as the cost-driven choice, each CAMB call is cheap (~0.08s) but a full
tau-refit bootstrap was skipped in favor of the standard curvature method). This is **0.53 sigma
from tau0=0.0544** -- fully consistent with no change. Converted to an extra-loss factor at l=1000
relative to tau0: factor = 1.0021, **gap_inverse_tau = -0.0021 +/- 0.0040 series-taus** (formally
negative -- i.e. very slightly less damping wanted, consistent with zero).

**Does the data want any extra loss beyond the standard model? No. Both real-physics inverse
routes land at 0.0 within error, not at 1.0-1.83, and not at the toy's 1.29.**

## 4. Plain words

With the real physics (CAMB's actual Silk damping, actual reionization suppression, actual
best-fit LCDM spectrum), **the sound at the beginning carries no extra loss at all beyond the
standard model** -- the data is fully explained by the textbook damping already built into LCDM,
with room left over of essentially zero, not room for a clock-gap-sized (1.10 or 1.83) extra loss
and not room for the toy's 1.29 either. Both independent inverse routes (an unconstrained extra
envelope, and the model's own physical tau parameter) land at 0.0 series-taus within tight errors
when fit to the real data starting from the real best fit.

**What killed the toy's 1.29:** the toy pass's own damped-oscillation fit (l_d=850, m=1.5) was
already a poor match to the real physics before any loss was applied -- its own l_d sat well
below the textbook range (l_d~1400-1500) and its own m sat well below m=2, so its chi2 (10,007
over 83 bins) was already ~123x worse than the real best-fit spectrum's chi2 (81.3) even before
touching the loss question. The toy's "inverse" of 1.29 was compensating for the toy's own
damping-length deficit relative to the textbook value, not detecting a real physical loss in the
CMB -- shrinking the toy's already-too-short l_d further just happened to nudge its badly-fit
ratios a bit closer to measured, in a narrow one-parameter family that had nowhere else to go.
Once the real physics (which already has the right l_d, right m, right everything) is used
instead, there is no leftover mismatch for an "extra loss" to fix, and the inverse correctly
reports 0.0. The toy's 1.29 was an artifact of the toy's own bad damping-length fit, not a hidden
signal in the sky.
