# THE CANON PASS -- the width printed beside every match, and the first withdrawal it produced

Bones, 8 October 2026. Reading only, no chip time. The pass flagged as Pi earlier the same day.

## The rule being applied

> **A match is only a match if the measurement is known tighter than the match.**

Written 8 Oct after finding that the binding constraint on our number-matches was never the look-elsewhere
burden but **the measurement's own width.** This is that rule turned on our own canon.

## What the canon can and cannot support

73 measurements in the Seven canon state a width. Sorted by how tightly each is known:

```
known tighter than  1% :  8 of 73
known tighter than  3% : 25 of 73
known tighter than 10% : 41 of 73
```

**Sixty-five of seventy-three cannot support a one percent match at all.** And of the eight that can, **three
are other people's neutron lifetimes** (878.4 +- 0.5 s, 887.7 +- 1.2, 877.2 +- 1.7). The remaining five are
ours, and all five belong to **one claim**: the spectator coherence ladder.

**So the canon contains exactly one of our own matches that this rule permits a verdict on.** Every other
number-match in it is laid beside a quantity we do not know to the tolerance we matched at. That is not a
finding against any particular claim; it is the state of the canon, and it is now written down.

## The one verdict available, and it is a withdrawal

The claim: a free-running spectator's coherence through successive looks reads `11/12, 10/12, 9/12`.

```
                claimed     measured            widths off    percent off
11/12           0.91667     0.921 +- 0.004        +1.08          +0.47%
10/12           0.83333     0.826 +- 0.005        -1.47          -0.88%
 9/12           0.75000     0.760 +- 0.006        +1.67          +1.33%
```

**Each value survives, loosely. But the signs alternate and the deviations grow** -- +0.47, -0.88, +1.33 --
which is the signature of a wrong functional form being fitted rather than of a relation.

**So judge the structure, which is what the claim actually was.** The defining feature of a ladder of
twelfths is **equal steps of 1/12**:

```
step 1 (11/12 -> 10/12)   claimed 0.08333   measured 0.0950 +- 0.0064   +1.82 widths
step 2 (10/12 ->  9/12)   claimed 0.08333   measured 0.0660 +- 0.0078   -2.22 widths
the two steps against each other           0.0290 +- 0.0101   +2.87 widths from equal
```

**The equal-step structure fails at about two widths in each direction, and the two steps differ from each
other by 2.9 widths. WITHDRAWN as a structure claim.** The three readings stand as readings; the ladder of
twelfths they were cited to demonstrate does not.

This is the standing order *judge a structure claim by the other ruler, not by its individual values*,
applied for the first time to this claim. Nobody had run it.

## And the alternative is not clean either -- three readings, honestly

```
equal RATIOS (a geometric decay):  0.8969 +- 0.0067  and  0.9201 +- 0.0092   ->  -2.05 widths apart
equal STEPS  (the twelfths ladder):                                          ->  +2.87 widths apart
```

- **NEITHER.** Both forms fail at about two widths. Three points cannot separate them, and a third form
  could accommodate both.
- **BOTH.** A geometric decay is locally near-linear over three points, so the two descriptions may be
  approximations of one thing, and the data cannot say which is the approximation.
- **MOVING.** With three points at widths of 0.4 to 0.8 percent the discrimination is not available; it
  moves with the number of looks measured. **A fourth and fifth point would decide it**, and that is a
  cheap, specific, pre-committable run.

**Less bad is not a fit, so nothing replaces the withdrawn structure. The sequence is unexplained, and it is
better to say so.**

## What this pass did not do

It read only measurements that **state a width**. Matches in the canon laid beside quantities quoted without
a width are not in the 73 and have not been judged. **Finding and widthing those is the remainder of this
pass, and it is unrun.**
