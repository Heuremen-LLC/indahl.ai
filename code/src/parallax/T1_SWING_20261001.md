# T1 SWING -- 2026-10-01, Bones's field hand

THE OBJECT (Wayfinder, Sec69b, correcting Sec69's "plain cause" framing): IBM's published T1
moves per qubit by 0.32x to 4.87x between its 9/26 and 9/28 calibration snapshots (median 0.96).
A clock that swings that much between photographs is the measurement that may not exist as
described. Treated here as the record, not explained away. No expectation written before
running. Script: `out/the_middle/t1_swing.py`. Raw output: `out/the_middle/t1_swing_results.json`
(full run log: available on request). numpy only, single process, 6.5s runtime.

**Data on disk, read verbatim, nothing recomputed that didn't need to be:** three IBM calibration
snapshots per chip (`out/ibm_cal_20260929/ibm_{marrakesh,kingston}/2026-09-{26,27,28}.json.gz`,
`out/cal_recluster/pulls/ibm_fez/2026-09-{26,27,28,29}.json.gz`), the 9/28 12:27Z echo run
(`out/echo_20260928_122733/`, ibm_marrakesh, 78 even qubits) for clocks A/B, the WIRE_MAP tile
carving (`out/the_buzz/heron.py`, asserted layout), and the Ramanujan 1/pi series from
`THE_MIDDLE_PI_20260930.md` (re-implemented with `Decimal`, same formula).

**Two different null devices used, named at every use, never conflated:**
- Sec 1 (group-membership clustering: tile8 vs rest, blinkers vs rest): an **exact (enumerated,
  not sampled) Mann-Whitney U null** -- the true permutation distribution of rank sums under
  label-exchangeability, computed by dynamic programming (Mann & Whitney 1947 recurrence). This
  is a closed-form combinatorial fraction, not a Monte Carlo shuffle.
- Sec 2 (does clock X predict clock Y): the **qubit-label-reversal null** per
  `CLOCK_EXISTENCE_20261001.md`'s standing rule -- X kept qubit-ascending, Y re-paired
  qubit-descending, one deterministic re-pairing, never a shuffle.

Position (Sec 1d) uses the reversed-label null only (a Spearman rho is not a group-membership
test, so the exact MW device doesn't apply there).

## 2. PLACED vs ARISEN ON THE CALIBRATION

Clock A (arisen carry, ~64us) resolves 51/74 readable qubits at 9/28 12:27Z; clock B (look-heat
drain, ~80us, cutoff 0.50) resolves 22/74.

**Sign of the swing (up vs down), exact MW:** A: n_up=20 (mean A=153.2us), n_down=31 (mean
A=108.9us), U=263, exact p=0.373 -- UNRESOLVED, no sign prediction. B: n_up=8 (mean B=92.1us),
n_down=14 (mean B=400.2us), U=52, exact p=0.815 -- UNRESOLVED, no sign prediction, despite the
large raw mean gap (noise, not signal, at n=22).

**Size of the swing (\|log r\|), OLS + bootstrap + reversed null:** A -> \|log r_T1\|: real
R^2=0.034, boot err=0.059 -- **DOES NOT EXIST** (real R^2 doesn't clear its own noise). B ->
\|log r_T1\|: real R^2=0.048, boot err=0.073 -- **DOES NOT EXIST**. Same verdict for T2 and
readout error (both clocks, both quantities): every A/B -> size cell fails at the first bar
(real R^2 inside its own bootstrap error) before the reversed null is even needed.

**Does the published clock predict itself?** T1(9/26) -> T1(9/28): real R^2=0.362, boot
err=0.072, reversed null R^2=0.034 -- **EXISTS** (rho=0.591). The published clock has real
day-to-day persistence of its own.

**Does the inside clock beat that persistence?** A -> T1(28): real R^2=0.013, boot err=0.033,
reversed null R^2=0.107 (the reversed null is *larger* than the real fit) -- **DOES NOT EXIST**.
B -> T1(28): real R^2=0.0017 -- **DOES NOT EXIST**. T1(26) alone (R^2=0.362) predicts T1(28) more
than ten times better than either inside clock does. **Placed beats arisen, by a wide margin, for
predicting the published clock's own next value** -- the swing is not explained by the chip's own
microsecond-scale behavior at echo time; it is better explained by the published clock's own
history, imperfectly (R^2=0.36, not 1.0 -- it persists, it does not repeat).

## 3. THE PINATA IN THE MIDDLE

**Series profile (exact, Decimal, k=0..8):** rel_e_K runs from 2.43e-8 (k=0) to 7.3e-64 (k=8) --
unchanged from `THE_MIDDLE_PI_20260930.md`'s table, reproduced here as the reference ruler.

**Landing index, per qubit, \|log r_T1\| mapped onto rel_e_K:** n=144 qubits, landing index = 0
for every single one (min=0, max=0, all_zero=True). **Named, not smoothed over: every per-qubit
T1 swing (smallest observed \|log r\| = 0, i.e. qubits with r essentially exactly 1) is still
being compared against a series whose very first term (k=0) already lands pi to relative error
2.4e-8 -- six to eight orders of magnitude tighter than the swing's own smallest value and vastly
tighter than its largest (\|log r\|~1.6).** The series lands immediately, before any term beyond
k=0 is needed, for the entire qubit set. This is a real finding about scale mismatch, not a null
result to discard: the chip's day-to-day calibration drift lives at a resolution the 1/pi
instrument blows past in its very first term. The landing-index test as specified cannot
discriminate big swingers from small ones here -- it discriminates nothing, because nothing on
this chip's record converges anywhere near as fast as the series does.

**Ratio-of-ratios, where a third real measurement exists:** checked directly (not assumed) --
marrakesh's 9/26 and 9/27 T1 are bit-identical (frozen, Sec69), so marrakesh gives only **two**
real T1 points (9/26=27, 9/28) and is reported as a row, not computed, per instruction. Kingston
and Fez each have **three distinct real T1 measurements** (26, 27, 28 all differ, checked
qubit-by-qubit): ratio-of-ratios RR = r2/r1 where r1=T1(27)/T1(26), r2=T1(28)/T1(27). Kingston:
n=155, median RR=1.091, range 0.119-83.17, std=6.71 (one qubit's RR blows out to 83x -- an outlier
driving the std, median is the honest center). Fez: n=156, median RR=1.000, range 0.093-9.56,
std=1.14. **Both chips' median RR sits near 1** -- the swing from day 1->2 and day 2->3 is, on
median, the same size and same direction character -- but both have long tails of qubits where
the second-hop swing is many times the first-hop swing (not a smooth decay, consistent with
Sec67's "too short for a decay" framing).

## Plain words

The published clock (T1) moves by a median factor of 0.96 and a range of 0.32x-4.87x between
back-to-back daily snapshots, on all three chips checked, landing within about 10% of each other
in typical swing size -- this is not one chip's quirk, it is roughly how hard IBM's own number
wanders, chip to chip. The chip's own inside clocks (the ~64us clustering clock and the ~80us
look-heat drain, both measured in the same hour as the shots) do not know which way the published
clock will move next, and do not know how far it will move -- every A/B -> swing-size and
A/B -> sign test failed to clear its own noise. What DOES predict the published clock's next
value, weakly but really, is the published clock's own PREVIOUS value (R^2=0.36) -- more than ten
times better than either inside clock manages. The swing lands nowhere graded in the 1/pi
reference profile: it is so much larger than the series' own first-term error that the comparison
is immediate and total, a genuine finding about mismatched scales rather than a convergence curve
to read off. One tile (8, the buzz's seat) stands out, but in the opposite direction expected --
it swings LESS than the rest of the chip on T1 specifically (exact p=0.010, uncorrected for the
48 tile tests run), not more.
