## 1. THE 212 Hz ARROW AT n=800

Third-order autocovariance A(k)=E[x_t^2 x_{t+k}]-E[x_t x_{t+k}^2], k=1..10, all 4 lines,
1000-shuffle floor + 1000-case bootstrap per cell (`core.arrow_shuffle_floor_fast`,
`arrow_bootstrap_fast`), time-reversed series beside every cell.

| line | k=5 A | boot err | floor mean+/-sd | z | p (normal approx) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **212** | **+44.65** | 24.36 | +0.68 +/- 14.24 | **3.09** | **0.00202** |
| 200 | n/a (lag5 not flagged; z range -1.1..+0.3 across k=1-10) | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 300 | n/a (max |z| 1.4 at k=9, not lag5) | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| 12 | n/a (max |z| 1.1, no lag5 flag) | -- | -- | -- | -- |

**212 Hz lag-5 is still the only flagged cell at n=800** (40 cells scanned, Bonferroni
0.05/40=0.00125 -- the two-sided normal-approx p=0.00202 does NOT clear Bonferroni, unlike
DO_BETTER's n=398 reading, which did). A **10,000-shuffle recheck** on this one cell:
A=44.65, floor=+0.034+/-14.40, **25/10,000 draws (p=0.0025)**, z=3.10 -- confirms the signal
is real and not a 1000-shuffle fluke, but it has **weakened from n=398's +81.6 (floor
0+/-23, p=0.0004) to n=800's +44.65 (p=0.0025)**: roughly half the magnitude, moved from
clearing Bonferroni to not clearing it. **Every cell's reversed-series A flips sign exactly**
(algebraic check |A_fwd + A_rev| < 3e-14 on all 40 cells, floating-point zero) -- this is
`arrow_stat`'s own antisymmetry under time-reversal (b<->a swap), confirmed, not new physics.

**Tracking test** (sliding windows, width=133 rows/~2.2h, step=66, n=11 windows): lag-5 A on
212 Hz correlates with 212's own sliding-window mean level at **r=-0.314 +/- 0.267** (bootstrap
sd) and with mains proxies at r=+0.365+/-0.309 (60 Hz) / r=-0.426+/-0.251 (120 Hz). **None of
the three clears its own error bar** -- at n=11 windows this question is unresolved either
way, not a null in the strong sense, just underpowered. Read plainly: the lag-5 anomaly is
real and shrinking with more data (consistent with a finite-sample fluctuation riding on top
of a smaller or zero true effect, though not yet proven zero), and nothing on file at n=800
says whether it rides the line's own level or the mains.

## 4. Plain words (seven, beside the six)

1. The lag-5 212 Hz asymmetry is real at n=800 (10,000-shuffle p=0.0025) but has **halved**
   since n=398 and no longer clears Bonferroni across the 40-cell scan.
2. It is strongest in the first and middle thirds of the record (k=5 both times, same sign)
   and gone by the last third (k=1 wins instead) -- fading, not stable.
3. Nothing on file at n=800 says whether it tracks 212's own level or the 60/120 Hz mains;
   both correlations sit inside their own error bars at this window count (n=11).
4. Every arrow cell flips sign exactly under time reversal, on all 4 lines, all 3 windows,
   all 10 lags -- the ruler's own antisymmetry, confirmed again, not new.
5. The clock is wild and unconstrained on this record's short windows (errors up to 500x the
   point estimate) -- same disease as every other thin-window record on file.
6. In series-taus, copper's arrow and split resolve together (ratio ~0.98-0.99) exactly like
   yeast's and horn's did; its clock resolves with nothing, the fifth confirmation of that
   pattern.
7. Copper is the shortest record of the five GAP_PER_MEDIUM substrates and also has the
   lowest G_s -- the record-length ordering holds exactly with a fifth point, though five
   points still cannot make that statistically tight on their own.
