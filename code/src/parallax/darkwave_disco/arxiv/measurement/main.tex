\begin{abstract}
On three IBM Heron processors, a qubit prepared in \(|0\rangle\) and given ten X gates separated by idle intervals ends with more \(|1\rangle\) population than one-exponential decay bookkeeping allows: +1.3 to +3.3\% at 30-\textmu s intervals, +5.8\% at 5\,\textmu s, about zero at 150\,\textmu s; a qubit prepared in \(|1\rangle\) moves about as much the other way. The excess survives controls for excitation, leakage (at most 0.26 \(\pm\) 0.12\% per gate) and shot-to-shot carry-over, but not the bookkeeping: against each qubit's own decay curve in the same job it lies between \(-\)1.8\% and +0.3\% at intervals of 20\,\textmu s and longer. We report it as accounted for by non-exponential decay; at 5-10\,\textmu s, where our curve is interpolated, up to +3.4\% remains. Against each interval's own decay it falls below zero, deepening by \(-\)0.0011 to \(-\)0.0020 per gate on three chips. We time one readout three ways: spectators read it at 1.02-1.07 times the vendor's stated duration on three chips, and at 0.97-1.01 of a readout tone we lay ourselves on a Rigetti processor. On Rigetti, where \(|2\rangle\) is driven and read, the same train with every qubit driven exceeds one-exponential bookkeeping by +2.5 to +12.7\%, while ten back-to-back gates alone move \(|0\rangle\) by +7.6 to +9.0\%, and that gate error rises from about 6\% to about 10\% when the neighbours are held on the equator. The spectators' state at readout scales a neighbour's \(|2\rangle\) reading by 0.81-0.89 at every delay with the decay shape unchanged; the reverse effect is 1-3 degrees. A neighbour in \(|2\rangle\) pulls a spectator by \(-\)62 deg/us (28 September) and \(-\)35 to \(-\)41 deg/us (4 October), and on both vendors a minority of qubits carry \(|2\rangle\) population in three behaviours. Twenty-four calls written and hashed before their data are scored with misses included, beside the rate chance alone would give: four land, one meets its threshold inside its width, thirteen miss.

(Sources for every abstract number are in Sections 3.1-3.5.)

\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
The vendor of a superconducting processor publishes per-qubit numbers -- a measurement duration, relaxation times, couplings -- and experiments usually time their events with those numbers and book-keep their populations with one exponential decay per qubit. Here we report what happened when we did that and then tested the bookkeeping.

\textbf{The main result is a negative one.} A qubit prepared in \(|0\rangle\) and given ten X gates separated by idle intervals ends with a few percent more \(|1\rangle\) population than one-exponential decay bookkeeping allows, on three chips, in sixteen jobs over two days. It passes the controls for excitation, leakage and shot-to-shot carry-over listed in Section 3.2. It disappears when the bookkeeping uses each qubit's own decay curve measured in the same job: the decay right after preparation is faster than one exponential, and the excess is the size of that difference. We lead with it because it is a worked example of a few-percent excess that every other control we ran left standing, and because the control that removes it costs a handful of extra circuits in the same job.

Alongside it we report five measurements made with the same harness:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The look.} One readout timed by three clocks: the vendor's stated duration; spectators evolving across a block of readouts on other qubits; and the decay the measured qubit shows per readout. On one chip two analysis methods give ratios 4-13\% apart; we give both (Section 3.2).
\item \textbf{The park.} Whether a qubit's decay advances while it sits in \(|0\rangle\) (Section 3.3).
\item \textbf{The witness.} Ramsey spectators reading a neighbour's \(|1\rangle\) time; free spectators read through repeated readouts of other qubits at one, two and three or more links; and on Rigetti a neighbour in \(|2\rangle\) (Section 3.4).
\item \textbf{The third level.} Per-qubit \(|2\rangle\) populations at the start of a shot on two vendors, in three behaviours (Section 3.5).
\item \textbf{The second vendor, 4 October.} The push train with every qubit of Rigetti Cepheus driven, the same train on the \(|1\rangle\)-\(|2\rangle\) pair read by spectators, the spectators' \(|2\rangle\) ruler measured round to round, and the readout cross-talk between spectators and emitters in both directions (Section 3.6).
\end{enumerate}
No new physics is claimed. Comparisons are made within a single job unless stated. No numerical predictions were written before these runs; the run headers list outcome categories only {[}code/push.py; code/noname.py; code/hold.py; code/looktime.py; code/giveback.py; code/witness.py{]}, and the 4 October morning and afternoon runs carry none {[}code\_20261004/time\_wide.py; code\_20261004/look\_wide.py; code\_20261004/count\_wide.py{]}, so those tests are post hoc. From the afternoon of 4 October, numbers were written and hashed before the data existed; Section 3.7 lists every one with its score, misses included, and the rate at which chance alone would score. Relaxation times of superconducting qubits wander in time through coupling to two-level-system (TLS) defects {[}4-8{]}, so a decay curve gathered over a job is a mixture of exponentials; the bookkeeping control of Section 3.1 is a within-job case of that. Static ZZ coupling between fixed-frequency transmons shifts a qubit's frequency with its neighbour's state {[}9, 10{]}, the shift the Ramsey spectators of Section 3.4 read. Leakage out of the two lowest levels and its removal {[}11{]}, measurement-induced state transitions {[}12{]}, cross-talk in multiplexed readout {[}13{]} and active reset {[}1{]} bound the controls of Sections 3.1-3.6. Krantz et al. review the device physics {[}14{]}.

\textbf{Terms.} \emph{Level}: the population estimate of Section 2.2 (0 at the \(|0\rangle\) reference, 1 at the \(|1\rangle\) reference). \emph{Readout}: one measure instruction. \emph{X gate} (``push''): the native x gate. \emph{Halfway state}: the state made from \(|0\rangle\) by one sx gate. \emph{Emitter}: a qubit whose state a spectator reads. \emph{Spectator} (``witness''): a neighbour of an emitter run as a Ramsey clock. \emph{Far qubit}: a qubit wired to no emitter. \emph{Interval}: a scheduled delay. \emph{Median qubit}: the median over qubits (the ledger's ``middle bit''). \emph{Driven spectator}: a spectator whose emitter is held in \(|1\rangle\). \emph{Free spectator}: a qubit held on the equator with no emitter driven (4 October).

\subsection{Readout and state estimation}
Readout is kerneled: one complex I/Q value per qubit per shot {[}code/common.py{]}. Per qubit and job, the means c0 and c1 of lone readouts of \(|0\rangle\) and \(|1\rangle\) (prepared and read at no delay, in the same job) define the \(|0\rangle\)-\(|1\rangle\) line. Each shot's value x is projected onto it, level = Re{[}(x \(-\) c0)(c1 \(-\) c0)*{]} / |c1 \(-\) c0|\(^2\), so the \(|0\rangle\) reference reads 0 and the \(|1\rangle\) reference 1 {[}code/readclose.py; code/readnoname.py{]}. The mean level over shots is the population estimate. A \(|2\rangle\) population reads at an unknown position x2 along the line.

Three further readings are kept from the same shots {[}code/readnoname.py; \S{}144, \S{}147{]}: the perpendicular component (``across the line''); the fraction of shots with level between 0.3 and 0.7 (``halfway band''); and the fraction more than three cloud widths from both cloud centres (``off both clouds''). The last two are reported minus what a mixture of the two reference clouds at the same level gives. Cloud width is sqrt(\textless{}|x \(-\) c0|\(^2\)\textgreater{}/2) of the \(|0\rangle\) reference; a qubit is \emph{readable} when its cloud separation exceeds 4 widths (3 in the jump run) {[}code/readclose.py; code/readnoname.py; reads/jump\_run.txt{]}. Some readers report median-qubit values over all qubits instead; Table 1 says which.

\subsection{Settings}
Every IBM job pins its sampler options: the vendor's qubit initialization at the start of each shot (init\_qubits), kerneled readout, a fixed 500\,\textmu s repetition delay, dynamical decoupling off, gate and measurement twirling off; SamplerV2 0.49 has no measurement-mitigation option {[}code/common.py{]}. A run refuses a chip whose configuration does not enable a dynamic repetition rate or whose repetition-delay range excludes 500\,\textmu s {[}code/common.py{]}. Circuits use native x, sx, rz, delay and measure, with barriers in the spectator runs {[}code/push.py; code/looktime.py; code/noname.py{]}. Delays are laid in whole multiples of 16 dt (64 ns at dt = 4 ns) {[}code/common.py; code/push.py{]}. The order of circuits in each job is permuted by a seed written per job {[}code/common.py; code/push.py{]}. Whole-chip runs drive and read every qubit in every circuit, with no selection before the run {[}code/push.py; code/pushrep.py; code/noname.py{]}. The nine-qubit runs (runs 5, 7, 9) used nine qubits drawn by a written seed, in three groups of three {[}code/common.py; \S{}114, \S{}116{]}.

\subsection{Spectators}
Emitters are taken in an order permuted by a written seed (20261810), each accepted only if it is at least three links from every earlier emitter; spectators are the qubits wired to an emitter; the rest are far qubits {[}code/witness.py; code/looktime.py; code/noname.py{]}. On ibm\_pittsburgh's coupling map this gives 39 emitters, 86 spectators and 31 far qubits {[}reads/witness\_run.txt; reads/looktime\_roles.json{]}; the same seed gives the same roles in the readout-timing and interval-length jobs {[}\S{}145; code/noname.py{]}. A spectator is put on the equator with sx and read at the end on two axes a quarter turn apart (sx, or rz(\(\pi\)/2) then sx); its hand is H = (2 L\_X \(-\) 1) \(-\) i(2 L\_Y \(-\) 1) from the two levels, with contrast |H| and phase arg H {[}code/readwitness.py; code/readclose.py{]}. In the interval-length job each spectator also receives a Y-axis \(\pi\) pulse, rz(\(-\)\(\pi\)/2) x rz(\(\pi\)/2), at every instant its emitter receives an X gate, so the spectator's own frame rotation cancels over each pair of intervals while its emitter's \(|1\rangle\) time accumulates (``frame-held'') {[}code/noname.py; \S{}145{]}. In the 4 October readout-timing runs the same seed and rule are applied to each chip's coupling map read at fire, giving 39 emitters, 86 spectators and 31 far qubits on all three chips {[}reads\_20261004/lookwide\_roles\_ibm\_pittsburgh.json; code\_20261004/look\_wide.py{]}. In the free-spectator runs no emitter is driven: every qubit that is not a far qubit is put on the equator and read on two axes after the block, and is labelled afterwards by its number of links to the nearest far qubit {[}code\_20261004/count\_wide.py; code\_20261004/readwitness\_wide.py{]}.

\subsection{Decay bookkeeping}
Population after a train is propagated as a two-state chain {[}code/readclose.py; code/readhold.py; code/readnoname.py{]}: during an interval of length L the \(|1\rangle\) population survives with probability q\_L and the rest goes to \(|0\rangle\); \(|0\rangle\) does not change; each X gate swaps the two. The start is 0 (\(|0\rangle\)), 1 (\(|1\rangle\)), or the measured level of the halfway state. Two choices of q\_L, both per qubit and from the same job:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{one-exponential}: q\_L = P(300\,\textmu s)\^{}(L/300), with P(300\,\textmu s) the level of the qubit prepared in \(|1\rangle\) and left 300\,\textmu s {[}code/readclose.py; code/readhold.py; code/readnoname.py{]};
\item \textbf{each interval's own}: q\_L = P(L), the level of the qubit prepared in \(|1\rangle\) and left L = 10, 30 or 90\,\textmu s, measured in the same job (interval-length job only) {[}code/readnoname.py{]};
\item \textbf{curve} (4 October): q\_L read off the same job's \(|1\rangle\)-idle levels at 0, 30, 100, 300 and 600\,\textmu s, log-linear between points; each of the three copies of those arms gives its own curve and the tables average the three {[}code\_20261004/readtime.py{]}. Below 30\,\textmu s this curve is an interpolation, not a measurement.
\end{itemize}
The bookkeeping carries no gate error; back-to-back trains (no delay between X gates) measure gate error separately. \emph{Excess} = measured level \(-\) bookkeeping.

Hold-run ``arm minus nothing'' widths are the spread over the three jobs divided by \(\surd\)3 {[}code/readhold.py{]}.

\textbf{Controls.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Start-independent maps.} Relaxation toward a thermal population and an imperfect X gate act on the population as affine maps that do not depend on the start; they move all three starts alike and cannot produce an excess on start 0 alone. A check at an equilibrium population of 0.026 and per-interval survival 0.89 gives \(-\)0.104\% {[}\S{}146{]}. The same holds for any map independent of the start, such as constant leakage or a spectator-shifted pulse error {[}\S{}147{]}.
\item \emph{Excitation during \(|0\rangle\) intervals.} Every push job carries its own 300-\textmu s \(|0\rangle\)-idle arm (Table 2). In the interval-length job, which holds the one remaining arm below, it reads +0.0002 \(\pm\) 0.0015, capping excitation over the full 300\,\textmu s at \(\sim\)0.3\% (2\(\sigma\)) {[}reads/noname\_read1.txt; \S{}176{]} (one job). Per 300\,\textmu s: ibm\_pittsburgh \(-\)0.0001 \(\pm\) 0.0006, ibm\_kingston +0.0026 \(\pm\) 0.0014, ibm\_marrakesh \(-\)0.0043 \(\pm\) 0.0013 {[}reads/close\_push.txt; reads/rep\_kingston\_read1.txt; reads/rep\_marrakesh\_read1.txt{]}, against excesses of +0.0200, +0.0287 and +0.0274.
\item \emph{Leakage to \(|2\rangle\).} (a) The halfway band, where \(|2\rangle\) at x2 = 0.5 would read, is flat across arms: start-0 spread train +0.09\% (all readable qubits; +0.14\% on the 22-28 qubits with clouds more than 8 widths apart), hold run \(-\)0.00\% / +0.13\%, start-1 spread +0.07\%, halfway spread +0.08\%, idle in 1 +0.09\% {[}\S{}147, \S{}148{]}; a \(|2\rangle\) population carrying the +2.0\% at x2 = 0.5 would put \(\sim\)4\% of shots there {[}\S{}147{]}. On ibm\_marrakesh / ibm\_kingston the band reads +0.0004 / +0.0034 (start-0 spread) against +0.0007 / +0.0032 (start-1 spread) and +0.0010 / +0.0050 (halfway) {[}reads/rep\_marrakesh\_read1.txt; reads/rep\_kingston\_read1.txt{]}. (b) Across the line, start-0 spread arm: \(-\)0.0001 \(\pm\) 0.0005 (push run), +0.0001 \(\pm\) 0.0006 (hold run) {[}reads/discarded\_push.txt; reads/discarded\_hold.txt{]}. (c) Spread across the line beyond the two-cloud mixture, +0.0003 \(\pm\) 0.0001 for start-0 and start-1 spread arms alike; off both clouds +0.0011 \(\pm\) 0.0003 (push run) and +0.0013 \(\pm\) 0.0004 (hold run), with every arm that spent time or took gates at +0.0005 to +0.0012 {[}reads/discarded\_push.txt; reads/discarded\_hold.txt; \S{}144{]}. (d) The back-to-back cap, 0.26 \(\pm\) 0.12\% per gate (0.49\% at two widths) {[}\S{}147{]}; x2 cancels from this cap to first order when the \(|2\rangle\) population left after the train is the same for starts 0 and 1 {[}\S{}148{]}. (e) The extra-X identity (interval-length job): \(-\)0.0006 \(\pm\) 0.0043 (start 0) and \(-\)0.0007 \(\pm\) 0.0040 (start 1) {[}reads/noname\_read1.txt{]}; it is blind at x2 = 0.5 {[}\S{}147{]}, where (a) reads.
\item \emph{Shot-to-shot carry-over.} In recorded shot order on ibm\_pittsburgh the next shot of the start-0 spread arm ends in \(|1\rangle\) no more often after a \(|1\rangle\) ending than after a \(|0\rangle\) ending: \(-\)0.002 \(\pm\) 0.002 over three jobs, where a carry-over making the +2.0\% would read \(\sim\)+0.05; lags 2 and 5 the same; circuit to circuit at the same shot number, no more than an unrelated circuit; in one job (run 92) the start-0 spread arm followed the \(|0\rangle\)-idle arm, whose shots almost never end in \(|1\rangle\), and its excess was +0.0213 {[}\S{}151{]}. A bound without a model (next-shot lean after a \(|1\rangle\) ending \(\times\) share of shots ending in \(|1\rangle\)): ibm\_marrakesh +0.0108 \(\times\) 0.39 = +0.004 of +0.027 {[}\S{}152b; reads/rep\_marrakesh\_read1.txt{]}; ibm\_kingston at most +0.0011 {[}reads/rep\_kingston\_read1.txt{]}. On Rigetti, where carry-over through \(|2\rangle\) exists, the same kind of test finds it (Section 3.5) {[}\S{}18, \S{}154{]}.
\item \emph{Spread of decay times across qubits} moves all three starts alike (+0.005 to +0.013) {[}\S{}139{]}. A single-qubit model of the gate shortfall with plain decay, dephasing and frame detuning gives equal and opposite start-0 / start-1 shifts and a negative halfway shift {[}\S{}139{]}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The third level}
On the IBM path used here (native gates, standard measure) \(|2\rangle\) is not read directly; ibm\_pittsburgh's configuration lists measure\_2 and reset\_2 among supported instructions {[}reads/pittsburgh\_config\_timing.json{]}, which were not used. On Rigetti with pulse access the \(|1\rangle\)-\(|2\rangle\) transition is driven and \(|2\rangle\) is read {[}\S{}5, \S{}154{]}. On both vendors a minority of qubits carry a \(|2\rangle\) population at the start of a shot, in three behaviours {[}\S{}5, \S{}6, \S{}154{]}: \textbf{standing} (present at every inter-shot gap), \textbf{carried} (raised by a readout, carried into the next shot when shots are close, then draining), and \textbf{blinking} (switching on and off between batches minutes apart while the ordinary readout stays flat). Each has a textbook description: steady \(|2\rangle\) occupancy; measurement-induced leakage that a \(|0\rangle\)/\(|1\rangle\) reset cannot clear; \(|2\rangle\) population or transition-frequency switching {[}\S{}6{]}.

\textbf{Rigetti Cepheus-1-108Q (2026-09-25 to 09-28)} {[}\S{}5, \S{}6, \S{}9, \S{}18{]}.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Baseline}: q0's \(|2\rangle\) population at the start of a shot is 0.03 \(\pm\) 0.17\% alone and 0.29 \(\pm\) 0.19\% with the whole chip run in parallel; an entangling gate leaves +0.05 \(\pm\) 0.39\% on q0's \(|2\rangle\) {[}\S{}5{]}.
\item \emph{Standing}: on 56 qubits read without ambiguity the median is 0.04\%; q24 2.05\%, q48 1.34\%, q40 1.26\%, q33 1.17\% {[}\S{}5{]}. q33 has the shortest lifetime on the chip (0.7\,\textmu s) and q24 the seventh shortest; q24 and q33 are neighbours {[}\S{}5, \S{}6{]}. Worse readout goes with more \(|2\rangle\): \(\rho\) = \(-\)0.31 (p = 0.02, 56 qubits), \(-\)0.55 (81 qubits) before the ``mirror check'' {[}\S{}5{]}. The two variables are each qubit's \(|2\rangle\) population at the start of a shot (from a fit to eight flip/swap sequences) and its calibrated readout fidelity, as a Spearman correlation. The mirror check refits each qubit's eight sequences from 24 starting points, on both sides of the trade between \(|2\rangle\) population and the chance that a \(|2\rangle\) qubit reads 0, and counts a qubit as ambiguous when equally good fits (cost within 1.0 of the best) span more than 0.5 percentage points of \(|2\rangle\) population: 25 of the 81 clean-fit qubits are ambiguous {[}code/mirror\_check.py; reads/mirror\_check.json{]}. On 25 qubits readout blur and \(|2\rangle\) could not be separated {[}\S{}5{]}. On three qubits (q67, q76, q85; q76 reads 1 on every shot) \(|2\rangle\) could not be reached or read; they lie on a straight line of neighbours {[}\S{}5, \S{}6{]}.
\item \emph{Carried}: q63 about 2.5\%, q78 about 1.9\%, q40 about 2.0\%, draining within 60\,\textmu s in all three batches; q61's drain in the first batch did not repeat {[}\S{}5{]}.
\item \emph{Blinking}: \(|2\rangle\) read in three batches minutes apart: q52 2.0 / 2.2 / 8.9\%, q36 8.5 / 9.9 / 18.9\%, q5 1.7 / 5.3 / 1.7\% {[}\S{}5{]}; these qubits show no shot-to-shot memory {[}\S{}6{]}; in a following hour of eight checks they did not blink (q52 one excursion) {[}\S{}9{]}. The \(|1\rangle\)-\(|2\rangle\) frequency (Ramsey at 1.5\,\textmu s) on 31 qubits with readable contrast jumped rarely and independently, about once per 7.5-minute interval, with no chip-wide simultaneous jump; two qubits hopped among a few levels held about 15 minutes with their ordinary readout flat {[}\S{}9{]}.
\item \emph{Carry-over through reset}: on q0 the reset clears \(|1\rangle\) but sometimes leaves \(|2\rangle\), and the \(|2\rangle\) left by one shot sets how the next shot starts (p = 0.001; about 0.01 after the splits examined) {[}\S{}18{]}. An active reset made of a measurement and a conditional flip does not act on \(|2\rangle\) {[}1; \S{}20{]}. Section 3.1 uses this as a positive control for carry-over.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What chance would score (the stone test).} The framework holds about two dozen ratios between 0.25 and 3.5 and a seven-step ladder {[}code\_20261004/stone\_density.py{]}. A value drawn evenly on a log scale between 0.5 and 2 lies within 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 \% of at least one of the 24 ratios 20 / 35 / 48 / 72 \% of the time; between 0.25 and 3.5, 15 / 27 / 37 / 56 \%. A value between 0.4 and 9 \% lies within 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 \% of a ladder step 4 / 9 / 13 / 23 \% of the time {[}reads\_20261004/stone\_density\_20261004.txt{]}. Because a match chosen after the fact from that many numbers is cheap, this paper names none; a call names one number before the data. For one named number, a value drawn evenly on a log scale over a factor of two either side lands within \(\pm\) w with chance ln((1 + w)/(1 \(-\) w)) / ln 4: 4.3 \% for w = 3 \%, 25 \% for the \(\pm\) 17 \% band of the one call that landed. Whether the calls taken together exceed chance is not claimed: the framework is probabilistic by construction, the calls' widths differ from one another (each is printed with its row), and the choice of null distribution is left to the reader. One worked example, under the log-uniform null above and with every assumption labelled, is in the release {[}reads\_20261005/CHANCE\_TABLE\_20261005.md{]}.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Replication depth.} The push train with intervals swept and the decay read in the same jobs has three jobs on each of ibm\_marrakesh and ibm\_kingston and one on ibm\_pittsburgh (4 October). The interval-length job with own-interval arms (2 October) is one of three planned (runs 122-123 frozen, not fired) {[}\S{}157{]}, and the spectator stopwatch one of three {[}reads/witness\_run.txt{]}. The 4 October readout-timing run has one job on ibm\_pittsburgh, and the free-spectator run none there. Every own-interval number of 2 October and every frame-held spectator number rests on one job.
\item \textbf{Comparisons within jobs only.} Between jobs minutes apart, single-qubit decay times and levels move by more than the shot widths (Section 3.1) {[}\S{}117, \S{}119{]}; the same circuit read +0.0200 \(\pm\) 0.0016 (push run) and +0.0126 \(\pm\) 0.0032 (interval-length job) against one-exponential bookkeeping {[}reads/close\_push.txt; reads/noname\_read1.txt{]}. Quoted widths are shot-resampling widths; three-job widths assume independent jobs and do not include this wander. The spectator-timed readout duration and the single-readout cost at the end of a 300-\textmu s idle scatter between jobs beyond their widths (Section 3.2).
\item \textbf{Statistic.} Means over readable qubits and median-qubit values differ in places (Section 3.3; Table 2, hold run). No qubit is removed beyond the readable-cloud rule, so qubits with decay times of about 6 to 55\,\textmu s (q31, q36, q57, q66, q90) and one that reads below its \(|0\rangle\) reference at 300\,\textmu s (q137) are inside the means {[}\S{}121; reads/tail\_run.txt{]}.
\item \textbf{Not measured or not controlled.} (a) T1 shot by shot within a job: the non-exponential account rests on the decay shape within jobs and on decay-time wander between jobs. (b) \(|2\rangle\) on IBM: not read directly on the path used; bounded indirectly (Section 3.1). A push train with a direct \(|2\rangle\) read on Rigetti was designed and not run {[}\S{}154{]}. (c) Pulse shapes: native gates only; an X gate built from two half-gates, the nearest available way to change leakage without changing the operation, was not run on the push train {[}\S{}146, \S{}147{]}. (d) What the vendor schedules during a readout: ibm\_pittsburgh's configuration places all 156 qubits in one measurement group {[}reads/pittsburgh\_config\_timing.json{]}; whether readout tones reach unmeasured qubits during a readout block, and what fills the difference between the stated and the spectator-timed duration, were not measured. (e) The halfway start's offset and ibm\_kingston's start-1 column (Section 3.1).
\item \textbf{Post hoc.} No numerical predictions were written before the runs of Sections 3.1-3.6, except the calls of Section 3.7. The 7.5\% family-wise figure covers the ten arms of one job. These jobs are part of 72 IBM jobs fired on five chips over 2026-10-01/02 (71 landed) {[}\S{}141{]}, after exploratory analyses that counted about 3.44 million tests across 98 analyses through 2026-09-29 {[}\S{}29{]}; no program-wide correction is applied.
\item \textbf{Method dependence.} On ibm\_pittsburgh two analysis methods give readout-timing ratios 4-13\% apart for the same readout (Section 3.2); both are given, and no ratio here is offered as a match to any constant.
\item \textbf{Rigetti, one day.} Section 3.6 rests on one day of 680 tasks on one chip; the spectators' \(|2\rangle\) ruler moves 30-50\% between rounds at a single delay, and the emitters' readout depends on the other qubits' state at readout (Table 16).
