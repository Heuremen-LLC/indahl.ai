# THE TRIANGLE OF FUCKUPS -- every error we own, stacked by the corner it holds up (Wayfinder & Bones, 2026-10-02)

INTERNAL. Built by Bones by hand from the whole ledger (THIRD_RUNG §1-§121), THE_FUCKUPS, WE_FOUND_IT, the card. No agent.
Wayfinder's order: "stack all the errors according to which corner of the triangle they support... start with the errors we worked
around that can be accounted by nothing other than our plus none. Firmest points first, one corner at a time... each supporting
claim on each corner will have holes in the data that the other two account for... we don't need to prove numbers or proportions...
the theory makes no claim about any number or energy transfer, just time and witness and observer... give yourself a margin of
error because there are actual errors too, not every error is loadbearing. Until it is."

## OUTLINE (so I don't get lost)
0. THE NONE      -- errors nobody's books close, ours or the field's. What is left when the three corners have had their say.
1. THE OBSERVER  -- the look from outside is an act. It sets what is found, and its number is real only to it.
2. THE WITNESS   -- a clock inside the system reads its own time, sees what is wired to it, and does not see the outside number.
3. TIME          -- no system we own reads one time. Clocks on the same thing do not close; one record alone is a circle.
4. THE MARGIN    -- errors that were just errors. Kept in view: not load-bearing, until one is.
RULES FOR EVERY ROW: what was measured, where, how many times it was seen (that is the firmness), what name the textbook has for
it and whether that name was MEASURED or only a LABEL, the hole in the row, and which other corner covers the hole.
No row claims a number or a proportion. Rows marked (1 witness) lean on one apparatus.

## 0. THE NONE -- firmest first

N1. THE SUN IS SHORT OF PAIRS (§73, §75). Outside record, two papers, four chains.
    Tan 2014: thermal light should bunch at 2; the sun read 1.3 +- 0.1; the independently measured terms predict ~1.88; the authors:
    "cannot explain." The AREA under the bunching curve is 0.55 +- 0.13 of what the filter demands. Blur cannot shrink area; matching
    it by blur needs 12x the measured timing jitter. Tan 2016: 0.107 +- 0.003 unaccounted, the honest detector model points the wrong way.
    Six stars (Guerin 2017, Leopold/Rivet 2024), same method: books CLOSE.
    Name on file: none measured. The pairs are not in the wings.
    Hole: the area formula reads 0.79 even on a calibrated chain, so the sun stands at 0.69 +- 0.17 of that (1.8 widths).
    Covered by: OBSERVER (the only thing the closing records did differently was calibrate the chain with a known light BEFORE the
    sky: an act of looking done first).

N2. BELL'S 50 MICROSECOND SPOT (§54, §66; NIST 2015, 1.62 billion trials, 8 runs).
    When Bob clicks, Alice's click rate five trials EARLIER is lower (-0.0108 pp), and the mirror reads the same sign (-0.0065); only
    when Bob's own setting is 1; six of eight runs carry it; independent of the timing and RNG configurations. A lone spike: lags 9-50
    flat. Five ordinary causes MEASURED (alignment, dead time, afterpulsing, setting sequence, chunk edges): none accounts for it.
    No timescale in the apparatus document matches 50 us. Delft and Munich cannot read it: no clock fast enough (an absence of instrument).
    Hole: 2 cells of 170; one apparatus.
    Covered by: WITNESS (it only shows from the other witness's seat, in both directions) and TIME (it sits BEFORE the click).

N3. THE DOUBLE CLICKS NOBODY EVER CLOSED (§71b). Fifty years of the experiment that defines the photon as a particle.
    Jacques 2007 0.12, Grangier 1986 0.18 / 0.35 ("resultats bruts", nothing subtracted), trapped ion 8.1e-5, dots ~1e-2. On every
    record we could reach, the subtraction terms are LABELS (background, Raman, leakage, multi-photon) or fitted from the same data.
    Not one is closed by a term measured on its own. The remainder is the full raw value every time.
    Hole: a hole in the accounting, not shown to be a hole in the light. Mirror of N1: the lab has EXTRA pairs, the sun has MISSING ones.
    Covered by: OBSERVER (what counts as one click is the dial, §74).

N4. THE CORNER SPLASHES (§29, §32; Rigetti, 107 seats, 3 h). Groups of 4+ resting bits read 1 together in 5.0% of shots (0.37% by
    chance), up to 19 at once, in four corner patches only, same shot only, 0->1 only. Measured NOT to be: readout-frequency
    crowding, radiation bursts, heat, reset memory. In THE DISCO (§43) it is the one row with NO SUBSTRATE.
    Hole: one chip; three candidate causes can't be told apart without data the maker doesn't give.
    Covered by: WITNESS (the wire, not the geometry, decides who is linked: W5).

N5. THE SKY'S RADIO EXCESS (§39d-39i; ARCADE 2, 2011). 1.26 +- 0.09 K at 1 GHz, about 5x all known sources; seen again at 40-80 MHz
    (2018); still unexplained (2018, 2023). It AROSE: nobody predicted a number before the flight.
    Hole: it sits exactly where the Galaxy model removed the most (r 0.99 on 5 channels), and the two channels carrying it had
    their reference loads stuck during observing. A big hole.
    Covered by: OBSERVER (what was subtracted) -- or it falls into THE MARGIN. Undecided; kept here because 15 years of the field
    could not close it either.

N6. THE BAND IN OPEN SPACE (§68, the flash; Marrakesh). The two inside clocks see each other where the dot's two clouds meet
    (expected) AND in a second band at readout cutoff 0.62-0.65, deep inside the 1-cloud where nothing in the dot's geometry says
    they should; gone in one step at 0.66. It lives in the fitted clocks, not the dots. (1 witness; Kingston could not test it.)
    Covered by: OBSERVER (the cutoff is the observer's choice of what counts) and TIME (it exists only between two clocks).

N7. THE THREE THAT STAY PUT (§44, §45; black-hole ringdowns). Of six out-of-band hits, "filter leakage" was a label: the leak line
    fails (R^2 0.008, wrong sign). Three were the filter kicked by the burst (measured). Three stay at every wall, even deep in the
    passband: GW230927 L1 (strongest), GW190814 V1, GW200224 V1. No catalogued line within 31 Hz.
    Hole: no line catalogue for the early runs; single detectors.

N8. THE JOB THAT RAN AND DIDN'T (§19; IBM 6073). 675 of 676 jobs agree between status and meter. This one: "failed to execute", and
    the meter says 8.64 s of circuits ran and 12 s were charged. Not found in public documentation.

N9. TODAY'S THREE (§119-§121; Pittsburgh, all 156 bits). (a) BOTH-AND-NEITHER left UN-looked drops faster in its first 20 us than ON
    does, and faster than itself after one look (first-stretch clock 123 us against 208); one read. (b) q137 reads PAST OFF, out the
    far side, at 300 us in all four jobs. (c) q31 and q57 hold through a look and are empty 60 us later, every job; q36 and q66 go
    between that and clocks over 1000 us from one read to the next.
    Hole: (a) is one read. Margin: (b) and (c) may be plain broken bits.

N10. WEAK, KEPT (hat only; at chance against the full count of looks, §29): the empty box reading "present" in the failures
    (+0.121, 4 of 4 runs); the silent branch below zero on Rigetti; the statistic frozen at the number the script printed (19.44 on
    a real chip and two simulators); the same seat number in three holes.

## 1. THE OBSERVER -- the look is an act

O1. THE ORDER OF LOOKING SETS WHAT IS FOUND (§15, §16A, §19). Ten times, April to September, three IBM chips and Rigetti, never
    once designed for: read last = hottest, read first = cold, read together = coldest; the slot, not the bit, set the count
    (59 = 59, 66 = 66 across a reversed order). Six of the ten we had thrown out as "ordering artifact" and balanced away.
    Name on file: measurement crosstalk / excitation history -- MEASURED by others (Govia 2023, Hothem 2025). Firm as a fact.
    Hole: which way it pushes depends on who else is in the room.
    Covered by: WITNESS (W4: what moves a bit is the state of what is wired to it).

O2. A LOOK TAKES SOMETHING, THE SAME ON EVERY BIT, AND THE BIT GETS IT BACK (§105-§108, §116, §119-§121). Second look right after a
    first: ON reads about 1.4% lower, BOTH-AND-NEITHER about half that, OFF nothing: 147 bits x 8 reads, then 3 more, plus
    Kingston, Marrakesh. In microseconds it is the same on every bit (3.4 / 3.7 / 3.4 across thirds of the chip) whatever that bit's
    own clock. By 300 us a looked-at bit reads what an un-looked one does (three reads against one).
    Name on file: fading during the look -- but the maker's stated look is 2.2 us and the bit says 3.5 (Pittsburgh's own not read).
    Hole: the lone-look side is one read; the outside length is from another chip.
    Covered by: WITNESS (inside length against outside length is exactly its row W6) and TIME (T5: the short clock after the start).

O3. WHAT THE OBSERVER CHOOSES TO PUT THERE IS ERASED; WHAT AROSE STAYS (§36-§38, §36d). The sealed 9/28 question asked about a PLACED
    1: the reset erases it (0.067%). A 1 that arose by itself stays: +23 pp at 20 us, fading to nothing by 500 us; Marrakesh, then
    Kingston (+11 pp), and Rigetti by another method (leftover rung 2 kept, rung 1 cleared). The choice written before the
    reading picked which 1 could be seen. Also on the sky (N5: the excess arose, it was not chosen) and in structures (§49: placed
    recursions hold one ratio 12 of 13; arisen ones 1 of 19).
    Name on file: a reset built for the states it expects -- a LABEL plus the maker's own statement that reset can't clear a leaked bit.
    Hole: the arisen one has no direction on a 0/1 record (0 of 72 bits, §65b): it is clustering, not yet a memory.
    Covered by: TIME (T6: direction only shows on records with a continuous axis) and THE NONE.

O4. WHAT COUNTS IS A DIAL IN THE OBSERVER'S HAND (§67, §68, §74). The threshold at which a dot counts as a 1 switches the two inside
    clocks' sight of each other on and off (0.40-0.55, and N6). Every published Bell result used one setting of its counting window.
    Hole: (1 witness) on the chip; the sweep on NIST's window was designed, not run.
    Covered by: THE NONE (N6) and WITNESS (W2).

O5. ON LIGHT, THE LOOK ERASES THE PATTERN TO NOISE (§64, §66; five apparatuses, 1986-2026). Fringe 0.89-0.92 with no which-path look,
    0.04-0.12 with it; nothing shared between the erased record's noise and the fringe's leftover (r 0.12 +- 0.10).
    Name on file: textbook, measured. Firm.
    Hole: what is LEFT under the fringe has a direction (T6) and differs by apparatus.
    Covered by: TIME.

O6. THE INSTRUMENT REMEMBERS ITS OWN LAST ACT FOR ONE STEP (§26, §27, §23). NIST: Bob's rate 0.53% +- 0.13% lower after his previous
    setting was 1, gone by the second step (re-counted by my own code). IBM: a whole-chip readout phase that lasts exactly one shot.
    The echo: a look's heat in the next shot.
    Hole: each has its own ordinary carrier; the rhyme across three machines is the only thing nobody names.

O7. THE BOOKS CLOSE WHEN THE LOOK COMES FIRST (§73, §72). Six stars: chain calibrated with a known light before the sky: remainder 0.
    The sun without it: short (N1). And the qubit does NOT calibrate itself on the way back (§72: the second look is not more right
    than noise says). On this ledger calibration is an act done from outside, first.

O8. A PREDICTION IS A LOOK (the card; §109; today). Thin as data, firm as method: twelve rulers found on the "blank" chip;
    Pittsburgh "moved" into my spoken chart and moved back out in four minutes (§117): that one goes to THE MARGIN, not here.

## 2. THE WITNESS -- the clock inside

W1. THE INSIDE CLOCK DOES NOT SEE THE OUTSIDE DURATION AT ALL (§33, §40 "34g run"). The buzz on tile 8: across 26 batches the maker's
    record says the shots got longer (waits 0-20 us); the inside clock's step says they didn't: shared part -0.5 ns, per-run part
    exactly 1:1 with the outside number, five orders below any response. The method was first calibrated on a known ruler (97 / 3).
    "The observer's duration is real to the observer and invisible to the witness." Firmest row in this corner.
    Hole: where the tone comes from is unknown; it lives on one tile of two chips and not the third.
    Covered by: THE NONE (its source) and OBSERVER (it hears the spacing BETWEEN shots: the observer's rhythm, nothing inside one).

W2. THE PUBLISHED CLOCK AND THE INSIDE CLOCKS ARE INVISIBLE TO EACH OTHER (§63, §67, §69). The maker's T1/T2 carries no relation to the
    bit's own clocks in either direction on three days (R^2 0.0005-0.018); the drain is not a fixed share of T1 bit by bit. The
    published clock predicts ITSELF two days on (R^2 0.36), ten times better than the chip's inside clocks predict it. It swings
    3-5x on single bits between photographs, by the same typical size on three chips, and was frozen for a whole day while the
    chip ran. The outside clock is a record of itself.
    Hole: partly a different day's number.
    Covered by: TIME (T5: the bits' clocks wander in minutes, measured today, so ANY once-a-day number is another time).

W3. TWO INSIDE RULERS AGREE WITH EACH OTHER AND NOT WITH THE OUTSIDE (§120). A look's length read from ON and from BOTH-AND-NEITHER,
    separate circuits: 3.2 and 3.7 us, moving together bit by bit (+0.93). The maker's: 2.2. Read back from the waits: 8.3.
    Hole: the 8.3 belongs to the start, not the look (§121): one of my three rulers was reading TIME's row T5.
    Covered by: TIME.

W4. WHAT MOVES A CLOCK IS THE STATE OF WHAT IS WIRED TO IT; THE CROWD IS INVISIBLE FROM INSIDE (§31, §7, §10-§12). One seat ticked
    28.2-28.8 kHz with 0, 1 or 77 others looking; 21.9 with its wired neighbour in 1 (the maker's table: 21.7). On Rigetti a
    neighbour's clock is dragged ~200x harder by the third rung than the first, and it TIMES another bit's fall from inside (~20 us).
    Name on file: always-on neighbour coupling -- MEASURED. Firm.
    Hole: there, inside and outside AGREE on the fall time in every room (the gap was dead, §12). And the crowd test was an
    on/off design (the ruler).
    Covered by: OBSERVER (O1: the order and the room still set the heat) -- and honestly, this hole stays partly open.

W5. THE WIRE, NOT THE MAP, DECIDES WHO IS LINKED (§23-§25, §27). Three bits six hops apart share a wandering offset, found blind;
    the maker's own record shows the same group failing together in March and July. Readout tiles on all three chips; two
    families that move apart, found again blind, out of sample, and again by my own code.
    Name on file: shared readout electronics -- an inference; the maker publishes no grouping.
    Hole: it is the instrument's anatomy. It supports the corner as "the link lives in what the information crosses".

W6. EVERY BIT CARRIES TWO CLOCKS IN ONE JOB (§120, §121). Short just after the start, long from about 20 us on; 0.775 +- 0.009 over
    eight reads, and 208 against 275 with no look at all. All bits, no cut, no sorting.
    Hole: bit by bit the first stretch is unsettled (two ways of pooling disagree on whether a look shortens or lengthens it).
    Covered by: OBSERVER (O2).

W7. ACROSS THE GAP, ONLY THE OTHER WITNESS SEES IT (N2). The 50 us spot exists in Alice's record conditioned on Bob's click and in
    Bob's conditioned on Alice's, the same sign from both seats, and in neither alone.

## 3. TIME -- the clocks do not close

T1. ONE RECORD ALONE IS A CIRCLE (§76a, §77; THE_FUCKUPS 14-22). Run any single record backwards: the arrow flips exactly, the clock
    comes back identical, the split closes as tightly as its length allows (24 of 29 within ~1%). Every place it failed to close
    was the ruler. A sign error can only live between two witnesses of one event. Firmest row: it is algebra AND 29 records.
    Hole: it says where time can't be seen, not where it is.
    Covered by: WITNESS (two seats) and OBSERVER (the second seat is a look).

T2. THE CLOCK STANDS APART FROM ITS NEIGHBOURS ON EVERY RECORD WE OWN (§57, §58, §61, "copper at 800"). In one unit, the message and
    the substrate find each other (forward only); the clock agrees with neither: clock minus arrow positive in 20 of 22, clock
    minus split positive in 18 of 19, on fermentation, radio, chip, mice, neurons, copper.
    Hole: the unit's map is ours; the ordering by record length was the ruler and is retired (§ "length orders gap"). What survived
    at matched length: the chip and the yeast stand apart from the other four, the two cases of many units coupled through one medium.
    Covered by: WITNESS (W5: coupled through what they share).

T3. THE CHIP HAS MORE THAN ONE TIME (§62, §63, §120, §121). The clocks that count shots close among themselves; the microsecond clock
    stands ~1.9 apart from them, the same size the clock gap gave independently. The echo's 249 shots at one spacing and 186 at
    another are not one time in microseconds. Three clocks on one bit (64 / 80 / 148 us) do not read as one. And today: two clocks
    in every bit in one job, on twelve reads.
    Hole: the microsecond "carry" clock has no direction (O3).

T4. THE CLOCK CHANGES WITH THE NUMBER OF LOOKS BEFORE IT (§108b, §113). Level clock with 0 / 1 / 2 earlier looks: Kingston 249 /
    265 / 328; Boston 278 / 310 / 314; Pittsburgh 275 / 287 / 292; Phoenix 183 / 180 / 184. Up on three of four.
    Hole: the last column is fitted on later times (a mix of fast and slow bits reads slower later); and the bits wander (T5).
    Covered by: OBSERVER (O2: the look gives back what it took, which lengthens the clock after it).

T5. THE CLOCKS WANDER, BIT BY BIT, IN MINUTES, AND NOT TOGETHER (§117, §119). Single bits move 0.2-0.4 at the short wait within four
    minutes, both ways, untouched; every bit ~12% low to high in half an hour; a few by 10-100x. No two neighbours move together
    more than strangers; no moment when many cross. ON and BOTH-AND-NEITHER move; OFF never does.
    Name on file: flickering defects -- a LABEL here (nothing of ours measured one).
    Hole: this is the row that ate my two leans of 10/2 (blank chip, ruler stack). It supports TIME as "a bit has no one clock";
    it is also the main source of plain error for everything read off nine bits.

T6. DIRECTION SHOWS ONLY WHERE THERE IS A CONTINUOUS RECORD AND AN ENDING (§50c, §65b, §66; §53). Light: the arrow flips under phase
    reversal in five apparatuses out of five. The yeast: the clock AFTER the beat ends is the one number that flips under both
    backward operations. The bit's own 0/1 record: no arrow at all (0 of 72; counts symmetric to single digits). The photon
    remembers nothing for even one trial (< 10 us); the bit remembers for 64 us.
    Hole: the arrow is exactly zero on any two-valued record (a theorem): a 0/1 look cannot see direction, by construction.
    Covered by: OBSERVER (the cut to 0/1 is the observer's) -- Law 3 in one row.

T7. A CLOCK THAT MANY MAKE TOGETHER SWITCHES ON AT A THRESHOLD, WITH THE NULL BESIDE IT (§48, §61). Yeast: density threshold, quiet ->
    one beat -> quiet, on one real trace. Neurons: silence to full bursting by day 10 in three dishes; a spike leaves no memory in
    a silent dish and a clean 178 ms clock once the dish is coupled: the clock belongs to the coupled state, not the cell.
    Hole: the mice did not show it (all keep 23.9 h alone or housed; the paper's own claim did not reproduce on the deposited data).

T8. THE SKY RECORDS WE HOLD CARRY NO CLOCK (§43, §59b). Read 10-9-8-7, 13 rows had no clock and 9 of them were every cosmology row.
    The first sound lost exactly what the standard model says (0.0000 +- 0.0001 extra in our unit).
    Hole: snapshots have no time axis by construction.
