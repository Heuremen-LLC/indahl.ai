# Literature check, 2026-09-29 (Wayfinder & Bones)

What the published record already has for the four findings in THIRD_RUNG_20260928.md (sections 17-19). Web and papers only. No shots, no money, nothing posted.
Every quote below was read in the source itself (arXiv text or the live IBM page) unless it is marked otherwise.

---

## Verdicts

1. **Echo / look-heat (Marrakesh, drain ~50-75 us, not tracking T1): PARTLY KNOWN.**
   Already known: IBM says in its docs that init error rises as rep_delay gets shorter. IBM's own Paul Nation posted 2021 data (ibmq_montreal) where the prep-0 error has an excess at short delay that is mostly gone within ~25-50 us. Readout pulses heating transmons is known too, and so is Heron's conditional reset being unable to clear leaked qubits.
   Not found anywhere: a per-qubit drain time on a many-qubit chip that stays near one value (~50-75 us) while T1 varies 30x, that doesn't care whether the last shot ended 0 or 1, and that is tested for reaching qubits that were never read. The closest timescale in print is Nho et al. 2026: after a quasiparticle burst the qubit's temperature spike falls back in about 50 us (hot phonons). That one comes from radiation hits, not readout.
2. **Order of looking (~0.4-2%): KNOWN, basically textbook.** Mid-circuit readout errors on spectator qubits, idle decay while others are being read, cleanup of leftover excitation, and TLS history are all published. IBM-affiliated authors order their calibration shots specifically "to minimise the chance of residual excitations." The only new thing is our compilation across eras and vendors, and that's a pattern note, not a new effect.
3. **Leftover rung 2 on Rigetti: KNOWN BY DESIGN.** A measure-then-flip reset flips only 0 and 1, so it can't touch |2>. That's documented for Heron, for Google's chips, and in Rigetti's own description of its reset. Our part is one measured case on Cepheus where the carry-over sets the next shot's start, and it's statistically thin (~1 in 100 after the splits).
4. **6073 ran-then-failed: PARTLY KNOWN.** Getting charged is documented policy: a job that fails for a user-error reason is billed for everything it used up to the failure. 6073 is a documented job-size code. Not found anywhere: a report or explanation of a 6073 (or any size-limit error) that shows up after the job has already started executing.

## A. Rep-rate heating, readout heating, leftover excitation, quasiparticles

- **IBM docs, "Qubit initialization" (quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/repetition-rate-execution).** "As you lower the rep_delay, the total QPU execution time decreases, at the expense of increasing the state preparation error rate." IBM puts the error down to "imperfections in the reset process." It gives no mechanism and no time constant.
- **Paul Nation (IBM), "Improving state prep errors on IBM Quantum systems," blog, 7 Nov 2021 (nonhermitian.org; now 404, read via web.archive.org).** He calls IBM's implicit start-of-shot step "unconditional reset," and he writes that reset "is a measurement followed by a conditional x-gate." His posted ibmq_montreal numbers show all-zeros error of 1.86% at rep_delay 0, 1.44% at 25 us, 1.33% at 50 us, and a ~1.2% floor. By eye from those numbers, the excess decays in roughly 20-30 us, faster than that chip's T1. Those are chip-average numbers, with no per-qubit fit and no T1 comparison. **This is the closest published relative of finding 1.**
- **IBM blog "IBM Quantum Nighthawk r2 -- more circuits, faster," Haas, McKay, Davis, 31 Aug 2026.** On Heron: "The processor measures the state of a qubit and, if it is found to be in the |1> state, applies a bit flip gate." Also: "conditional reset is limited by the fidelity of measurement and cannot reset qubits that have leaked outside the computational state," so "the system must sit idle for hundreds of microseconds between circuit executions." So Heron's start-of-shot init *does* include a measurement. The docs don't say whether idle qubits get it too.
- **Connolly, Kurilovich, ..., Devoret, "Coexistence of nonequilibrium density and equilibrium energy distribution of quasiparticles in a superconducting qubit," PRL 132, 217001 (2024), arXiv:2302.12330.** Supplement: "reading out sparsely allows us to avoid transmon heating by the readout pulses. Reading out more frequently can systematically bias the extracted parity switching rates." They read only once every ~2 ms and measured the bias against the delay (Fig. S5b). So readout heating that depends on the gap between reads is known, on a single qubit.
- **Nho, Connolly, ..., Devoret, "Recovery dynamics of a gap-engineered transmon after a quasiparticle burst," PRL 136, 050601 (2026), arXiv:2505.08104.** Two findings matter here:
  - "the qubit temperature abruptly jumps to ~200 mK but then quickly decays down to ~90 mK after about 50 us," which they put down to high-energy phonons, followed by a ~6 ms tail from phonons slowly leaving the chip.
  - With a read every ~5 us, "the steady-state qubit temperature (~50 mK) exceeds the base temperature of fridge (~20 mK). This elevation is likely due to the readout-induced qubit transitions."
  - **This is the best published match for a ~50 us clock that isn't T1.** But the trigger there is a radiation burst, not a readout.
- **Wang, Gao, ..., Schoelkopf, "Measurement and control of quasiparticle dynamics in a superconducting qubit," Nat. Commun. 5, 5836 (2014), arXiv:1406.7300.** Quasiparticles injected on purpose decay steeply at first (recombination), then with trapping times of ~1.5 ms and tails up to ~18 ms. Plain quasiparticle recovery is usually slower than 50-75 us.
- **Wenner et al., "Excitation of superconducting qubits from hot non-equilibrium quasiparticles," PRL 110, 150502 (2013), arXiv:1209.1674.** "By injecting hot quasiparticles into a qubit, we experimentally measure an increase of Pe." Pe goes back down as the delay after injection grows. This is the injection version of our "look, then wait" curve.
- **Lenander et al., PRB 84, 024501 (2011), arXiv:1101.0862.** After injection, the decay rate takes ~300-500 us to settle.
- **Jin et al., "Thermal and residual excited-state population in a 3D transmon qubit," PRL 114, 240501 (2015), arXiv:1412.2772.** The floor sits at ~0.1% and is put down to hot quasiparticles. At a 2 kHz repetition rate they estimate the carry-over error is "a negligible 0.005%." That's the textbook floor our 0.31% sits on.
- **Measurement-induced state transitions (leakage):**
  - **Sank et al., PRL 117, 190503 (2016), arXiv:1606.05721.** Readout "can push the qubit out of the two-level subspace."
  - **Khezri et al., PR Applied 20, 054008 (2023), arXiv:2212.05097.** "occupation in these higher energy levels poses a major challenge for fast qubit reset."
  - **Hazra et al., PRL 134, 100601 (2025), arXiv:2407.10934.** Readout leakage of 0.12-7.76% even at >99.5% readout fidelity.
  - **Wang, D'Anjou, Gigon, Blais, Blok, PRX 16, 021033 (2026), arXiv:2505.00639.** Maps where the population lands after "ionization."
  - **McEwen et al., Nat. Commun. 12, 1761 (2021), arXiv:2102.06131.** Readout is a leading source of |2>, which is why they built a "multi-level reset."
- **Readout degrades T1 while it's running:** Thorbeck, Xiao, Kamal, Govia (IBM), PRL 132, 090602 (2024), arXiv:2305.10508. Also Huang et al. (Painter group), PRX Quantum 2026, arXiv:2603.13857. Both cover effects *during* the readout pulse, not tens of us after it.
- **Chip-wide (unread qubits heating too)?** Not found. Here's what does exist about readout acting on spectators:
  - dephasing through shared lines (Heinsoo et al., PR Applied 10, 034040 (2018), arXiv:1801.07904);
  - spectator errors from mid-circuit readout (Govia et al. 2023, below);
  - multi-qubit MIST theory, "the spectator qubit can be impacted by the measurement-induced transition of the readout qubit" (Hoyau, McDonald, Varbanov, Munoz-Arias, Blais, arXiv:2606.05010, June 2026).
  - None of them report an excited-population rise on unread qubits that drains over tens of us after other qubits were read.

**What this means for finding 1:** the ingredients are all published: short-gap init error, readout heating, and quasiparticle/phonon recovery with a ~50 us fast part. What hasn't been done in public is putting them together on a 74-qubit Heron r2 with per-qubit fits against T1.

Caveats we have to carry:
- 9 fits and rank -0.6 is not significant (n=9 needs about |0.7| for p<0.05). So "doesn't follow T1" holds, but "runs the wrong way" doesn't.
- IBM's T1 numbers come from a different time than our shots, and T1 drifts (Klimov et al., PRL 121, 090502 (2018), arXiv:1809.01043).
- The drain time of |2> is set by the 1-2 transition's own surroundings (TLS at a different frequency), not locked to T1/2. So "not rung 2" is **not yet ruled out** for any single qubit.

## B. Order of looking, crosstalk, history

- **Govia, Jurcevic, Wood, Kanazawa, Merkel, McKay (IBM), "A randomized benchmarking suite for mid-circuit measurements," New J. Phys. 25, 123016 (2023), arXiv:2207.04836.** Detects "measurement-induced errors on spectator qubits and entangling errors between measured and spectator qubits" on an IBM Falcon.
- **Hothem, Hines, Baldwin, Gresh, Blume-Kohout, Proctor, "Measuring error rates of mid-circuit measurements," Nat. Commun. (2025), arXiv:2410.16706.** Measures "measurement-induced crosstalk error on a 27-qubit IBM Q processor."
- **Harper, ..., Brown (IBM), Bartlett, "Characterising the failure mechanisms of error-corrected quantum logic gates," Nat. Commun. (2026), arXiv:2504.07258.** On Heron: spectator fidelity loss during others' readout "occurs when the qubit relaxes during the measurement of the other qubits. The same effect occurs if we use delays instead of measurements." On Heron r1 (Jan 2024), "mid-circuit measurement of certain qubits caused considerable crosstalk between many qubits ... mainly resolved on ... Heron r2."
  - This is textbook order-of-reading: the qubit read last waited longest.
- **Lee, ..., Harper, Brown (IBM), "Scalable quantum error correction tailored for a heavy-hex qubit array," arXiv:2604.14296 (2026, ibm_pittsburgh).** "To minimise the chance of residual excitations, we prepare all |0> states first, followed by all |1> states and then all |2> states." So people who know the hardware already order their shots to avoid carry-over, and they treat that as housekeeping.
- **TLS history:**
  - Klimov et al. 2018 (above): T1 wanders over time and frequency because of TLS.
  - Spiecker et al., "Two-level system hyperpolarization using a quantum Szilard engine," Nat. Phys. (2023), arXiv:2204.00499. Repeated measure-and-flip cools or heats a TLS bath with ">50 ms" memory and moves the qubit population "four times lower" or up to ~80%.
  - Zhuang et al., Nat. Commun. (2026), arXiv:2503.16381. TLS "with millisecond lifetimes" on fluxonium.
- **The scheduler moving unpinned measurements late** is standard ALAP scheduling. It's a known trap, not physics.

## C. How the machines reset between shots

- **IBM Heron:** conditional reset, meaning measure then pi-pulse if the result is 1 (Nighthawk r2 blog). It's on by default (`init_qubits`: "Whether to reset the qubits to the ground state for each shot. Default is True"). `rep_delay` is "the delay between a measurement and the subsequent quantum circuit" (ExecutionOptionsV2 docs).
  - IBM documents that state-prep error rises at short rep_delay (above).
  - Not found: whether unused qubits get the init measurement, and whether Marrakesh uses excited-state-promoted readout (`measure_esp_enabled` in the backend config; free to check, and it would leave |2> after a readout of 1).
  - Nighthawk r2 (Aug 2026) swaps this for a dissipative reset with "around a 25x reduction in initialization error."
- **Rigetti:** active reset works in loops. "If the result is 1 ... the feedback block containing the X gate program is executed." That's three rounds, about 9-12 us on Aspen-4 (Karalekas et al., Quantum Sci. Technol. 5, 024003 (2020), arXiv:2001.04449).
  - "The Amazon Braket execution process ... always uses Active Reset" (Pelofske, Bartschi, Eidenbenz, arXiv:2203.03816, Aspen era).
  - An X on the 0-1 transition does nothing to |2>, so leftover |2> is expected by design.
  - Not found: any Cepheus-specific reset documentation.

## D. Error 6073

- **IBM error registry (quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/errors):** "6073 -- The size of the job exceeds the memory limits of the classical control hardware. If possible, consider breaking the job up into multiple separate jobs or reducing the use of measurement results in subsequent conditions." Related codes:
  - 6057 is too many classical registers.
  - 6116 is "Control system resources exceeded."
  - 8038 is "A timing error occurred during execution ... Try increasing the rep_delay value or reducing the number of measurements/resets."
- **IBM "Job limits" page:** jobs are "first validated to try to ensure that the job can run on the QPU, to decrease the likelihood that your job fails after you've waited." Over the limits, a job "will usually fail." IBM doesn't promise that validation catches everything.
- **IBM "Workload usage" page:** "If the failure or cancellation occurred because of a system error, the reported usage is zero. For jobs that failed due to user error ... the reported usage is any consumption that has occurred up to that point, including the overhead incurred to prepare the QPU." 6073 is written as a user-side size problem, so 12 s charged for 8.64 s of circuits fits the stated policy.
- **Not found:** any GitHub issue, forum post or doc that describes a 6073 (or any size-limit code) arriving *after* partial execution. My guess is that the controller loads the job in pieces and a later piece overflowed, but I haven't verified it. The GitHub search API hit its rate limit, so that search wasn't exhaustive. Web search found nothing.

## E. What a convincing new test looks like

These are built from the published designs above: Govia/Hothem measured-vs-spectator splits, Harper's delay-instead-of-measurement control, Wenner/Nho's "event, then variable wait," and Nation's init_qubits=False trick.

1. **Half-chip heater (section 17 plan) plus the controls reviewers will ask for:**
   - Run a "heater" half of the chip on repeated measurements, and read the other half's start excess against the gap.
   - Run a matched arm where the heater measurements are swapped for delays of the same length (Harper's control).
   - Raise the dose: 0, 10, 40, all heater qubits; 1, 2, 4 back-to-back reads.
   - If unread qubits heat on the same ~60 us clock, and the heat scales with dose and vanishes in the delay arm, that's the new result.
2. **Get the IQ points, not just bits.** `meas_type="kerneled"` in SamplerV2 returns IQ data. If the excess "1"s sit in a third blob, it's leakage (MIST/|2>). If they sit in the |1> blob, it's heating to rung 1. This is what separates the textbook explanation from the new one.
3. **In-circuit wait.** Fix a short rep_delay and add a delay *inside* the circuit before the first read. If the excess drains on the same ~60 us clock, the heat is on the chip and not in the init step. Also try `init_qubits=False` with 1 vs 3 explicit resets (Nation 2021) to see whether IBM's own init measurement is the heater.
4. **Where it shows up tells you the cause (per the literature):**
   - **Quasiparticles/phonons (Nho 2026):** all qubits at once, with a weak distance dependence and a fast ~50 us part plus a slow ms tail.
   - **Readout crosstalk (Heinsoo 2018):** clusters on qubits sharing a feedline or readout group.
   - **Leakage (Khezri 2023):** shows up as a third IQ blob, depends on the state before the read, and drains per qubit.
   - **Free checks first:** Marrakesh's `meas_map`, `measure_esp_enabled`, `default_rep_delay` and `rep_delay_range`.
