## Q-5 Elon University, Dept of Mathematics and Statistics (private, ~6,200 undergraduates) - "Comparing Alternative AI Syllabus Policies for Teaching Introductory Statistical Computing" (Bussberg, Doehler, Taylor, Weaver; eCOTS 2026 slides, 15 pp.)
Source (read in full by Bones, 25 Oct): https://causeweb.org/cause/sites/default/files/ecots/ecots22/materials/Elon_eCOTS2026_AISyllabus_slides.pdf  [slides, not a paper; no data link; no funding line on the slides]
- Design (slide 3): "'Must cite' - use of AI was allowed for most coding assignments ... 'No AI' - use of AI was prohibited on all coding assignments"; "Each of the presenters 'enrolled' two sections and assigned one to each syllabus policy (assignment was not random)"; 8 sections, 4 fall 2025 + 4 spring 2026. The instructors are the authors.
- What was collected (slide 4): "2 SAS coding assessments" + an anonymous post-semester survey (Elon IRB). The slides show NO assessment results; only the survey.
- Response (slide 5): 72 of 118 (61%) in "Must Cite" vs 47 of 96 (49%) in "No AI" (the two arms respond at different rates; Fisher exact p = 0.097 on the rate gap, our calculation).
- Self-reported AI use (slide 6): "Must Cite" 36 of 72 (50%, Wilson 95% [39%, 61%]); "No AI" 15 of 47 (32%, [20%, 46%]) said they used generative AI on a SAS coding assignment in a section where it was prohibited. Self-report, anonymous, 49% response: a floor for use in the "No AI" arm among respondents, not a rate for the sections.
- Perception items (slide 7): five items (confidence, enjoyment, pride, build on SAS, excited to tell others), CMH row-mean p = 0.33 to 0.66, "no significant or notable differences". No equivalence bound printed -> CANNOT_TELL for "same"; every item comparable-looking at these n.
- By AI use (slide 15, appendix): enjoyed learning 76% (no AI) vs 67% (some AI), p = 0.02; would like to build on SAS 49% vs 29%, p = 0.02. Observational, self-selected, not adjusted for policy arm on the slide.
- Conclusion (slide 10): "no significant or notable differences in student perceptions"; take-away: "All four presenters intend to implement 'no AI' policies in this course in upcoming semesters, and will shift to using more in-class coding assessments to monitor AI use" - a policy change decided with the performance data (collected) unreported on the slides.
Reading: a small-private-school faculty study that measured a perception outcome of its own AI policy by section, and found that a third of respondents in the prohibited arm used AI anyway. Not an institutional measurement of Elon's AI policy; Elon is not a Wall row. Open: the coding-assessment results; ask for the paper/data only after launch (no outreach now).

Source (read 25 Oct; the university's own news item, not the paper): https://www.clarkson.edu/news-events/clarkson-university-professors-publish-study-ai-writing-coach-first-year-seminar (2025-04-09)
