- S0 self-evaluation: MEASURED. Institution-run (Eberly Center + Office of the Vice Provost for Teaching & Learning Innovation) randomized course-level
  study of its own GenAI learning modules, Fall 2024: "1,368 students across 53 different courses taught by 46 instructors" ... "Each course was randomly
  assigned to either the treatment or control condition." Result: "Engaging with the learning modules significantly improved students' knowledge for how
  LLMs work (LO1), prompt engineering skills (authentic task 1), and self-efficacy above and beyond control students" ... "The modules did not affect
  students' knowledge for responsible use (LO3)". Skills scored on a subset: "n = 174 students".
  (https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/gaitar/gaitaratscale/asynchronouslearningmodules.html). Policy consequence stated: "CMU has incorporated portions of
  these modules into a required online course taken by all first-year undergraduate students."
  Second institution-run study (GAITAR@Scale 2, Spring 2025, quasi-experimental, Copilot as feedback partner vs peer): "424 students across 28 first-year
  writing courses taught by 16 instructors"; "The only significant difference in the rate of improvement (i.e., learning) was between condition 0 (peer)
  and condition 3 (genAI - receive multiple)." (https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/gaitar/gaitaratscale/feedbackpartner.html).
  Programme-level review of the fellowship itself: Hershock et al. 2026, Teaching and Learning Inquiry 14, "a failed project is one that did not collect
