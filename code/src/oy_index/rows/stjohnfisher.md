## S5 testable claims - the abstract's headline, checked against the article's own tables (Bones, 25 Oct)
1. Abstract: "the proportion of courses with blanket prohibition dropped sharply from 57% in the fall of 2023 to 23% in the fall of 2025". Table 1/2: "Prohibited" overall is 57% -> 59% (Fall 23 -> Fall 25), i.e. flat; the 23% is "Only Prohibited". The article says so: "the number of courses choosing some amount of prohibition has remained relatively stable". CONSISTENT as written (blanket vs any), but the abstract does not say any-prohibition was flat.
2. The size of the drop is mostly a form change: the faculty form allowed ONE category in Fall 23 / Spr 24 and MULTIPLE categories from Fall 24 ("Faculty teaching during the fall 2023 or spring 2024 semester were only able to select one of these options, while ... fall 2024 ... able to designate the level ... at the assignment level"). 57% -> 32% (Fall 23 -> Fall 24) happens at the form change (25 points); 32% -> 23% (Fall 24 -> Fall 25, same form) is 9 points (Wilson 95% on our recomputed 262/804 = [29.4%, 35.9%], 189/866 = [19.2%, 24.7%]). The authors note the form change for "Permitted" ("may be a result of faculty being able to indicate more than one category") but not for the headline drop. -> the like-for-like change is about 9-11 points, not 34.
3. "more than five-fold increase" in required-use courses: 14 -> 79 is 5.6x in COUNT; as a share of syllabi collected 2.5% -> 9.1% is 3.6x; and it spans the form change. Fall 25 printed "79 (8%)": 79/866 = 9.1%.
4. Printed percentages vs printed counts: Fall 23, Spr 24, Spr 25 reproduce from the collected totals. Fall 24 percentages imply a denominator of about 815-826 (collected 804); Fall 25 has no single denominator that fits (502 -> 59% implies 844-858, 627 -> 71% implies 877-889, 79 -> 8% implies 929+, 189 -> 23% implies 804-840; collected 866 gives 58.0%, 72.4%, 9.1%, 21.8%). Differences <= 1.5 points; "fewer than one quarter" still holds (21.8%). Noted, not a verdict.
