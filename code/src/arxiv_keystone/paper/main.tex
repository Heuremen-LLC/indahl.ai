\title{A Readout's Kick Is a Turn: Giving It Back, and How Long the Correction Lasts}

\author{K. Indahl}
\email{Indahl@heuremen.com}
\affiliation{Heuremen LLC}

\date{November 1, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
A mid-circuit readout of 39 qubits on an IBM Heron processor turns the phase of the 117 qubits it never touches by a median of about two degrees per readout, and by more than ten degrees on some of them. We correct each untouched qubit with a virtual $R_z$ of its own measured kick after every readout, the correction proposed by Krinner and coworkers, and compare it with a symmetric echo and with idles of the same scheduled length in the same job. Three setups taken from published work were run on ibm\_kingston and ibm\_marrakesh on 31 October 2026: all untouched qubits on the equator, 48 untouched Bell pairs, and a Page--Wootters history state with the readout in the middle of its build. Two correction tables were used, one measured ten days earlier and one measured minutes before. On ibm\_kingston the ten-day table gives back 0.92 [0.87, 0.95] of the untouched qubits' kick, 0.93 [0.84, 0.96] of the Bell-pair phase and 0.76 [0.73, 0.78] of the clock state's distance from its idle, and the fresh table gives back 0.93, 0.96 and 1.00 (99\,\% bootstrap bands). On ibm\_marrakesh the median kick read on 31 October was $-1.68$ [$-2.34$, $-1.02$] degrees per readout against $-2.39$ ten days earlier, and the ten-day table over-corrects: its residual lands on the other side of zero, and it moves the clock state further from its idle. The fresh table gives back 0.80 [0.67, 0.88] of the kick and 0.92 [0.83, 0.96] of the pair phase there, and the marrakesh clock keeps a fidelity deficit of 0.07 to 0.08 in every corrected and echoed arm. What a frame change cannot give back on ibm\_kingston is small: a coherence loss of about 0.006 on the untouched qubits, and a decrease of 0.014 [0.002, 0.023] in the Bell pairs' X-part concurrence with the ten-day correction. The plan, code and synthetic tests were fixed and timestamped before any job was submitted, and a design review of the plan was acted on before then.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Krinner and coworkers measured a coherent phase on untargeted qubits when another qubit is read out in a 17-qubit surface code (mean $0.6^\circ$, and $13.4^\circ$ on the pair with the strongest dephasing) and wrote that they expected to correct it ``by utilizing virtual-Z rotations of equal magnitude and opposite sign'' \cite{Krinner2022}. A surface-code experiment by Google Quantum AI included ``phase corrections to correct for unintended qubit frequency shifts'' in a cycle that reads out its measure qubits \cite{Google2023}. Mid-circuit-measurement work on IBM devices found coherent $Z$ phases on qubits next to a measured one \cite{Govia2023,Chen2022}, and the usual remedy is an echo or dynamical decoupling during the readout \cite{Shirizly2025,Chen2022,Bultink2020,Kan2026}. Kan and coworkers compared a readout with a delay of equal length on neighbours prepared in $|+\rangle$ and read in $X$ and $Y$, and used an echo \cite{Kan2026}. Seif and coworkers appended $Z$ corrections for coherent errors that accumulate while a data qubit idles through measurement and feedforward, with angles calibrated by sweeping the window \cite{Seif2024}.

Our earlier work compared readouts with idles of the same scheduled length in the same job on IBM 156-qubit processors \cite{readout}. With 39 of 156 qubits read out, the phase of the 117 untouched qubits turned by about two degrees per readout, beyond the idle. Several recent protocols treat a readout as its waiting time or treat the qubits next to it as unaffected. A mitigation scheme for dynamic circuits applies ``the same delay as the one measured qubits experience due to the measurements'' to spectators so that its mitigation also covers them \cite{SantosUzdin2025}. A learned measurement instrument assumes that spectator qubits influencing the measured qubit are ``either negligible or effectively absorbed into a stationary single-qubit instrument description'' \cite{Chu2026}. A heat-pump study's noise simulation models each ancilla measurement as its waiting time plus an assignment error \cite{Caselli2026}, and an error-correction study's baseline noise model gives qubits idling during measurement ``the same reported ID error, even though measurements can take substantially longer than gates'' \cite{McLauchlan2026}. Separately, in a zero-noise-extrapolation study a spectator was found ``rotating coherently'' when an idle window was stretched without an echo, which the author attributed to a static frequency offset or ZZ coupling \cite{Kumar2026}.

Here we apply the correction Krinner and coworkers proposed, per qubit, on two IBM Heron processors, and ask two questions. How much of what a readout takes from the qubits next to it is a turn that a change of frame gives back, and how much is lost? And how long does a correction table stay good? We ask both inside three setups taken from the work above, including a Page--Wootters history state \cite{PageWootters1983,Moreva2014,Diaz2025,Coppo2024} whose clock is read in the middle of its build. We name no mechanism.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Devices, roles and the look}
Two IBM Heron processors, ibm\_kingston and ibm\_marrakesh (156 qubits each), on 31 October 2026, through Qiskit 2.5.2 and qiskit-ibm-runtime 0.49.0. On each we reuse the roles of our control run of 21 October \cite{readout}: a pack of 39 qubits whose mid-circuit readout is the \emph{look}, and the 117 qubits that are never measured or gated during a look, which we call untouched. The pack starts in $|0\rangle$ in every arm. A look step reads out the whole pack with the default \texttt{measure}. Its idle counterpart holds every qubit of the pack idle for exactly the scheduled readout length $L$ (545 and 671 samples of the device's time step dt on ibm\_kingston and ibm\_marrakesh). Barriers on all qubits surround every step, and the untouched qubits receive no gates during a step.

\subsection{The correction}
After every look step each untouched target $q$ receives a virtual $R_z(-k_q)$, where $k_q$ is its kick in degrees per readout. A virtual $R_z$ is a change of frame and takes no time. Every untouched qubit is corrected with its own point value, and none is selected or dropped by the size of its kick. Two tables are used. The ten-day table comes from our control run of 21 October, read with the committed estimator (phase $\phi=\mathrm{atan2}(\langle Y\rangle,\langle X\rangle)$, kick $=(\phi_\mathrm{look}-\phi_\mathrm{idle})/N$ after $N$ steps) and taken at the dose it is applied at ($N=4$ or $N=8$). The fresh table is measured in a calibration job on the same chip minutes before the main jobs (idle and look, $N=4$ and $8$, read in $X$ and $Y$, 4{,}000 shots).

\subsection{Arms}
Six arms run interleaved in the same job: idle (D), idle with a symmetric echo (DE), look (M), look with the ten-day correction (MC), look with the fresh correction (MF), and look with the echo (ME). The echo is an $X$ on every untouched target after step $N/2$ and after step $N$, a net identity. It also refocuses each qubit's own precession during the idle, so the echo arm is compared with the idle-echo arm and every other look arm with the plain idle. Scheduled durations on the untouched qubits are identical for D, M, MC and MF, and for DE and ME (the two $X$ gates add 16\,dt on ibm\_kingston and 18\,dt on ibm\_marrakesh).

\subsection{Setups}
\emph{Untouched qubits.} All 117 are put on the equator ($\sqrt{X}$), undergo $N=8$ steps, and are read in $X$, in $Y$, and after $\sqrt{X}^\dagger$ in $Z$, where a qubit that kept its state reads 0 (2{,}000 shots per circuit and copy). Their coherence is $|\langle X\rangle+i\langle Y\rangle|$. \emph{Pairs.} 48 coupled untouched pairs are prepared in $|\Phi^+\rangle$ (the other 21 untouched qubits on the equator), undergo $N=4$ steps and are read in $ZZ$, $XX$, $YY$, $XY$, $YX$ (2{,}500 shots). From these we read the Bell fidelity $(p_{00}+p_{11})/2+(\langle XX\rangle-\langle YY\rangle)/4$ and the X-part concurrence of our earlier work \cite{readout}, which a $Z$ rotation of either qubit leaves unchanged. \emph{Clock.} A Page--Wootters history state of two clock qubits $c_0,c_1$ and a system qubit $s$, $|\Psi\rangle=\tfrac12\sum_{t=0}^{3}|t\rangle\otimes R_y(t\theta)|0\rangle$ with $t=c_0+2c_1$ and $\theta=\pi/4$, is built as $H$ on both clock qubits, a controlled $R_y(\theta)$ from $c_0$ to $s$, the look block ($N=4$), and a controlled $R_y(2\theta)$ from $c_1$ to $s$. The phases on $c_0$ and $c_1$ commute with the second controlled rotation, but the phase on $s$ does not, so only an in-circuit correction can undo that part. All 27 Pauli settings are read (1{,}000 shots), giving the 63 non-identity Pauli expectations $\vec{P}$ by linear inversion, and the history fidelity $F=\tfrac18\sum_P\langle P\rangle\langle P\rangle_\Psi$. The clock qubits are $(c_0,s,c_1)=(3,16,23)$ on ibm\_kingston, as in our run of 24 October, and $(104,105,106)$ on ibm\_marrakesh, chosen before any job by a rule on the ten-day table and the day's calibration record. Both clocks were chosen for a large spread of kicks across their three qubits, so the clock readings describe those qubits, not a chip average.

\subsection{Jobs, readings and rulers}
Per chip: the calibration job, then a main job with 210 circuits in order and again reversed, giving two copies (A and B). Dynamical decoupling and twirling are off, and circuits are transpiled once (optimization level 1, trivial layout, seed 31). Every reading has four outcomes (above, below, consistent, cannot tell), is read on each copy and pooled, and is reported as the pooled outcome only when the two copies agree. Readings on the untouched qubits and pairs use the median over qubits (pairs) with a 2{,}000-draw bootstrap over qubits (pairs) and a 99\,\% band (600 draws for the regression slopes), treating qubits (pairs) as independent. Clock readings use a 400-draw shot bootstrap and a 99\,\% band, whose ends vary in the second decimal between reruns of the bootstrap (no outcome changed in five reruns). A known-good check on each chip required the look's median kick to reproduce our 21 October value within a factor of two, with the band's upper end below zero.

The share given back by an arm is $1-\mathrm{median}|r_X|/\mathrm{median}|k_M|$, where $r_X$ is the arm's residual phase per readout and $k_M$ the uncorrected kick, on the same resampled qubits. Beside it, a per-qubit regression of $r_X$ on the kick measured in the other copy (Theil--Sen) gives a slope of 0 when all of the kick is given back and 1 when none is. For the pairs the same share is read on the phase of the $|00\rangle\langle 11|$ coherence, $\mathrm{atan2}(\langle XY\rangle+\langle YX\rangle,\langle XX\rangle-\langle YY\rangle)$, against its own idle. For the clock it is read on the squared distance of $\vec{P}$ from the arm's own idle, $1-|\vec{P}_X-\vec{P}_\mathrm{none}|^2/|\vec{P}_M-\vec{P}_D|^2$, each squared distance less the shot-noise floor measured from the two copies, so a share can pass 1 by noise. These rulers do not depend on how far the idle state itself sits from the ideal one. Fidelities to the ideal states are printed beside them as secondary readings. ``More than half given back'' was the stated threshold for the claim. The echo arm, which leaves no kick, reads 0.95 and 0.91 on the share ruler for the untouched qubits, so values near those are at the ruler's own ceiling, and the regression slope is the cleaner statement there.

The plan, code, correction tables and synthetic tests were committed and their hashes timestamped with OpenTimestamps before the calibration job. In planted worlds (no kick, a stable kick, a 30\,\% drift, loss only, kick and loss, with 30 draws each on each chip's own table) the readers gave 3.6\,\% (ibm\_kingston) and 3.8\,\% (ibm\_marrakesh) false calls among planted-zero readings, most of them cannot-tell rather than a resolved wrong call (0.9\,\% and 1.1\,\% resolved wrong), and the headline readings resolved as planted in 30 of 30 draws wherever the planted kick was stable. A design review of the plan was acted on before any job was submitted.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Known-good}
The look's median kick on the untouched qubits was $-2.42$ [$-3.98$, $-1.93$] degrees per readout on ibm\_kingston (21 October: $-2.86$) and $-1.68$ [$-2.34$, $-1.02$] on ibm\_marrakesh (21 October: $-2.39$). Both chips passed. Six jobs, one attempt each, used 165\,s on ibm\_kingston and 171\,s on ibm\_marrakesh. Defined after the data: the largest kick read was about $21^\circ$ per readout, and 23 (ibm\_kingston) and 15 (ibm\_marrakesh) of the 117 untouched qubits turned by more than $10^\circ$ per readout.

On ibm\_kingston the regression slope of the residual on the kick is $-0.01$ [$-0.04$, $+0.02$] for the ten-day table and $-0.00$ [$-0.02$, $+0.02$] for the fresh one, against 0.99 for the uncorrected look (its own ceiling, set by noise in the other copy). The ten-day table's median residual is $-0.01$ [$-0.10$, $+0.11$] degrees per readout. Read in plain bits, untouched qubits that should return to 0 after $\sqrt{X}^\dagger$ read 1 more often after the look by 0.078 [0.037, 0.110] and by 0.002 [$-0.004$, $+0.008$] after the ten-day correction. Defined after the data, the tail the medians do not show: on ibm\_kingston 44 untouched qubits have a residual above $5^\circ$ per readout under the look, 4 with the ten-day table, 3 with the fresh table and none with the echo. On ibm\_marrakesh the counts are 28, 15, 3 and 2.

For the clock, the look takes the history fidelity from 0.719 (idle) to 0.165 on ibm\_kingston. The ten-day table brings it to 0.568 and the fresh table to 0.715, and the fresh table's clock state differs from its idle by $-0.0045$ [$-0.024$, $+0.016$] in fidelity. Putting the ten-day table's total phase back at the end, after the second controlled rotation, gives 0.631. The in-circuit ten-day correction is below that end correction by 0.063 [0.044, 0.087] on ibm\_kingston and above it by 0.058 [0.034, 0.082] on ibm\_marrakesh.

\subsection{How long the correction lasts}
On ibm\_kingston the ten-day and fresh tables cannot be told apart on the untouched qubits within $\pm0.03$ in slope: the difference of their regression slopes is $-0.01$ [$-0.03$, $+0.02$]. On the clock the ten-day table gives back 0.76 and the fresh one 1.00. Defined after the data, three readings: the kick on the 117 untouched qubits read on 31 October scales to the ten-day table by 0.96 [0.85, 1.00] (Theil--Sen, 99\,\%), but two clock qubits moved. Qubit 3 went from $-3.0$ to $-4.9$ and qubit 23 from $-18.4$ to $-31.2$ degrees per readout at four readouts, while qubit 16 stayed at $-7.2$ and $-7.3$ (point values of the two tables). A chip-wide median or slope does not show a few qubits that move.

On ibm\_marrakesh the ten-day table over-corrects. Its median residual is $+0.85$ [$+0.55$, $+1.45$] degrees per readout, on the other side of zero from the look's $-1.68$, its regression slope is $-0.38$ [$-0.64$, $-0.20$], and the difference from the fresh table's slope is $-0.41$ [$-0.62$, $-0.23$]. On the pairs its residual is $+3.2$ [$+2.0$, $+4.2$] degrees per step. On the clock it moves the state further from its idle than the look does (squared distance 0.72 against 0.49), although the clock's fidelity to the ideal state rose from 0.601 to 0.656, a recovered share of the fidelity loss of 0.42 [0.27, 0.57]. The fresh table, measured minutes before, gives back 0.80 of the kick, with a slope of $+0.03$ [$-0.02$, $+0.07$], and 0.92 of the pair phase. Defined after the data: the kick read on 31 October scales to the ten-day table by 0.52 [0.42, 0.64] qubit by qubit, and 84\,\% of the untouched qubits kick less than they did. Defined after the data: the kicks read at four and at eight readouts differ by more than two degrees per readout on 8 (ibm\_kingston) and 12 (ibm\_marrakesh) of the 117 qubits in the ten-day table, and on 7 and 8 in the fresh one, so a table is read at the dose it is used at.

\subsection{What does not come back}
A change of frame cannot change coherence. On ibm\_kingston the untouched qubits' coherence after eight looks is below the idle's by 0.006 [0.001, 0.012] without correction and by 0.006 [0.0002, 0.011] with the fresh table, and the ten-day correction leaves it where the look left it (difference $+0.001$ [$-0.004$, $+0.006$]). On ibm\_marrakesh no coherence reading resolves (points between $-0.009$ and $-0.005$). The pairs' X-part concurrence is below the idle's on ibm\_kingston by 0.014 [0.002, 0.023] with the ten-day correction, and by 0.006 to 0.009 in the other arms, which do not resolve. On ibm\_marrakesh it is below by 0.0075 [0.001, 0.020] under the look and by 0.010 [0.005, 0.021] with the echo. Pairs whose idle concurrence is zero are left out of these readings: 2 of 48 on ibm\_kingston and 6 (3 against the idle-echo arm) on ibm\_marrakesh. Defined after the data: the squared distance from its idle that the fresh and echo arms leave on the clock sits on the shot-noise floor on ibm\_kingston (fresh 0.032, echo 0.040, floor 0.038, against 8.49 for the look) and above it on ibm\_marrakesh (fresh 0.14, echo 0.10, floor 0.035, against 0.49 for the look). The ten-day arm leaves 2.07 on ibm\_kingston and 0.72 on ibm\_marrakesh. The marrakesh clock's fidelity stays below its idle by 0.07 to 0.08 in every corrected and echoed arm.

\section{Discussion}

Against the stated threshold of more than half, every ibm\_kingston cell passes with either table: about nine tenths of the kick on the untouched qubits and of the pair phase is given back, and all of the clock state's distance from its idle with the fresh table. On ibm\_marrakesh the ten-day table fails it on all three setups (0.24, 0.40 and $-0.51$, no band above 0.5), and the fresh table passes on the untouched qubits and the pairs while the clock does not resolve. A symmetric echo gives back a similar share (0.91 to 1.00 where it resolves), at the cost of two $X$ gates on every untouched qubit. No test of the echo against the correction was committed. The correction itself is a change of frame and costs no time and no gate.

The table does not stay good on its own. On ibm\_kingston a table ten days old could not be told apart from a fresh one on the untouched qubits as a whole, and gave back 0.76 on the clock, whose qubits include two that moved. On ibm\_marrakesh the kicks read on 31 October were about half those of ten days earlier by a per-qubit scale defined after the data, and the old table over-corrected. A table measured minutes before, in the same session, gave the kick back on both chips.

What this does not show: it names no mechanism for the kick or for its change between the two dates, and one ten-day gap on two chips is the only measure of how long a table lasts. It covers one readout pack of 39 qubits read from $|0\rangle$ (Kan and coworkers report a phase that depends on the state of the qubit being read \cite{Kan2026}), and the clocks were chosen for large kicks. The bands treat qubits as independent, the clock bands are shot noise on one triple per chip, and each chip is read alone. The marrakesh clock's fresh-table and echo readings cannot tell, because the two copies disagree. Defined after the data: on our 24 October data the 21 October table rotated a looked-at clock's tomography back to within 0.002 of its unlooked fidelity, with the look at the end of the build, where a frame correction is algebra on the tomography. That check is not a result of this run.
