\title{Papers Against Themselves: A Two-Reader Errata Index}

\author{K. Indahl}
\email{Indahl@heuremen.com}
\affiliation{Heuremen LLC}

\date{November 13, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Published papers can contradict themselves. A caption interval may not match the paper's own released samples, table percentages may not follow from the table's own counts, and a table row may disagree with the text. Replication does not look for these, because finding them needs no outside data. We index them. Every paper with an arXiv identifier among the gravitational-wave papers that cite the open-data releases of the Gravitational Wave Open Science Center was read under one procedure, with the readers not told the authors' group. Of these, 277 of 942 outside papers (0.29, 95\% interval 0.27 to 0.32) and 9 of 34 papers from the LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA collaboration (0.26, 0.15 to 0.43) carry at least one erratum that two automated readers confirmed, or 0.32 and 0.28 counting only papers whose tables could be read. The collaboration rate is lower by 0.03, with a 95\% interval from $-0.15$ to $+0.14$, so whether the groups differ cannot be told; the index is a list of corrections and not a ranking of authors. Theory papers carry fewer (0.15 of outside theory papers) than reanalysis (0.32) and methods papers (0.36). Text that contradicts the paper's own table, caption or file is the most common finding. The rate depends on the procedure: on 126 papers read both ways, an earlier procedure finds 15 papers (0.12) and the full-slice procedure 43 (0.34), and run twice on 24 papers one procedure changes its call on 4. In one worked example the paper's headline bound recomputes from its released posterior samples while its stated 90\% interval does not. Errors can run both ways: the drafting reader's recall is not measured, and the counted findings rest on two automated readers, with a third re-reading a sample.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two bodies of work set the current standard for checking empirical results against data. Chen and Zimmermann built a public set of code and data that reproduces nearly all of the cross-sectional stock return predictors they study \cite{ChenZimmermann2022}. Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen studied factors across 93 countries and report that the majority of asset pricing factors can be replicated \cite{JKP2023}. Each takes a paper's claim, rebuilds the result from public data and compares the rebuilt number with the printed one.

A paper can also disagree with itself before any data is touched. A percentage in a table can fail to follow from the counts printed beside it. An interval in a caption can differ from the interval the paper's own released posterior gives. A row's components can fail to sum to the row's total. An abstract can headline the best of many runs with no spread beside it. Replication checks whether a result rebuilds from data. It does not check whether a paper's text, tables and captions agree with each other.

Several groups check papers for internal errors of this kind. statcheck recomputes reported $p$-values from their test statistics in psychology papers \cite{Nuijten2016}. The GRIM test asks whether a reported mean is possible given integer data and the sample size \cite{BrownHeathers2017}, and SPRITE reconstructs possible samples from summary statistics \cite{Heathers2018}. Two projects use language models to look for errors in published papers. As reported in 2025, the Black Spatula Project had analysed about 500 papers and contacts authors privately rather than publishing, and YesNoError, funded by its own cryptocurrency, had analysed more than 37,000 papers in two months according to its founder \cite{Gibney2025}.

\paragraph*{What this work adds.} We define a small set of internal-consistency codes, freeze which code may fire on which type of paper before reading, and apply them to a complete, defined slice of physics papers with a control arm drawn from the same slice. Each listed finding needs two readers to agree, carries a verbatim quote and location, and, for arithmetic, a recompute from the paper's own printed numbers or released data. We call a place where a paper disagrees with itself an erratum candidate. The label is for indexing. It is not an assessment of the study or its authors, and we do not claim that any paper's conclusions are wrong.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{The slice}

The slice is the set of papers that cite the open-data papers of the Gravitational Wave Open Science Center \cite{GWOSC2021,GWOSC2023,Vallisneri2015}, taken from NASA ADS for publication dates from September 2015 to the freeze date. It holds 1,042 papers in two arms. The outside arm holds 1,003 papers. The control arm holds 39 papers from the LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA (LVK) collaboration, defined as papers with more than 100 authors or with a LIGO, Virgo or KAGRA collaboration author. Papers are indexed by arXiv identifier and DOI. Every paper in the slice with an arXiv identifier is read, 976 of the 1,042, under one fixed procedure in which the readers are not told a paper's arm: 942 outside papers and 34 control papers. The other 66 have no arXiv identifier (61 of 1,003 outside, 5 of 39 control) and are not read.

\subsection{Paper types and codes}

Each paper is assigned a type from its title and abstract before any code is read: R, reanalysis (re-derives event or population numbers from public strain or released posteriors); M, methods (a new search, pipeline or classifier benchmarked on public strain); or T, theory and other (cites the open data but headlines no number recomputed from it). The map of which code may fire on which type was frozen on 12 November 2026, before any paper in the slice was read. Table~\ref{tab:codes} lists the four codes that are counted and published.

F3 & A detection, tension or new-signal claim with no significance or interval. & R, M \\
F5 & A printed number does not follow from the paper's own printed numbers, or a headline does not recompute from released data. & R, M \\

\hline\hline
\end{tabularx}

Three further codes are drafted and not counted: F1 (a reference built from the paper's own pipeline or injections), F4 (posteriors, samples, code or trigger list not released) and F7 (a simpler model or the standard pipeline does as well). F1 fires on 7 of 8 non-T control papers, because a paper's own background estimate is standard practice, so it does not sort papers. The convention form of F2, a standard upper-limit or continuous-wave headline, is excluded from the pages and the counts. Every paper receives one of PRESENT, ABSENT, CANNOT\_TELL or NOT\_APPLICABLE per code, and every ABSENT records the scope that was searched. Text against text, with no table, caption or file on either side, is CANNOT\_TELL under F6.

\subsection{Readers}

A drafting reader reads the paper text and proposes a cell for each code with verbatim quotes, machine-checked as substrings of the extracted text. A blind second reader receives one neutral task per drafted PRESENT, for example ``Compare the value stated in the text with the value in Table I'', without the drafting verdict, and returns a verdict, quotes, its arithmetic, the innocent explanations it checked and a confidence. A finding counts when the second reader returns the matching verdict with high or medium confidence. Neither reader is told the paper's arm, but the extracted text keeps the author list, which names the collaboration on control papers. Both instructions and the counting rule were committed to the code repository at 17:05 EST on 12 November 2026, before the full read and before the comparison in Sec.~\ref{sec:full}; the one later change to the drafting instructions (20:01) replaced the file used as a format example. The notes file the drafter reads changed once during the run (19:05), in the tier wording and not in the counted codes. This is the full-slice procedure, and Sec.~\ref{sec:full} reports it.

Outside batches 1 to 10 and the newest 12 control papers were also read under an earlier procedure, and the rates from it use the first 96 of these papers (outside batches 1 to 7 and the 12 control papers). Every drafted PRESENT was re-read against the paper by a third reader, which was not blind to arm, and the blind second reader then audited the survivors. The tiers and worked examples come from this hand-checked subset (Sec.~\ref{sec:rates}). All readers are automated; the Acknowledgments name them.

\subsection{Severity tiers}

Each confirmed finding takes its highest tier. T1, arithmetic or typo: a printed number does not follow from the paper's own printed numbers and no claim is built on it. T2, contradiction: the text and the paper's own table, caption, appendix or file disagree. T3, headline-affecting: the error sits in, or sets, a number the abstract states or a result the conclusion states as the paper's main result. F2 and F3 are T3 when the claim is in the abstract and T2 otherwise. The tiers were defined after the first 23 PRESENT rows had been read, and the T3 wording was narrowed after Example A was tiered (Sec.~\ref{sec:examples}); the tier was not changed.

\subsection{Receipts and flow}

A receipt states the data used, what the paper states, what was recomputed, the width of the recompute, the scope checked and a script that reproduces it. No paper is listed without a verbatim quote and, for F5, a receipt. A page is published and its authors are emailed the link the same day. Each page carries a correction and removal form and changes on request.

\section{Results}

A paper counts when at least one of F2 (non-convention), F3, F5 or F6 is confirmed on it. Intervals are 95\% Wilson score intervals, and differences between rates carry Newcombe 95\% intervals.

\subsection{The full slice}
\label{sec:full}

All 976 papers with an arXiv identifier were read under the full-slice procedure (Table~\ref{tab:full}). Of 942 outside papers, 277 carry a confirmed finding (0.29, [0.27, 0.32]), and so do 9 of 34 control papers (0.26, [0.15, 0.43]). The control rate minus the outside rate is $-0.03$ ($-0.15$ to $+0.14$). The interval contains zero and differences of 0.1 in either direction, so whether the arms differ cannot be told.

The rates differ by type more than by arm. Outside theory papers, which can carry only F6, have a rate of 0.15, against 0.32 for reanalysis and 0.36 for methods papers. Within types the control-minus-outside differences are $-0.33$ ($-0.38$ to $+0.003$) for R, $+0.07$ ($-0.15$ to $+0.32$) for M and $+0.12$ ($-0.06$ to $+0.42$) for T. A type-weighted (Mantel--Haenszel) difference is $-0.01$. Type T papers can carry only F6, and R and M papers can carry four codes. Counting F6 alone, the outside rates are 0.22 for R ([0.18, 0.27]), 0.23 for M ([0.19, 0.27]) and 0.15 for T ([0.11, 0.20]), so part of the gap by type is the number of codes that can fire. Rates by type were planned before the full read; the R comparison rests on 8 control papers with no finding and its interval reaches zero. Among the outside papers with a finding, 194 carry an F6, 80 an F5, 40 a non-convention F2 and 15 an F3. The nine control papers carry 5 F6 and 5 F5, and no F2 or F3.

The unreadable-table filter does not fall equally on the arms: 175 of 942 outside papers (0.19) and 2 of 34 control papers (0.06) had a table or file the drafting reader could not read. Unreadable papers stay in the denominators. Counting only readable papers gives 244 of 767 outside (0.32) and 9 of 32 control (0.28), a difference of $-0.04$ ($-0.17$ to $+0.14$), the same reading.

The second reader returned the matching verdict on 339 of the 372 drafted items it was given (0.91, [0.88, 0.94]). Of the 33 it did not confirm, 22 were the matching verdict at low confidence, 10 were CANNOT\_TELL and 1 was a different verdict. A third automated reader re-read 20 outside papers drawn at random (seed 20261112) from the 74 with a full-slice finding in batches 11 to 28, against the text, and 20 of 20 kept at least one confirmed finding ([0.84, 1.00]); of the 22 items in those papers, 21 were confirmed and one was CANNOT\_TELL. This sample speaks to precision. It does not measure recall.

\subsection{The two procedures paper by paper}

The 126 papers of outside batches 1 to 10 and the 12 control papers, less six papers named in the rule changes, were read under both procedures. Both procedures found something on 11 papers, neither on 79, only the earlier procedure on 4, and only the full-slice procedure on 32. An exact McNemar test on the 36 discordant papers gives $p = 2\times10^{-6}$. To see how far one procedure disagrees with itself, the full-slice procedure was run a second time on 24 papers (two batches of 12). The two runs agreed on 20 papers and split 2 and 2 on the other four, so the call on a single paper changes on 4 of 24 reruns (0.17, [0.07, 0.36]). Run twice, the procedure flips papers in both directions. Run against the earlier procedure, the flips go 32 to 4 in one direction, so the earlier procedure's rate (Sec.~\ref{sec:rates}) is lower than the full slice's and not a second estimate of the same quantity. On these 126 papers the earlier procedure finds 15 papers (0.12) and the full-slice procedure 43 (0.34). The 32 papers returned only by the full-slice procedure are findings the earlier drafting run did not propose. No third reader has re-read them, so they may be misses of the earlier procedure or over-calls of the full one.

The 20 audited items are every PRESENT re-read in the first ten outside batches and the control batch, excluding F1, F4 and convention-form F2 (Table~\ref{tab:agree}). The second reader agreed on 19. Those 19 are the findings in the index. \subsection{Three worked examples}
\label{sec:examples}

Quotes are verbatim from the text extraction the readers used.

\paragraph*{Example A: an interval that differs from the released samples (2603.29019, F6 and F5, T2).} The abstract and body state ``$\sigma < 19.7$ km/s (95\% credible upper bound)'' with a median of 3.9 km/s. The Figure 5 caption and the conclusions state ``$3.9^{+19.7}_{-3.3}$ km/s (90\% HDI)''. Read as offsets, as the paper's own entry ``$0.16^{+0.31}_{-0.16}$ (90\% HDI)'' confirms, the caption interval runs from 0.6 to 23.6 km/s. If 19.7 is the 95th percentile, a 90\% highest-density interval of the same single-peaked posterior with median 3.9 cannot reach 23.6. The authors released the samples (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.21221337, file \texttt{sigma\_posterior.h5}, 6,560 samples). Treating them as equally weighted, the median is 3.85 km/s and the 95th percentile 19.67 km/s, matching the paper, while the equal-tailed 90\% interval is 0.45 to 19.67 km/s and the 90\% HDI is 0.00 to 15.08 km/s. A bootstrap (2,000 resamples) gives [19.10, 20.42] km/s for the 95th percentile and [14.58, 15.52] km/s for the HDI upper edge; no draw reaches 23.6. If the samples are correlated draws these widths are understated, but the gap remains large. The headline bound recomputes, so the abstract is unaffected; the conclusions repeat the caption interval. Only the one released file was checked.

\paragraph*{Example B: percentages that do not follow from the counts (2510.14291, F5, T1).} The text states ``In this study, Omicron detected 45,345 glitch noises.'' Table 2 lists counts and percentages for eight classes. The counts sum to 45,345. Six rows recompute to rounding. Class 6 is printed ``60 (1.3\%)'' and class 7 ``27 (0.6\%)'', where 60/45,345 is 0.13\% and 27/45,345 is 0.06\%, each a factor of ten. The printed percentages sum to 101.7\%. Counts that would match the printed percentages (about 590 and 272) would break the stated total. No claim is built on the two values.

\paragraph*{Example C: a table row against the text (2303.17628, F6, T2).} The text states that the collaboration ``presents this population in Table 1 with 76 events consisting of 70 BHBH, 4 BHNS, and 2 NSNS mergers''. The paper's Table 1 row reads ``76 69 4 2''. The row's reference marker and its asterisk note, which says the population is a subset of a catalogue, explain neither 70 against 69 nor the row summing to 75.

\section{Discussion}

The codes that sort these papers are the ones a reader can check from the paper alone: a printed number against the paper's own printed numbers, and text against the paper's own table, caption or file. They find errata in the collaboration's own papers and in outside papers, and nothing here ranks either group. The quantitative-finance slice runs under the same procedure and will appear in a later version.

\paragraph*{Limitations.} (i) The full-slice findings are two-reader agreement between automated readers; a third reader re-read only a sample of 20 papers, and the re-read that sets Table~\ref{tab:rates} was not blind to arm. (ii) The drafting instructions changed during the run, all on 12 November 2026: F1 was narrowed and a version field added (batch 4); F6 was restricted to text against the paper's own table, caption or file and arithmetic on printed numbers was allowed under F5 (batches 5 and 6 and the control batch); F2 and F3 wording was tightened and a convention flag added (batches 7 to 10). The frozen code-type map did not change, and the rates mix instruction versions. (iii) Errors run both ways. Only drafted PRESENT cells are checked, and the drafting reader's recall is unmeasured; the counted full-slice findings rest on two automated readers at 0.91 agreement, with a third reader on a sample of 20 papers. On the same papers the two procedures return 15 and 43 papers with a finding, and 32 papers are returned only by the full-slice procedure. (iv) The tiers were defined after the first rows were seen, and the text-against-text finding is post hoc and kept out of the rates. (v) The second reader is a model of the same family as the drafting reader, so the two can share a blind spot, and 19 of 20 measures consistency, not accuracy against an outside standard. (vi) The slice is papers with arXiv identifiers citing one set of open-data papers, and is not a sample of physics; the intervals treat papers as independent, though control papers cluster by catalogue and observing run; the control arm is small (34 papers), and the hand-checked control papers are the newest 12 of 34, not a random draw. (vii) The full slice drafts every paper under the final instructions, so it does not mix instruction versions; Table~\ref{tab:rates} does.
