\section{Setup}
\paragraph{Bench.} Our fault bench (v2) asks a model 170 short questions in four failure families drawn from our earlier documents on
the failure modes of a language model doing measurement work \citep{faults,guards}: calling a winner between two scores whose intervals
overlap (P1), reporting an accuracy without a width (P2), confirming a wrongly computed percentage (P3), and reporting the best of several
seeds as the result (P4). Each family has scored items and controls with a known answer. Every model answers every item three times: a bare
prompt, an exact repeat of it (bare copy) and a guard wording that names the failure. Requests are sent one at a time at temperature zero
with a fixed seed (20261025), a context of 8,192 tokens and at most 1,500 new tokens, with the server's thinking switch off where a model has
one. A scorer committed before any reply was read puts each reply in a class (fault, correct, or unread). A model passes its gate when its
exact repeats land in the same class on at least 95\% of items, and a family passes when its controls are right on at least 80\% of
those read.

\paragraph{Models.} Five small open-weight models served by Ollama: phi4-mini, llama3.2:3b, qwen2.5:1.5b, granite4.1:3b and
ministral-3:3b. Each model's manifest digest (weights, template and parameters) is the same on every server we read.

\paragraph{Machines and servers.} A Mac with an Apple M5 Max (Metal), and a Windows workstation with an NVIDIA RTX 3080 10 GB (CUDA,
compute capability 8.6). Every server ran with flash attention off. On the Mac, three servers in turn, each with one parallel slot and one
model loaded at a time: the Homebrew bottle of Ollama 0.34.2 (the server Pub~\#2 used), and the official release builds of 0.34.2 and
0.34.3 from the Ollama repository, each checked against the digest the repository publishes. On the workstation, its official 0.34.3
install, once as it ran for Pub~\#2 (four parallel slots, started 29 October) and once restarted with one slot and nothing else changed.
Requests to the workstation went over the local network from the Mac.

\paragraph{Plans and readings.} Every run's plan was committed before its first request, and every change to a reading before it was
computed. For a pair of runs, $I$ is the set of items whose bare reply and bare copy are byte-identical within each run. A pair reads
consistent when no item of $I$ changes between the runs (with the exact 95\% upper bound on the share that could), above when any does,
and cannot tell when $I$ holds fewer than 100 items. Beside it we count byte-identical replies across the runs on the bare and guard arms,
and, on families whose gates pass in both runs, the items whose scored class changes, by direction.
