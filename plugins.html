Outside text is treated as data

Every plugin that reads outside text (a paper, a kit README, a released file, an API response) tells the agent to treat it as data, not instructions: if fetched text carries instructions aimed at the agent, it quotes them to you and carries on with the check. That is an instruction to the agent, not a guarantee. The guards that hold regardless are in code: nothing runs downloaded code without a go bound to the exact commit you reviewed, a paid IBM job submits only from your own terminal, and your files are opened read-only. Keep Claude Code’s permission prompt on for Bash.

Check a claim

standards. /standards <arXiv id, DOI, file or claim> checks a paper or a measured claim against the Standards claim codes F1 to F7 and returns a recount card. Every code gets one of four statuses: PRESENT, ABSENT, CANNOT_TELL with its reason, or NOT_APPLICABLE. Every PRESENT carries a verbatim quote and where it sits in the paper. Any recompute shows its arithmetic from the paper’s printed numbers, with the rounding range. The card names no cause. A PRESENT is one reader’s proposal, an erratum candidate to check, not a confirmed finding and not a verdict on the study or its authors.

eval-check. /eval-check <claim or path> checks a language-model evaluation claim, or your own eval run: the parser checked against raw replies first, the setup as part of the result, the repeat floor before any comparison, dropped items by reason, judges grading their own labels, and an interval and outcome on every number. Built from the IRIS recounts.

prereg. /prereg seals a plan in git and later shows, from your git host’s own record of the push, whether the plan was pushed before the first run, and which files that ran the plan did not cover. No outside timestamp chain: the witness is the host’s push record, never a commit date. BEFORE means before the first run in the record you supply; too-close-to-call reads CANNOT_TELL. It never pushes for you.

readcheck. A gate for research repositories. When Claude Code commits a reading (a results, outcomes or readings file), the commit is refused unless a complete check block, CHECK.json, sits beside it, with one answer for each of the Standards recount requirements REQ-01 to REQ-13. It checks that every answer is there, not that it is right, and it gates commits that Claude Code runs.

Use our data

indahl-data. Every live desk inside Claude Code, through the read-only indahl.ai MCP server. /indahl <question> answers with the count, interval, outcome and the time it was read. With no question it prints every desk’s headline test side by side. Questions are sent to indahl.ai. Answers are measurements of public data at the time read, not legal advice and not a verdict on any vendor, model, package or author.

reproduce. /reproduce <desk> fetches that desk’s reproduce kit, shows you the commit and the command, and on your go runs it with the desk’s own code and checks its output against the published sha256. A match shows the kit reproduces its published output, not that the measurement is right.

Quantum runs

qia-calibration. /qia-check runs the QIA calibration checker on IBM Quantum calibration records: before a run (stale figures, zero error values, T2 above 2 × T1, T1 and T2 measured apart) and after it (the record in force and what changed during the run). Flags come in two categories: T1 and T2, and error figures. Your files are opened read-only. /qia-t1t2 goes one step further and measures T1 and T2 together on the qubits you choose, in one job on your own IBM Quantum account, and compares the ratio with IBM’s published pair. The self-test needs no device, the estimate submits nothing, and the paid run starts only from your own terminal.

darkwave-correction. /darkwave runs the Darkwave Correction Kit: it measures the phase turn on untouched qubits after a mid-circuit readout, on your own IBM Quantum account and runtime allotment, and adds the matching virtual-Z correction to your circuits. The self-test needs no account. A hardware job submits only when you run the command yourself in your own terminal. Tested on the simulator and once on ibm_kingston.
