When the CDC flu forecast states an interval, how often does the reported count land inside it?

The CDC FluSight Forecast Hub collects forecasts of the weekly number of laboratory-confirmed flu hospital admissions for the United States, each state, Washington DC and Puerto Rico. Each week it publishes the FluSight ensemble, which its metadata describes as “a median of quantile outputs” of the eligible forecasts. CDC’s forecast page shows “prediction intervals, which indicate the bounds of uncertainty” and says these ensemble forecasts “have been among the most accurate for previous influenza forecasting efforts.”

The ensemble is published as quantiles. The quantiles at 2.5 and 97.5 percent state a 95 percent interval, and the quantiles at 25 and 75 percent state a 50 percent interval. This page sets each interval beside the count that was later reported for that place and week, for forecasts of the current week and up to three weeks ahead, from 2023-10-14 to 2026-11-07. Below, the outer interval is the stated 95 percent interval and the middle interval is the stated 50 percent interval. It reads the count two ways. The latest count is the one in the newest release and is the primary reading. For the newest weeks it is also the only release. The first reported count is the one in the earliest release the hub keeps, used when that release came out within 14 days of the week’s end. The hub keeps no releases for some summer months, the longest gap being 203 days. This page does not say why a count lands outside. The plan, with the rules and the known-good checks, was fixed before any interval was set beside a reported count. All four known-good checks passed on this run.

One row is one forecast: a place, a week and a horizon. The places are the United States as a whole, the 50 states, Washington DC and Puerto Rico. Inside means the reported count is at or between the two stated quantiles. Above and below are printed apart, and a well-calibrated interval leaves about the same share on each side. Cannot tell is never folded into either side. Rows are not independent. Every place in a week shares one flu season, and forecasts made at different horizons share target weeks. So the interval is a moving-block bootstrap over target weeks, in blocks of 4 consecutive weeks, 2,000 draws, and it is much wider than an interval that treats rows as independent. Reading: below if the whole interval is under the stated level, above if it is over it, otherwise cannot tell (STRADDLES_EDGE) because the interval holds the stated level. The plan named no tolerance around the stated level, so no cell reads consistent (plan addendum of 12 November 2026, Standards 2.0). A cell needs 30 scored rows and 8 target weeks, and with fewer it reads cannot tell (TOO_WIDE_TO_RESOLVE). The block length was fixed in the plan. Tried after the data, longer blocks widen the all-horizons interval for the outer interval to 79.9 to 94.9 percent with blocks of 8 weeks and 79.1 to 95.3 percent with blocks of 13 weeks, which read below and cannot tell (STRADDLES_EDGE).

Against the hub’s baseline model

The weighted interval score is a common score for quantile forecasts. It is the distance from the reported count to the median plus a penalty for each interval’s width and for counts outside it, and lower is closer. The hub runs a baseline model on the same weeks. Below is the ensemble’s total score over the baseline’s on the rows where both stated all 23 quantiles. The hub states no number for this ratio. Comparing it with 1 is our ruler. Below means the ensemble scored lower than the baseline. The ratio adds raw scores, so places with more admissions weigh more. The United States row carries 44.8 percent of the ensemble’s total score. Without it the ratio is 0.69 (0.57 to 0.78), and in 53 of 53 places the ratio is under 1. The hub’s own season rankings use a relative score on log-transformed counts, which this page does not reproduce.

What this page holds and what it leaves out

Every ensemble file in the hub is read, every place in it and horizons 0 to 3. The week before the reference date (horizon -1) is not scored. Its count can be released before the forecast is due. That leaves out 1,749 rows. Described and not scored: the median distance from the stated median to the latest count, as a share of the 95 percent interval’s width, is 0.06 at horizon 0 and 0.05 at horizon 3, where a positive value means the count came in higher. Set aside: rate-change categories, peak week and peak size, emergency department visit shares, sample trajectories, individual team models and seasons before 2023-24.

The hub states that the 2.5 and 97.5 quantiles “correspond to the central 95% Prediction Interval”.
