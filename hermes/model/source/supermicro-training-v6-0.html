StatementComparedStatedIn the fileOutcomeNote
“demonstrated linear scaling in training performance for 3 different models, scaling from 8 GPU to 72 B300 GPUs”Supermicro 8 x B300 and 72 x GB300 result sets, official-style time to train (olympic average with scaling factor), benchmarks with both: Llama 3.1 8B, Llama 2 70B LoRA, GPT-OSS 20Blinear scaling for 3 models, 8 to 72 GPUsllama31_8b 8 GPUs 74.787 min, 72 GPUs 11.860 min, speedup 6.31x for 9x the GPUs (70% of linear), 16 GPUs 55.050 min, speedup 1.36x for 2x (68% of linear). llama2_70b_lora 8 GPUs 6.674 min, 72 GPUs 1.173 min, speedup 5.69x for 9x the GPUs (63% of linear), 16 GPUs 6.259 min, speedup 1.07x for 2x (53% of linear). gpt_oss_20b 8 GPUs 84.283 min, 72 GPUs 18.186 min, speedup 4.63x for 9x the GPUs (51% of linear)BELOWLinear would cut time to train by 9x. The file shows 51% to 70% of that. The post does not define performance, and larger runs change batch size. Time to train is the primary MLPerf metric.
“2 systems of HGX-B300 showed even better performance than the linear scaling for HGX-B300 to NVL72”Llama 3.1 8B, 16 GPU entry against the straight line (linear axes) and the power-law line (log axes) joining the 8 and 72 GPU entries16 GPUs above the 8 to 72 GPU scaling line16 GPUs give a 1.36x speedup over 8 GPUs. The line gives 1.66x (linear axes) or 1.79x (log axes)BELOWThe 16 GPU point sits under the line on both axis choices. The post does not say which axes.
“the 2 systems of HGX-B300 follow the linear scaling of the HGX-B300 to NVL72”Llama 2 70B LoRA, 16 GPU entry against the same two lines16 GPUs on the 8 to 72 GPU scaling line16 GPUs give a 1.07x speedup over 8 GPUs. The line gives 1.59x (linear axes) or 1.73x (log axes)BELOWThe post calls the model Llama 3.1 70b. The benchmark in the repository is llama2_70b_lora.
