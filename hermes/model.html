Model claims against the models (rounds ended 30 Oct)

Open-weight models state results in their own cards. Each round reruns one model's stated results with EleutherAI lm-evaluation-harness 0.4.13 through mlx-lm 0.32.2 on Apple silicon, on the original bf16 weights, with the shots the card gives. Where the card does not say whether the prompt uses the chat template or the few-shot examples as turns, we run the variants and read the stated value against every one. The ± after a value is 1.96 harness standard errors plus the rounding of the card's printed figure, a 95% interval. A variant is marked CANNOT_TELL (parser) when more than 1 of 10 replies read in full disagree with what the harness extracted, and it no longer counts toward the outcome. The rules and the claim list are committed before the first run. A card row that the round did not run is listed as not run, with the reason. The plans, full reads, claim lists, scorers and raw outputs of every round are in the claims_stack folder on GitHub.

What vendors state and what the results files show

MODEL sets what a vendor or a paper states next to what the published files show. The vendor tables hold statements about computing hardware, set against the results MLCommons publishes and, for the Apple table, against readings from the MacBook Pro in our lab. Each row quotes the vendor’s sentence, names the file entries it points to, and ends in one of four outcomes. ABOVE and BELOW mean the file shows more or less than the statement. CONSISTENT means the file agrees within the rounding of the statement. CANNOT_TELL means the file cannot settle it. The numbers in the “In the file” column are computed by us from the public MLCommons files and the scripts to recompute them are below. The papers table is on its own page: Papers. A row whose items fall in both directions reads CANNOT_TELL.

Results by accelerator

Every closed-division datacenter throughput result (tokens, samples or queries per second) in MLPerf® Inference v5.0, v5.1 and v6.0 with an accelerator count: 1,727 rows in 569 groups of model, scenario and accelerator. Per-accelerator throughput is the result divided by the number of accelerators in the entry. That is how NVIDIA’s post computes it, and it is not an MLPerf primary metric. Where an entry’s accelerator name states its own count and the count differs from nodes times accelerators per node, the count in the name is used. Rows with no accelerator count, which are systems without an accelerator, are left out: 49 in v5.0, 61 in v5.1 and 24 in v6.0. Closed datacenter results that have an accelerator count and are given as a latency are also left out: 6 in v5.0, 0 in v5.1 and 7 in v6.0.
