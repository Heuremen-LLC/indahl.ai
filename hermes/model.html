Live: model claims against the models

Open-weight models state results in their own cards. Each round reruns one model's stated results with the published harness, on the original weights, with the settings the card gives. The rules and the claim list are committed before the first run. A claim the card states and the harness cannot reproduce in the same form is listed as not run, with the reason.

What vendors state and what the results files show

MODEL sets what a vendor or a paper states next to what the published files show. The vendor tables hold statements about computing hardware, set against the results MLCommons publishes and, for the Apple table, against readings from the MacBook Pro in our lab. Each row quotes the vendor’s sentence, names the file entries it points to, and ends in one of four outcomes. ABOVE and BELOW mean the file shows more or less than the statement. CONSISTENT means the file agrees within the rounding of the statement. CANNOT_TELL means the file cannot settle it. The numbers in the “In the file” column are computed by us from the public MLCommons files and the scripts to recompute them are below. The papers table after them sets what each study prints against what its own released data shows.

Results by accelerator

Every closed-division datacenter result in MLPerf® Inference v5.0, v5.1 and v6.0 with an accelerator count: 1,727 rows in 569 groups of model, scenario and accelerator. Per-accelerator throughput is the result divided by the number of accelerators in the entry. That is how NVIDIA’s post computes it, and it is not an MLPerf primary metric. Where an entry’s accelerator name states its own count and the count differs from nodes times accelerators per node, the count in the name is used. Rows with no accelerator count, which are systems without an accelerator, are left out: 49 in v5.0, 61 in v5.1 and 24 in v6.0.
