CENSUS · Official Statistics Against Their Revisions

117 of 117

Census states a 90 percent sampling interval with each first print. Later revisions are small next to it. For the second and third print, the 90th percentile of the miss, in stated half-widths, is 0.40 to 0.65 for the national housing series and 0.46 to 0.57 for advance retail. For the second print, the later value sat inside the interval for new home sales in 117 of 117 months, housing starts in 117 of 117 months, housing completions in 115 of 117 months and advance retail and food services sales in 112 of 115 months, each a share above 90 percent. In three months (September 2025, November 2025 and February 2026) the second print came in the same release as the first, so those rows show no revision. The interval states sampling error of the level, so a later value inside it is not a test of the stated width. The tables on the desk page hold every step.

What CENSUS is

CENSUS sets what the U.S. Census Bureau states about the uncertainty of its first estimates beside what the record shows later. CENSUS is the name of this division of Heuremen LLC. It is not the U.S. Census Bureau and the Bureau does not endorse it.

The first desk reads the 90 percent interval Census states for each first print of new home sales, housing starts, housing completions and advance retail sales against the revised values that followed. Its page is CENSUS Revisions, and the newest reading is in /census/live.json.

Sources: U.S. Census Bureau, Economic Indicators Time Series API and explanatory notes. Federal ReserveFollow Bank of St. Louis, ALFRED archival series. This product uses the Census Bureau Data API but is not endorsed or certified by the Census Bureau.
