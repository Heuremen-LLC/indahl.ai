Changes

What Heuremen LLC has built and shipped, and what is next. Every item has its live page and its plan.

Shipped

  • arXiv version drift on every indexed study (live, code). Flag when a later arXiv version changes the numbers or the claim we read, both versions shown. arXiv API, no key.
  • Support windows against release activity (live, plan and two addenda; an error in our first classifier caught by hand check before publishing). Stated end-of-life and support dates (endoflife.date) against actual release and support activity.
  • Package claims against the files (PKGLICENSE live: PyPI-stated license against the repository license, every disagreement opened by hand)
  • Hugging Face license claims against the license files (live, plan and four addenda: the planted-text gate and hand checks caught four reading errors before anything was shown). PyPI metadata (license, requires-python, stated contents) against what the release files hold.
  • Older corners onto heuremen_kit (Wilson interval moved to the kit in FORECAST, CADENCE, LAUNCH, CADA; an error in three of them found and corrected, see each plan). TIME, CADA, UPTIME, FORECAST, CADENCE move to the shared kit with summaries confirmed unchanged.
  • Second seat for LINKROT (plan addendum 2). Runner-network seat; a disagreement between seats reads BLOCKED.
  • T1 and T2 rerun (fez q148, plan note 4: T1 measured 0.82 then 1.05 microseconds against 5.86 published; T2 still unresolved, the echo decays faster than the first delay step; results). Interleaved circuits and delays sized to the measured T1 (fez q148 measured T1 0.82 microseconds against 5.86 published).
  • Security claims against DNS records (SECRECORDS live, plan and note: an HSTS preload header against its own requirements, an SPF record against the 10-lookup limit; every mismatch opened by hand and the SPF counts repeated with a second script).
  • Stated model sizes against the weights (HFSIZE live, plan, addendum and notes: a reader error and an inconsistency in the Hugging Face API found by hand before anything was shown).
  • Vendor shutdown dates kept over time (CHANGE live, plan). Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Cloudflare tables; every move, addition and removal logged. The test of whether stated dates hold fills as dates pass.
  • Queue at each IBM processor (on QIA, plan). Pending jobs hourly beside the waits our own jobs saw.
  • Stopped trials in the last two years (TRIALS, plan addendum 3).
  • Hash-chained archive of our records (plan). OpenTimestamps proofs, Software Heritage, Internet Archive, and Zenodo copies of the repositories.
  • Stated margin against later revision (REVISE live, plan and note). Monthly payroll first estimates against the BLS's stated interval, from the Philadelphia Fed's real-time data. No prices.
  • The Fed's stated projection ranges against outcomes (FEDSEP live, plan, addendum and notes). September projections 2015 to 2025 against the 70 percent error ranges the Fed publishes. No rates or prices of ours.
  • Every test side by side (STACK live, plan and addendum). The headline test of every live page in one table, ordered by outcome, no reading of its own.
  • An outside seat reads our own pages (SEAT live, plan). A GitHub runner compares every page with the repository and every feed with its limit.
  • Install commands against resolution (PKGINSTALL live, plan, two addenda and a note: a placeholder the reader missed and commands that did not install the project, both caught by hand before anything was shown).
  • Our data as SQL tables (dataset heuremen-llc-data.heuremen_public, plan and addendum). Every trend history, the hash chain and the STACK rows in BigQuery, readable by any signed-in Google account, row counts read back after each load.
  • MLCommons AILuminate Safety results, kept and checked (AILUMINATE live, plan and note). The results page against its own reports, and every read kept so a later change to a published score shows. Release month against Hugging Face is next.
  • The documents behind OY, kept (CAMPUS live, plan and addendum). Every external source cited on the OY page, read and kept pass after pass so a moved, changed or removed page shows. Next for OY: the school list to every degree-granting US institution, then plans to measure against results published.
  • One model, many hosts (HOSTS live, plan). Every open-weight model on OpenRouter, every host serving it, stated quantization and uptime, polled every 12 minutes and kept. The stated one-day uptime is checked against our own 5-minute record once 24 hours exist.
  • Model names against their weights (WEIGHTS live, plan). The most downloaded open-weight models read pass after pass. The first pass is read and the comparison starts with the second.
  • Hospital price-transparency files (HPT live, plan and two addenda). The cms-hpt.txt at the root of each hospital website, the file it links, and the template header. Hand check found non-subjects in the list and eleven reading errors in the first read, both corrected in the addenda.
  • Scheduled flight time against flown time (AIRLINE live, plan and two addenda). Bureau of Transportation Statistics record, January to August of 2019, 2025 and 2026, routes as the independent units, every ABOVE hand-checked on its largest routes.
  • Air quality sensors against EPA monitors (AIRQ live, plan and note). PurpleAir outdoor sensors within 200 metres of EPA continuous monitors, June to September 2025, against the EPA's own sensor performance targets.

Next

  • A second outside seat on a different network. One runner is one seat.
  • New Zenodo versions every month (script written and tested; first scheduled run after the next release).
  • Amazon Braket deviations table. Parked: needs a funded account.
  • Boston T1 and T2. Job queued at IBM since the 29th, waits behind hundreds of pending jobs.
  • ATLAS interconnection queues. Large-load interconnection queues against announced data center MW, onsite generation permits, a live grid layer.
  • ISP broadband labels against M-Lab. Access to the M-Lab tables works. A label states a typical speed for one plan, and M-Lab measures every plan of a network together, so a fair test needs single-plan providers or a plan mix we do not have. The plan will name which providers qualify before any measurement is read.
