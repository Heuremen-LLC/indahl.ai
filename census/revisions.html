Division: CENSUS

When the Census Bureau states how uncertain a first print is, does the later value stay inside?

This product uses the Census Bureau Data API but is not endorsed or certified by the Census Bureau.

The Census Bureau publishes advance monthly estimates of new home sales, housing starts and completions, and retail and food services sales. Each comes with a sampling uncertainty, and each month is then revised. This page sets the first print of each month, with the 90 percent interval Census states for it, beside the value the same month carries after one revision, after two, and today.

What the stated width is

For sales, starts and completions the Census API field E_TOTAL is the estimated relative standard error in percent, rounded to a whole number. Census prints it as the average relative standard error for the latest six months. Census gives the 90 percent interval as 1.645 times the standard error. For advance retail the field E_SM is the median estimated coefficient of variation Census gives for the monthly total, in percent. Census gives the interval as the estimated value plus or minus 1.753 times the coefficient of variation times the value. Quotes and links are in the desk notes.

Census states this width for sampling error of the level. Most of what moves a first print later is late reports and re-benchmarking, which sampling error does not describe, so a later value inside the interval is not a test of Census’s sampling error. Census states 90 percent as the share of its intervals that would hold the value a complete count gives, and a later revised estimate is not that value. Reading the share of later values inside the interval against 90 percent is our reading.

One row is one month. Inside means the later value sits inside the interval stated for the first print. A share is inside rows over inside, above and below rows. Cannot tell rows are shown beside every cell and are never part of a share. The interval is a 95 percent Wilson interval. Months overlap in what they measure, so the interval is narrower than it should be. A cell reads above when its interval lies over 90 percent, consistent when its interval holds 90 percent, below when it lies under, and cannot tell with fewer than ten scored rows. In three months the second print came in the same release as the first (September 2025, November 2025 and February 2026), so those rows show no revision. How big the revisions are

Median absolute revision from the first print to the second print: new residential sales 3.1 percent of the first print, or 0.22 stated half-widths, housing starts 1.2 percent of the first print, or 0.14 stated half-widths, housing completions 1.5 percent of the first print, or 0.16 stated half-widths, advance retail and food services 0.2 percent of the first print, or 0.19 stated half-widths. Moving the served width down and up by its rounding, half a point for sales, starts and completions and 0.05 for retail, moves the share of second prints inside to (up, down): new residential sales 100.0 to 99.1, housing starts 100.0 to 100.0, housing completions 99.1 to 98.3, advance retail and food services 97.4 to 96.5 percent. These are described and not scored.

The 90th percentile of the miss, in stated half-widths, for the second and third print: new residential sales 0.54 and 0.65, housing starts 0.40 and 0.49, housing completions 0.46 and 0.47, advance retail and food services 0.46 and 0.57. The stated interval is several times the size of a typical revision. Cutting every stated half-width to two thirds would leave the national cells at 90.5 to 99.1 percent inside. Cutting them to half would take them to 75.2 to 94.9 percent. These two lines are computed in code after the data and are described and not scored.

The retail revisions

There are two retail revisions in the record. The vintage of 2025-04-25 is the April 2025 annual revision, in which Census removed nonemployers from the series. It changed 159 months by a median of 1.8 percent and at most 2.2 percent. The vintage of 2026-09-28 is the annual revision Census announced in its advance retail release of 15 October 2026 (CB26-165): “Monthly retail sales estimates were revised on September 28, 2026 based on historical corrections and the results of the 2023 and 2024 Annual Integrated Economic Survey.” It changed 140 months by a median of 0.1 percent and at most 4.7 percent. The sealed rule in the plan, a change of more than 3 percent in a month at least 14 months older than the newest, caught only the second. First prints before 2026-09-28 are not compared with values from after it, and every retail current-value row falls there.

Annual revisions

A second or third print is not scored when an annual revision vintage falls between the first print and that print. Such rows are counted as cannot tell. The current value is the annual revised one by design. The rows dropped for an annual revision are the larger revisions. Counted in, advance retail would read second print 124 of 128 inside (92.2 to 98.8 percent, above) and third print 118 of 127 inside (87.1 to 96.2 percent, consistent). The national housing cells read the same with those rows counted in.

What this page holds and what it leaves out

Widths. Census serves the current width of each month and does not keep the width as printed at the time. Our own dated copies of the Census response began on 2026-11-11. Until a month has a copy made within three days of its release, its width is the one served now, and the table says so in the Width column. 0 rows have a width as printed. The history is read with widths as served in 2026 and is labelled that way everywhere.

Dates. First prints and later values come from the St. Louis Fed ALFRED archive, one vintage per call. A vintage date is the day ALFRED first recorded the values. It stands in for the Census release date. In the release texts we compared, the two dates were the same. The first print of a month is the earliest vintage that holds it, from January 2016 vintages on. The second print is the value in the vintage where the next month first appears, and the third print the value in the vintage where the month after next first appears. The retail release text before 2026 was not read.

The frame has 5,031 rows from 2016-01 to 2026-09 across all series and steps. The plan was fixed before the comparison, and its seal and the run code are in the public desk folder linked at the foot of this page. M3 durable goods orders, construction spending and international trade are copied by the snapshot job and are not read on this page.
