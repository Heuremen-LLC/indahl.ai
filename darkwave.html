DARKWAVE

A readout’s kick is a turn, and most of it can be given back.
Wayfinder and Bones.
31 October 2026.

Darkwave Correction Kit

Measure the turn a mid-circuit readout gives the untouched qubits next to it on your own IBM account, and add the matching virtual-Z correction to your circuits. Tested on ibm_kingston: the correction gave back the turn, with both hardware runs included.

Download What is in it

Reading out 39 qubits in the middle of a circuit on an IBM Heron processor turns the phase of the 117 qubits it never touches, by a median of about two degrees per readout, and by more than ten degrees on some of them (defined after the data). A turn can be undone. We gave most of it back with a virtual Rz of each untouched qubit’s own measured kick after every readout, the correction Krinner and coworkers proposed in 2022, and compared it with a symmetric echo and with idles of the same scheduled length in the same job.

Brackets give 99 % bootstrap bands, over qubits for the untouched qubits, over pairs for the Bell pairs and over shots for the clock state. A share of 1 means all of the readout’s effect was given back, 0 means none, and below 0 means worse than no correction. “Copies disagree” means the two copies of the job read differently, so the cell cannot tell.

Giving it back

Two correction tables were used, one measured ten days earlier and one measured minutes before, on three setups taken from published work: all untouched qubits on the equator, 48 untouched Bell pairs, and a Page–Wootters history state with the readout in the middle of its build. A symmetric echo, two X gates on every untouched qubit and no table, was run beside them.

How long it lasts

On ibm_kingston a table ten days old could not be told apart from a fresh one on the untouched qubits as a whole, and gave back 0.76 on the clock state, whose qubits include two that moved (defined after the data). On ibm_marrakesh the median kick read on 31 October was −1.68 [−2.34, −1.02] degrees per readout against −2.39 ten days earlier, and the ten-day table over-corrected: its residual landed on the other side of zero. A table measured minutes before, in the same session, gave the kick back on both chips.

That is one ten-day gap on two chips. So we fire the same tables again on later days and read each against a table measured less than a minute before that fire, on all 117 untouched qubits. Each fire is one point. A table holds when its share given back is within 0.05 of the fresh table’s across the whole band, in both copies of the job. A share is a median over the 117 qubits, and a median can hide a few qubits that move. A table error of about a quarter of a degree per qubit would mostly not be seen by this ruler. Changes of one degree read below in 79 of 80 planted tests.

What does not come back

A change of frame cannot change coherence. On ibm_kingston the untouched qubits’ coherence after eight readouts sits below the idle’s by 0.006 without correction and by 0.006 with the fresh table, and the ten-day correction leaves it where the readout left it. The Bell pairs’ X-part concurrence sits below the idle’s by 0.014 [0.002, 0.023] with the ten-day correction, and by 0.006 to 0.009 in the other arms, which do not resolve.

What this does not show

It names no mechanism for the kick or for its change between the two dates. It covers one readout pack of 39 qubits read from |0⟩, the clock qubits were chosen for large kicks, and each chip is read alone. The bands treat qubits as independent, and the clock-state bands are shot noise on one triple of qubits per chip.

Paper, data and code

Paper: A Readout’s Kick Is a Turn: Giving It Back, and How Long the Correction Lasts, submitted to arXiv (quant-ph) on 1 November 2026. Data and code: Zenodo, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23843468 · code and run files (git). For the main run the plan, code and synthetic tests were fixed and timestamped before any job was submitted.

The questions came from published work: Santos and Uzdin (equal delays for spectator qubits), Chu and coworkers (a three-readout instrument on ibm_kingston), Krinner and coworkers (the virtual-Z correction), Page and Wootters, Moreva and coworkers, Coppo, Cuccoli and Verrucchi (clocks made of correlations), and Kumar (spectators under extra measurements). Every reference is in the paper.
