Is a cycle that is stated as supported still being released?

Vendors state how long each release cycle of a product is supported. This page takes those stated dates from endoflife.date, which collects them from the vendors, and holds them against the date of each cycle's latest release. A supported cycle counts as unreleased when it was released more than 400 days ago and its latest release is more than a year old. Unreleased is not abandoned: a vendor can patch through a channel the release date does not capture. The page counts what the record shows.

The first row is the test whose rules were committed before the first read. Its interval treats whole products as the independent units, because the cycles of one product are not independent. The two product-level rows were defined after the first read and are labelled as such. The plan and its three dated addenda are in the public repository.

Named here and want to answer a row? Respond

Supported, as the plan states it: the stated end of life is in the future or not stated, the stated support end, if any, has not passed, and the cycle was released more than 400 days ago. Cycles with no latest release date, no release date, an end of life stated only as passed, or a support end that has passed are counted apart and not tested. Until 10 November 2026 the code still tested a cycle whose support end had passed when its end of life was in the future. The plan wins, and the count under the old rule is shown once beside the first row (plan addendum 3). The dates are as endoflife.date gives them and are not checked against the vendors. In the list of unreleased cycles, False in the end-of-life column means no end of life is stated.

Outcome rule, moved to Faults and Guards 2.0 by a plan addendum on 12 November 2026: above means the lower end of the 95 percent interval of the unreleased share is at or above 5 percent. Consistent means the whole interval lies below 5 percent. Anything else is cannot tell, with a reason beside it. A share of 10 percent is the size that matters, so an interval that holds both zero and 10 percent reads TOO_WIDE_TO_RESOLVE, and so does any row with fewer than 30 cycles or products. An interval that overlaps the 5 percent edge reads STRADDLES_EDGE. Outcomes recorded before that date stand under the rule they named. REQ-13 beside each headline row gives the products that returned nothing and the share of cannot tell by reason. The first row is judged on its interval over products. The Wilson columns, which treat cycles as independent, are shown beside it and are not what the outcome uses.

Plan, code and trend history for this page are public: support. Every plan was committed before the data it describes.
