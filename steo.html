Division: HERMES

When EIA states a price interval, how often does the price land inside it?

Each monthly Short-Term Energy Outlook from the U.S. Energy Information Administration charts the WTI crude oil price and the Henry Hub natural gas price with a “95% NYMEX futures price confidence interval”, an upper and a lower bound for each coming month. The chart note says the interval is “derived from options market information for the five trading days ending” a stated date, and that “Intervals not calculated for months with sparse trading in near-the-money options contracts.” The January 2012 outlook describes the bounds as “the lower and upper limits of a 95-percent confidence interval for the market’s expectations of monthly average WTI prices”. Since October 2025 the outlook says: “We have temporarily suspended our publication of confidence intervals.”

The numbers behind each chart are in the outlook’s chart gallery workbook. EIA serves only the current one, so this page reads the copies the Internet Archive made at the time, from the Nov 2011 issue to the Aug 2025 issue. Each stated interval is set beside EIA’s monthly average spot price for that month, WTI at Cushing and Henry Hub. Only months that began after the issue was released are scored. Lead is how many months after the release month the month falls. This page does not say why a price lands outside. The plan, with the rules and the known-good checks, was fixed before any interval was set beside a price. All five known-good checks passed on this run.

One row is one issue, one price and one month. Inside means the monthly average spot price is at or between the two stated bounds. Above and below are printed apart, and a 95 percent interval leaves about 2.5 percent on each side. Rows are not independent. Many issues state an interval for the same month, and prices in neighbouring months move together. So the interval is a moving-block bootstrap over outcome months, in blocks of 12 consecutive months, 2,000 draws, and it is much wider than one that treats rows as independent. Reading: below if the whole interval is under 95 percent, above if it is over, consistent if 95 percent is inside an interval no wider than 15 points, otherwise cannot tell. A cell needs 30 scored rows and 24 outcome months.

What this page holds and what it leaves out

Every archived copy of the workbook is read, and copies with the same bytes are read once. The issue of a copy comes from the date in its chart note and EIA’s release table. Where two copies of one issue exist the earliest is used, and 0 values differed between copies. EIA states no interval for the release month or earlier, so no row is dropped for having begun before release (0 rows). The Internet Archive holds a workbook for 78 of the 163 issues released over this span. Described and not scored: 45 of the 60 WTI rows below the interval in the plan’s reading fall in outcome months from November 2014 to February 2016. As a check that both sides are the same price, each workbook’s own history column agrees with EIA’s current monthly spot series within 2 percent in 3,660 of 3,660 WTI months and 3,620 of 3,660 Henry Hub months before release. Set aside: issues with no archived workbook, the outlook’s own point forecast, other prices, and the probability workbooks.
