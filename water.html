When Reclamation projects a lake level, where does the lake end up?

Each month the Bureau of Reclamation publishes the 24-Month Study. It states where Lake Powell and Lake Mead will stand at the end of each coming month. Every study has a Most Probable run. Many also have a Probable Minimum and a Probable Maximum run. Reclamation describes the Most Probable inflow as a median that would be exceeded 50 percent of the time, the Minimum as exceeded 90 percent of the time and the Maximum as exceeded 10 percent of the time.

We read every study Reclamation lists from January 2000 on. Each projected end-of-month elevation is set against the elevation Reclamation's own database recorded at the end of that month. Months ahead counts from the month a study was issued to the month it describes.

Since January 2010 the Probable Minimum and Probable Maximum reports also state: “There is approximately an 80% probability that a future elevation will fall inside the range of the minimum and maximum inflow scenarios. Additionally, there are possible inflow scenarios that would result in reservoir elevations falling outside the ranges indicated in these reports.” The band test reads that sentence. Reading the Most Probable elevation as a median is our reading, since Reclamation describes the Most Probable inflow, not the elevation, as a median. A gap between a projection and the measured level is a gap. This page does not say why it happened. The plan, with the rules and the known-good checks, was fixed before any projection was set against a measured level. Two dated addenda record what changed, and no rule or threshold changed.

Inside means the measured end-of-month elevation lies between the Probable Minimum and Probable Maximum projections issued in the same month. Above and below mean outside that range. Each interval is a 95% Wilson interval counted on water years, because months in one water year share weather. It treats water years as independent, and at leads past about six months neighbouring water years are correlated. Band outcome: above if the lower bound is over 80 percent, below if the upper bound is under 80 percent, consistent if the interval lies inside 70 to 90 percent, cannot tell otherwise or with fewer than 10 water years. Most Probable outcome: the same rule around 50 percent, with 40 to 60 percent for consistent. With about 20 water years in a cell no interval is narrow enough to read consistent, so a cell reads a miss or cannot tell. Error is the measured elevation minus the Most Probable projection, in feet.
