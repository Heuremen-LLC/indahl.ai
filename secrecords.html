Do a domain's security records do what they say?

A site that sends the HSTS preload directive asks to be put on browsers' preload lists. Among the list's requirements, the base domain must send a max-age of at least one year and includeSubDomains, and a redirect it sends must carry the header itself. A domain that publishes an SPF record states which servers may send its mail, and the standard allows an evaluation ten DNS lookups before it fails. This page reads both records for the most visited domains and compares each with the rules for what it asks for. It does not say whether a domain is on a preload list or whether its mail is delivered.

The plan was committed before the first domain was read. Every mismatch was read in full, and the count still waiting for this is shown above the tables. Every SPF count was repeated with a second tool and a second script. Until 9 November the header was read on the page a redirect lands on, often another host. The list asks for the header on the base domain's own response, a redirect included, so the reader now follows no redirect, and every domain was read again (plan, Addendum 2).
