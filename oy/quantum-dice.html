Quantum Dice

What it is


A die with a little screen on every face. Inside: a tiny computer, a sensor that knows which way is up, and a random generator. You use two of them at once.


The colours tell you the direction: yellow faces front and back, red left and right, blue top and bottom. The black frame is soft plastic, so it can take a fall.


They were made to turn superposition, entanglement and secret quantum codes into something you can hold and throw.



How to play


    

  • Classic mode is a normal die: every face has its own number.
    • 

  • Quantum mode: every face shows all six numbers at once. Roll it, and only the face that lands on top picks one.
    • 

  • Roll it again so the same colour axis lands up, and it remembers its number. Land on a different axis and it picks again.
    • 

  • Entangle the two, then roll one. If the other lands on the same colour axis, the two numbers add up to 7. On a different axis, it’s random.
    • 




The secret


Real entangled particles don’t send each other messages. These dice do: a tiny radio tells the second die what the first one rolled. The rules they follow are the real rules of quantum physics. The radio is the trick.


Scientists have a test that catches the trick, called a Bell test. One day you might run one.



Inside


Tick See inside. The green board behind the back yellow face is their real circuit board: the silver block is the ESP32-S3 computer, the tiny chip near the edge is the motion sensor that knows which face is up, and the six white plugs are for the six round screens. The grey block in the front yellow face is the battery.
