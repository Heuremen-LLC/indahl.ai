Tesla

The real Tesla

Nikola Tesla was born in 1856 in a small village in what is now Croatia. He came to New York in 1884 with almost nothing, worked for Thomas Edison for a short while, and then went his own way.

His big idea: electricity that flips back and forth, called alternating current, and a motor with no brushes that it could spin. In 1895 his system ran the power plant at Niagara Falls, and the next year it sent power all the way to the city of Buffalo. Almost every plug in your house runs on his idea today.

He built the Tesla coil, which throws long purple sparks, and dreamed of sending power through the air with no wires at all. His tower for it on Long Island was never finished, and it was torn down in 1917. He died in 1943 in a New York hotel room, with very little money. Today the unit scientists use to measure how strong a magnet is is called a tesla.

The spinning field

Here is the trick inside his motor. Three coils, three currents, each a little behind the one before. Nothing moves, but together their pull spins around in a circle (the black arrow). Put a metal rotor in the middle and it spins with it. Switch to steady current, the kind a battery gives, and the spin stops.

Three coils, three currents: there’s a triangle hiding inside almost every big motor in the world.
