The Three Body Problem

Two is easy. Three is not.

Two things pulling on each other with gravity, like the Earth and the Moon, follow a path you can work out with a neat formula. Add a third and there is no formula that works every time. A mathematician named Henri Poincaré showed that in the 1890s.

What you’re seeing

  • The figure eight is a special dance where three equal stars chase each other forever. Cris Moore found it in 1993, and two mathematicians proved it in 2000. It’s steady: nudge it a little and it keeps dancing.
  • Chaos starts three stars from a messy spot, twice: the second time (dotted) moved by a thousandth. For a few seconds they match. Then they don’t, and never will again.
  • That’s chaos: not random, every step follows the rules, but the tiniest change at the start becomes a different future.
