Riddles

Every riddle here has an answer that isn’t yes or no. Think first, then tap.

I get bigger the more you take away. What am I?

A hole. The thing that’s missing is the thing that’s there. Sometimes what isn’t there is the answer.

A light switch has two answers: on and off. How many does a dimmer have?

As many as you can turn it to. Two answers was a choice somebody made when they built the switch. It isn’t a rule of the world.

Two friends look at a number painted on the ground from opposite sides. One says 6. The other says 9. Who’s right?

Both of them. Each one is reading it truly from where they stand. Put their two answers together and now you know where the number is, and which way it faces.

Throw a ball straight up. At the very top, is it going up or coming down?

Neither. For one instant it’s doing neither. Between two directions there is a moment that is both stopped and on its way.

A spinning top is tipping a little. Is it leaning left or right?

Neither, while it spins. Some things aren’t one or the other until they stop. Asking too early just stops the spin.

Take a strip of paper. Give it half a twist and tape the ends together. How many sides does it have?

One. Draw a line down the middle without lifting your pencil and you’ll come back to where you started, on both “sides”. It’s called a Möbius strip.

Who’s taller, you or your shadow?

It depends on when you ask. At lunchtime your shadow is short. At sunset it’s taller than you. Time is part of the answer.

What’s the space between two notes in a song?

Part of the song. Take out the silences and you don’t have music anymore, just noise. The gap is doing work.

I’m not yes and I’m not no, and the best scientists say me all the time. What am I?

“I can’t tell yet.” That’s a real answer. It means the measuring isn’t good enough yet, and it’s much better than guessing.
