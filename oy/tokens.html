How a Computer Reads

Pieces

Computers that talk don’t read letters or words. They read pieces, called tokens. Nobody picks the pieces by hand: the computer finds the bits that show up most often and glues them together, one lesson at a time.

This one is real, and it’s learning on seven nursery rhymes right now. Slide to teach it.

Meet YO

YO is our little model. YO has all the same pieces a big model has. But the first time we trained YO, it learned to use just one of them: I. Every question, every time: I.

We paused YO to think about what to teach it next.

Guess the next piece

This is the whole game the big models are trained on, billions of times: look at the pieces so far and guess what comes next.

When the rhymes never had anything after a piece, it says so: I can’t tell.
