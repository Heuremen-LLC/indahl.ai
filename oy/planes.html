Paper Planes

The Dart

  1. Fold in half the long way, then open it again.
  2. Fold the top-left corner to the middle line.
  3. Fold the new left edge to the middle line.
  4. Fold in half along the middle, the folds on the outside.
  5. Fold each wing down along a line 2.4 cm up from the bottom fold, one wing on each side.

The Soft-Nose Glider

  1. Fold in half the long way, then open it again.
  2. Fold the top-left corner to the middle line.
  3. Fold the new left edge to the middle line.
  4. Fold the point back behind, about 3 cm: a soft nose.
  5. Fold in half along the middle, the folds on the outside.
  6. Fold each wing down along a line 2.4 cm up from the bottom fold, one wing on each side.

What the numbers say

The folding engine works out every fold before you make it. For these planes, on Letter paper: wings 16.8 cm across, about 203 square centimetres of wing, and the whole plane weighs about 6.6 grams.

The balance point sits about 1.5 cm ahead of the spot where the wings lift. That’s why it flies nose-first instead of flipping over. Try a paper clip on the nose, then on the tail, and see what changes.

Flight test

  • Throw each plane ten times. Same spot, same throw.
  • Write down every throw: how far it went, and which way it turned.
  • Put the bad throws right next to the good ones. The misses are part of the answer.
