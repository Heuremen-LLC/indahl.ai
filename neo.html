When JPL states how close an object will pass, where does the latest solution put it?

NASA's Center for Near-Earth Object Studies at JPL publishes a table of close approaches to Earth. For each approach it states a nominal distance, a minimum distance and a maximum distance. The data service defines the last two as the “minimum (3-sigma) approach distance” and the “maximum (3-sigma) approach distance”. Each row names the orbit solution it was computed from. The CNEOS page on uncertainties says: “Earth close-approach statistics for a given NEO can change (often by large amounts) as new data become available and the orbit is updated.”

JPL serves the current solution only. An earlier statement can be read only from a copy made at the time. This page uses the copies of the JPL close-approach service that the Internet Archive holds from January 2024 on, and the copies this desk has stored since 2026-11-10. Each stated range is set beside the distance from the latest solution for the same approach. The first statement used was copied on 2024-02-09 and the last on 2026-11-11.

JPL does not state how often a later distance should fall inside the range. Reading a 3-sigma range as one that holds about 99.7 percent of later values comes from a normal distribution and is our reading. CNEOS bases the minimum on a 3-sigma ellipse in the target plane. Under a normal distribution a point falls inside such an ellipse about 98.9 percent of the time, and every cell reads the same against 98.9 percent as against 99.7 percent. This page does not say why it moved. The plan, with the rules and the known-good checks, was fixed before any stated distance was set beside a later one. All four known-good checks passed on this run.

Inside means the whole 3-sigma range of the latest solution lies inside the range stated earlier. Farther and closer mean the whole latest range lies outside it. Judged on the later nominal distance alone, counting the rows that cross an edge, 501 of 517 are inside, which reads the same. This was added after the data. Cannot tell is never folded into either side. In the first row it holds 168 where the stated solution is still the latest one, 38 where the latest range crosses an edge of the stated range, 21 where the stated range is wider than 0.05 au, 1 where no current record of the approach was found. The interval is a 95% Wilson interval. Reading: below if the upper bound is under 99.7 percent, consistent if the lower bound is 98 percent or more, otherwise cannot tell. At this sample size a cell can read consistent only if nearly every row is inside. It takes about 190 scored rows, all inside, for the lower bound to reach 98 percent. The interval treats rows as independent. Rows from one copy share one moment of JPL's data, so the interval can be too narrow. The shift is the move of the nominal distance divided by one sixth of the stated range. It describes the move and is not a test.

What this page holds and what it leaves out

The copies are what the Internet Archive and this desk happened to store. They are not a random sample of approaches. One archived copy holds the full table out to the year 2200. It was made on 9 February 2024 and it gives 169 of the 479 scored rows and 13 of the 14 rows outside the range. Without that copy 309 of 310 scored rows are inside and the first row reads consistent, and the 0 to 7 day cell holds 186 of 186 and reads cannot tell. These readings without the copy were added after the data and decide nothing. All 91 scored statements made more than 365 days ahead are from the 9 February 2024 copy, and 80 are inside. From other copies more than 30 days ahead: 25 scored, 24 inside. This was added after the data. The statements in that copy were made months or years before the approach, for objects already known then. Objects found days before they pass appear only in the smaller copies. The JPL table itself lists only approaches whose 3-sigma time uncertainty is within 10 days and whose position uncertainty is within 0.1 au.

The latest solution is the delivered side. For an approach that has passed it may include observations made during and after the approach. It carries its own 3-sigma range, and that range is what is set against the stated one.

Set aside: Sentry impact probabilities and removals, which have no stated range to test and are stored on each run for later. Scout. Minor Planet Center circulars and orbits. Other agencies' lists. Orbit fits of our own. Approaches to other bodies, approaches before 2024 and stated distances beyond 0.05 au. Later statements of an approach are scored and kept in the data, and are not in the cells: 235 rows.
