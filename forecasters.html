FORECASTERS · Stated Against Delivered, One Axis

Do public forecasters get it about as right as they say?

Several of our desks set a public forecaster’s own stated figure beside what happened. This page puts cells from each desk on one axis, so the weather service and the lake projections, the Fed and the payroll estimates, and the asteroid distances can be read side by side. Each desk counts its own cells, and nothing on this page is counted again. Each desk’s section names its cells, and the full tables are on its page.

A stated figure comes in one of two kinds. A range with a stated coverage says how often the outcome should land inside it, or outside it. A probability forecast states a chance, and its reliability is how often the event happened when that chance was stated. On the axis each point is the share the desk counts minus the share the statement implies, in percentage points. Zero is a statement that held exactly. The bar is the desk’s own interval, moved by the same stated share. Each desk’s section says what its share counts, inside the range for some and outside it for others, so a point right of zero means that thing happened more often than stated.

The label beside each point is the desk’s own outcome, exactly as the desk gives it. ABOVE means the whole interval sits above the stated share and BELOW that it sits below. CONSISTENT means the interval holds the stated share and meets that desk’s rule for width or size. CANNOT_TELL means it does not, so a point clear of zero can still read CANNOT_TELL when its cell is small or its interval wide.

The members measure unlike things: precipitation at airports, lake levels, economic projections, payroll revisions and asteroid distances. Their units, sample sizes, intervals and outcome rules differ, and each desk’s page says how its cells are made. The width of a bar reflects the desk’s sample size and its interval method, and on some desks neighbouring cells are not independent of each other, as their pages say. This is not a ranking of agencies. The rows are sorted by desk name, never by how close they sit to zero.

Where the stated share is our reading of the statement rather than the forecaster’s own words, the desk’s section and its rows say so. For a probability forecast a second score, Brier skill against climatology, is added in its own column when the desk’s feed publishes it.

Read is the stamp of the desk’s own feed, in UTC and in the desk’s own format, when this page was built. The live figures are on each desk’s page.

How a desk joins

A desk joins this page when its feed carries its stated figure beside the delivered one, with an interval and an outcome. Desks being built join on the first build after they ship. Every number here comes from a desk feed or a ledger row, and the page is checked against both before it is released.
