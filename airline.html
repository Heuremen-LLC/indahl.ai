Are schedules getting longer while flights are not?

Every flight is published with a scheduled elapsed time, and the government on-time rate counts a flight as on time when it arrives within 15 minutes of schedule. If schedules lengthen while flown times stay the same, the on-time rate rises with no change in how long a flight takes. For each airline and route we take the median scheduled minutes minus the median flown minutes in January to August of 2019, 2025 and 2026, then the change between periods. Routes are the independent units.

The first table is live. The FAA publishes the airport delay programs it is running now, with its own stated delay, and we keep every snapshot every few minutes. The Bureau of Transportation Statistics publishes flights only monthly, with a lag of about two months, and no free source gives a live schedule for every flight. So the live record is kept now and set against what flights delivered when each month of flight data is published: for each program, the FAA's stated average delay against the mean arrival delay of the flights scheduled into that airport in those hours.
