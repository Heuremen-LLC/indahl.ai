HOSTS · One Model, Many Hosts

Why this number has no kit, and what can be checked.

Division: IRIS

Who serves the same model, and how steadily?

An open-weight model can be served by a dozen companies. OpenRouter, an aggregator, lists for each model its hosts, each host's stated quantization (the numeric precision of the weights), prices, limits and uptime figures. We read all of it every 12 minutes and keep every read, so the page shows where hosts differ for the same model, who is down right now, and how uptime varies with quantization.

The first table compares the one-day uptime a host states with the mean of the 5-minute uptime figures from our own polls over the same 24 hours, with the 2 percentage point tolerance fixed in the plan. As the plan sets, the comparison is made at the latest poll that closes a 24-hour span holding at least 100 of our polls, and the table says when that span ended and how many polls it holds. It reads cannot tell (NO_INTERVAL_FORMABLE) only when no kept span holds 100 polls. The share of endpoints that disagree is read against a band of 0 to 15 percent, open at 15, with 5 percent as the size that matters. CONSISTENT needs the whole 95 percent interval inside the band and not reaching both 0 and 5 percent, and ABOVE needs its lower end at or above 15 percent. This rule is in a plan addendum dated 12 November 2026, committed before any reading under it. The count of failed reads and the share of cannot tell by reason sit under the table. Latency and throughput are empty in the public data and are not shown. A model with one host is listed as such. Plan, addenda and code.

Plan, code and trend history for this page are public: hosts. Every plan was committed before the data it describes.
